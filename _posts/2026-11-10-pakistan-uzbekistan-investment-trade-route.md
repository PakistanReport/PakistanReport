**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan and Uzbekistan have discussed expanding investment in textiles, food processing, agriculture and special economic zones, with the two countries also examining a proposal to improve trade routes through Afghanistan.

Uzbekistan's Ambassador to Pakistan, Alisher Tukhtaev, met Special Investment Facilitation Council (SIFC) Director General Sajid Amin in Islamabad on November 8. The talks were reported by Pakistani state media on Tuesday.

The meeting covered opportunities for joint investment in manufacturing, property development and agriculture, as well as closer cooperation between businesses in the two countries.

Transport difficulties were also raised during the discussions. According to Uzbekistan's Dunyo news agency, bilateral trade has fallen sharply following the closure of the traditional route linking the two countries.

Uzbekistan proposed a dedicated corridor from Peshawar through the Torkham border crossing and Afghanistan to Termez, with cargo movements subject to special monitoring arrangements.

The Pakistani side attributed existing transport restrictions to security concerns and considered the proposal workable, according to the Uzbek account.

Further talks involving Uzbekistan's investment and transport ministries and relevant Pakistani agencies were proposed to examine the arrangements.

The meeting also covered opportunities for Pakistani construction companies to participate in the New Tashkent development project and for Uzbek businesses to establish manufacturing and logistics facilities in Pakistan.

Officials discussed participation in business events, stronger links between the two countries' central banks and the possibility of regular flights connecting Pakistani cities with Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara.

Pakistan and Uzbekistan have been seeking to expand commercial ties under agreements reached during Uzbek President Shavkat Mirziyoyev's visit to Islamabad in February.

The two governments have set a target of increasing bilateral trade to $2 billion by 2029, with cooperation in industrial zones, agriculture and transport among their priorities.
