Ukraine resumed strikes on Russian oil infrastructure on Thursday, 15 October, with an attack on a refinery in Russia's Ryazan region, as a short-lived attempt to pause attacks on energy facilities broke down.

The Ryazan regional governor reported that a Ukrainian drone attack caused a fire at an oil refinery. The extent of any lasting damage and the effect on refinery output were not independently established in the initial reports.

## Dispute over the pause

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Kyiv no longer regarded itself as bound by the proposed pause in attacks on energy infrastructure. The announcement followed continued Russian strikes affecting Ukrainian power supplies.

US President Donald Trump had earlier announced an arrangement intended to halt attacks on energy targets. The parties' accounts of the proposed understanding and its implementation differed, making it important not to describe the arrangement as a durable, mutually observed ceasefire.

Reuters reported that Zelenskyy linked any future Ukrainian restraint to further support for Ukraine's air defence, including US-made Patriot interceptors.

## Energy infrastructure remains a target

Attacks on refineries and electricity facilities can disrupt fuel production and civilian services, although the actual effects of individual strikes require confirmation.

The renewed attack adds to tensions over how energy infrastructure should be treated during the war. Claims made by either side about military objectives, damage and responsibility require attribution and independent verification where possible.

This development is distinct from the Russian strikes on Kyiv and Dnipro reported earlier in October.
