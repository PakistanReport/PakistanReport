Pakistan has offered its seaports and transport infrastructure as potential trade gateways for landlocked Central Asian countries, seeking to strengthen regional commerce and attract investment.

Federal Minister for Investment Qaiser Ahmad Sheikh outlined the proposal at the Second Tianshan Forum for Central Asia Economic and Quality Investment in Almaty, Kazakhstan, on November 2.

## Pakistan's ports in focus

According to the Press Information Department, the minister identified Karachi Port, Port Qasim and Gwadar Port as important links that could connect Central Asian economies with international shipping routes.

He highlighted Karachi's industrial and commercial infrastructure and Gwadar's potential to support regional trade and transport connectivity.

Sheikh also invited investment in energy, logistics, transportation, manufacturing and infrastructure.

## Regional cooperation and investment

The minister emphasised the role of the Central Asia Regional Economic Cooperation (CAREC) programme in improving transport links and economic integration.

He said reliable energy supplies and stronger connectivity were necessary to expand regional trade, encourage investment and generate employment.

The remarks reflect Pakistan's ambition to position itself as a regional trade corridor, with the proposed opportunities depending on further infrastructure development and cooperation among participating countries.
