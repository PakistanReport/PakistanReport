The Federal Board of Revenue is encouraging small shopkeepers and retailers to join a voluntary simplified tax scheme designed to reduce paperwork and bring more businesses into Pakistan's documented economy.

Eligible traders can file a one-page return and pay tax calculated at 1% of turnover, subject to a minimum liability of Rs25,000.

Turnover means business sales or receipts before expenses are deducted. A 1% turnover tax is therefore different from a 1% tax on profit, an important distinction for shops operating on narrow margins.

### How the scheme works

FBR officials say eligible participants may receive relief from certain routine compliance requirements, including audit, point-of-sale integration and digital invoicing obligations under the scheme.

The authority has also described a green identification plate for registered participants and safeguards intended to reduce unnecessary inspections.

Participation is voluntary, and eligible traders can compare the simplified arrangement with the normal tax regime.

### Why it matters

The initiative aims to address a longstanding challenge: persuading smaller businesses to register without creating excessive paperwork or unpredictable compliance costs.

Pakistan has a large retail sector, ranging from neighbourhood shops to major commercial establishments. Bringing more activity into the tax system could broaden the revenue base.

For shopkeepers, the practical decision depends on turnover, existing tax obligations and whether the scheme's conditions apply to their business.

The FBR's outreach to traders will be important, but the programme's success will ultimately depend on how consistently its rules are implemented.

Businesses should consult official FBR guidance before deciding which filing arrangement suits them.
