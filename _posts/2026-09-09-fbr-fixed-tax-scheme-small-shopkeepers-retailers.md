Pakistan's Federal Board of Revenue is encouraging small shopkeepers and retailers to opt for a simplified tax scheme designed to make it easier for smaller businesses to enter the formal tax system.

Under the voluntary arrangement, eligible traders can use a simplified one-page return, with tax calculated at 1% of turnover subject to a minimum liability of Rs25,000.

The initiative forms part of the government's broader effort to increase tax documentation among Pakistan's large retail and trading sector.

## How the scheme works

The special procedure offers eligible small shopkeepers an alternative to filing under the normal tax regime.

According to details presented by FBR officials, the system follows a simplified "one page, one return, one percent" approach and requires less information from participating traders.

The tax is calculated at 1% of turnover, although a minimum tax liability of Rs25,000 applies.

Participation is voluntary, meaning eligible traders can choose between the simplified arrangement and the normal tax regime.

## What participating businesses receive

FBR officials say businesses using the scheme can receive exemptions from several routine compliance requirements.

These include exemptions from routine audit, Point of Sale integration and digital invoicing requirements for eligible participants.

Registered businesses are also expected to receive a green plate intended to identify their participation in the scheme and facilitate interactions with tax officials.

FBR says safeguards are intended to reduce unnecessary inspections of participating businesses unless significant irregularities are identified.

## Why the government wants more retailers in the tax system

Pakistan has repeatedly sought to broaden its tax base and increase documentation of economic activity.

The retail sector is particularly important because a large number of businesses operate across the country, ranging from small neighbourhood shops to much larger commercial establishments.

Bringing more businesses into the formal tax system could increase government revenue while reducing the burden on taxpayers already operating within documented parts of the economy.

The challenge is convincing smaller traders that registration will not expose them to excessive paperwork, unpredictable tax demands or unnecessary enforcement activity.

The simplified scheme appears designed partly to address those concerns.

## What it means for shopkeepers

For an eligible small retailer, the decision will depend on the business's turnover, existing tax position and whether the simplified compliance requirements are preferable to the normal tax regime.

The 1% turnover rate should not be confused with a 1% tax on profit. Turnover refers broadly to sales or business receipts before expenses are deducted.

That distinction can make a significant difference depending on a retailer's margins.

Businesses should therefore assess their own circumstances and consult official FBR guidance before choosing a tax regime.

## What happens next

The success of the initiative will depend heavily on how many traders voluntarily participate and how consistently the scheme is administered.

FBR has been conducting outreach with traders and business organisations as part of its effort to improve participation and simplify tax compliance.

Pakistan Report will track further changes to the scheme and its implementation as additional guidance becomes available.
