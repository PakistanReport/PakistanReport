The Sindh government has directed education authorities to take immediate action against private schools operating in dangerous or structurally unsafe buildings, putting student safety at the centre of a new inspection and enforcement drive.

The School Education and Literacy Department has issued instructions to the Directorate General of Inspection and Registration of Private Institutions concerning the identification of unsafe premises, relocation of students and urgent protective measures.

The department's notifications, dated October 19, reflect a zero-tolerance approach towards buildings that could put children and staff at risk.

### Inspections and relocation

The initiative requires authorities to identify institutions where structural deterioration or other building hazards create an unacceptable risk.

Schools found to be operating in dangerous premises may face closure or restrictions until safe arrangements are made.

The government's instructions also address the relocation of students, an important consideration because shutting an unsafe building should not leave children without access to education.

Private-school operators will need to cooperate with inspectors and demonstrate that their premises are suitable for continued use.

The process is likely to place particular scrutiny on schools operating in older buildings or properties that were not originally designed as educational facilities.

### Safety beyond classroom instruction

School safety is not limited to teaching standards or academic performance.

The structural condition of classrooms, staircases, roofs, boundary walls and emergency exits can become a serious concern when buildings are poorly maintained or overcrowded.

Recent concerns over school-building safety have renewed attention on the responsibilities of regulators and institutional management.

In Sindh, the immediate challenge is ensuring that inspection findings lead to corrective action rather than remaining administrative paperwork.

That includes identifying genuinely dangerous structures, communicating decisions to parents and arranging suitable alternative premises where necessary.

### What parents should expect

Parents whose children's schools are affected will need clear information about whether classes can continue safely, where students will be relocated and how any interruption will be managed.

Authorities should also distinguish between schools requiring immediate evacuation and those where specific repairs can address identified deficiencies.

A transparent inspection process will be important to prevent uncertainty for families and school operators.

The enforcement drive could become a significant test of private-school regulation in Sindh, particularly if inspections reveal widespread maintenance problems.

Its success will ultimately be measured not by the number of notices issued but by whether students can attend classes in buildings that are demonstrably safe.
