Islamabad's long-delayed local government elections have been given a new polling date, with the Election Commission of Pakistan announcing that voters in the federal capital are scheduled to cast their ballots on December 20, 2026.

The commission's election programme, announced on October 21, also sets December 24 as the deadline for declaring official results. The decision moves the capital's local government process from prolonged procedural uncertainty into a defined timetable for candidates, parties and voters.

## Key dates for candidates

According to the schedule reported by the Associated Press of Pakistan, nomination papers may be submitted from November 2 to November 6. A preliminary candidate list is due on November 7, followed by scrutiny of nomination papers between November 9 and November 13.

Candidates will be able to challenge returning officers' decisions between November 14 and November 18. Appellate tribunals are expected to decide those appeals by November 23, with a revised candidate list scheduled for November 24. November 25 is the withdrawal deadline, and the final list and election symbols are due on November 26.

This sequence gives prospective contestants a relatively narrow window to assemble documents, respond to objections and finalise their campaigns. For voters, the dates provide a basis for following which candidates ultimately appear on the ballot.

## Restrictions during the campaign

The ECP has also announced a code of conduct intended to limit the use of government machinery for electoral advantage. The measures restrict transfers and postings without the commission's approval where required and prohibit announcements of development projects in Islamabad during the election process.

The code also bars senior public office-holders, including the prime minister, ministers, governors and chief ministers, from participating in election campaigns. Government employees within Islamabad's territorial limits face restrictions on taking leave without ECP approval.

These safeguards matter because local elections can be shaped by decisions about public works, administrative resources and official influence. Consistent enforcement will be central to confidence in the contest.

## Why the capital's local vote matters

Local governments are intended to bring elected representation closer to neighbourhood-level problems, from sanitation and streets to community facilities and municipal accountability. In a capital city where residents often look to federal or administrative authorities for solutions, an elected local tier can offer a more direct channel for everyday concerns.

The repeated delays have also carried a democratic cost. Without a functioning elected local structure, residents have fewer opportunities to hold neighbourhood representatives accountable through the ballot box.

The December 20 date now provides a clear target, but meeting it will require the election administration to complete nominations, appeals, candidate lists, polling preparations and results management on schedule.

For Islamabad residents, the immediate priorities are to check their voter registration details through official channels, follow constituency information issued by the ECP and review the final candidate lists once published.
