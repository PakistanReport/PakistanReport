**DUBAI, 18 November 2026 —** Iran launched fresh attacks targeting US military facilities in Kuwait and Bahrain on Wednesday, intensifying tensions across the Gulf following American airstrikes on an Iranian missile installation.

Iran's military said it targeted drone and missile facilities at Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait. Kuwaiti authorities reported intercepting hostile drones and condemned the attacks as a violation of the country's sovereignty.

Bahrain also said its air defences intercepted Iranian aerial attacks, the third such incident reported in the kingdom within a week. Authorities issued security alerts urging residents to seek shelter before announcing that the immediate threats had passed.

Neither country initially reported confirmed damage from Wednesday's attacks.

### Strikes follow US operation in Iran

The latest exchanges followed a US strike on a missile launch site on Iran's Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday.

A US military official said the operation was carried out in response to Iranian attacks on American personnel and facilities, including an attempted strike on a radar installation in Bahrain.

Iran has described its recent operations as retaliation for US military activities and attacks on Iranian infrastructure.

Bahrain hosts the US Navy's Fifth Fleet, making it a strategically important location in the confrontation.

### Gulf security and shipping under pressure

The renewed attacks have raised concerns about security around the Strait of Hormuz, a major route for international oil and gas shipments.

Shipping has already been disrupted by the wider conflict and a US naval blockade of Iran.

In a separate incident, Abu Dhabi National Oil Company's shipping division reported that an attack on one of its vessels in the strait left one crew member missing and 11 injured.

The escalation comes as Oman and Qatar continue diplomatic efforts to reduce tensions between Washington and Tehran.

Officials from Qatar and Iran held discussions on Tuesday aimed at narrowing differences between competing proposals to end the conflict.

With attacks continuing despite mediation, Gulf governments face growing concerns over the safety of military facilities, commercial shipping and regional energy supplies.
