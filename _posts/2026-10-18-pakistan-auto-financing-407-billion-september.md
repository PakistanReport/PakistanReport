Pakistan's outstanding automobile financing rose to approximately Rs407 billion in September 2026, extending a run of monthly increases even as new vehicle sales declined from August and from a year earlier.

The financing balance reached **Rs407.12 billion**, up 3.49% from approximately **Rs393.4 billion** in August, according to [Mettis Global's report citing State Bank of Pakistan data](https://mettisglobal.news/Auto-financing-in-Pakistan-rises-to-Rs407bn-in-September-64286). The September balance was also 33.61% above the Rs304.71 billion reported for September 2025.

These figures refer to the **outstanding stock of vehicle loans**, not the value of new car loans issued during September. Outstanding credit can rise even when showroom sales soften, because it reflects repayments, new borrowing and the existing loan portfolio.

## September sales fall, quarter still higher

Sales of cars, sport utility vehicles, pickups and vans fell 12% year on year and 3% month on month to **15,104 units** in September, [Dawn reported](https://www.dawn.com/news/2037908/auto-loans-rise-despite-sales-slump), citing automotive industry figures.

The same report said cumulative sales for the first quarter of FY27 rose 19% to **50,480 units**, showing that the September decline did not erase the gains recorded over the three-month period.

The [Pakistan Automotive Manufacturers Association](https://pama.org.pk/) publishes monthly vehicle production and sales releases, including its September 2026 statistics. The sales figures above are those reported in the published analysis; Pakistan Report has not independently recalculated the complete manufacturer-level tables.

## Why the measures can diverge

Vehicle sales measure units sold during a particular month, while outstanding auto financing captures the loan balance carried by borrowers at the end of that month. A rise in financing therefore does not establish that more cars were purchased in September, or that the increase was caused by any particular policy change.

Dawn described the September financing balance as the 22nd consecutive monthly increase. The newspaper also reported industry uncertainty about the timing of a new automotive policy and tax treatment of hybrid vehicles. Those are reported market concerns, rather than demonstrated explanations for the financing data.

The combination of a larger loan portfolio and slower monthly sales illustrates why both indicators are needed to assess demand. Subsequent State Bank credit releases and PAMA sales reports will show whether the divergence persists.
