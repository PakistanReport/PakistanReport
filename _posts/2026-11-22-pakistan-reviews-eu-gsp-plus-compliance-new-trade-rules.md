**ISLAMABAD:** Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar has directed federal and provincial authorities to strengthen compliance with European Union trade-preference requirements as Pakistan prepares for upcoming reviews of its GSP+ status.

Chairing an inter-ministerial meeting on Saturday, November 21, Dar reviewed progress in implementing international conventions and regulatory standards linked to the Generalised Scheme of Preferences Plus (GSP+) arrangement.

According to an official government statement, the meeting also examined institutional follow-up measures and Pakistan's diplomatic engagement with the European Union.

Dar stressed the importance of the arrangement for Pakistani exports and called for timely compliance, closer coordination between ministries and proactive engagement ahead of future reviews.

Pakistan has benefited from GSP+ since 2014, receiving reduced or zero tariffs on a wide range of goods exported to the European Union, particularly textiles and clothing.

The European Commission estimates that Pakistan had €7.5 billion in exports eligible for GSP+ preferences in 2024, benefiting from approximately €732 million in tariff exemptions.

Under revised EU rules taking effect in January 2027, the number of international conventions linked to the scheme will increase from 27 to 32, covering areas including human rights, labour standards, environmental protection and governance.

Pakistan will need to submit a fresh application under the new framework to retain preferential access beyond the transition period ending in 2028. Continued benefits during that period remain subject to compliance.

A European Commission assessment published earlier this year acknowledged some legislative progress but identified continuing concerns over the implementation of rights protections and governance commitments.

Saturday's meeting was attended by senior officials from the foreign affairs, commerce and information ministries, Pakistan's ambassador to Belgium and the EU, and representatives of federal and provincial departments.
