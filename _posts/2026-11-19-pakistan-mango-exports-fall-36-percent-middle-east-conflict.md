**KARACHI, 19 November 2026 —** Pakistan's mango exports fell **36% to 78,000 tonnes** during the 2026 season, as conflict in the Middle East, rising freight costs and regional trade disruptions affected shipments.

According to the All Pakistan Fruit and Vegetable Exporters, Importers and Merchants Association (PFVA), exports declined from **123,000 tonnes** last season, while earnings dropped from **$92.2 million to $58.5 million**, a loss of $33.7 million.

The country narrowly missed its export target of 80,000 tonnes.

### Gulf Shipments Decline Sharply

Exports to Gulf countries fell to **14,500 tonnes from 36,000 tonnes** a year earlier.

PFVA patron-in-chief Waheed Ahmed attributed the decline to disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, higher freight charges and weaker demand in key Middle Eastern markets.

He said freight costs on affected routes had risen from around $1,000–$1,400 to as much as $8,000, while longer shipping times created difficulties for exporters of perishable fruit.

Shipments to Qatar fell from 1,900 to 880 tonnes, while exports to Oman and Saudi Arabia also declined.

Trade restrictions at the Afghan border further affected access to Afghanistan and Central Asian markets, with exporters estimating a loss of 24,000 tonnes in potential shipments.

### Climate Pressures on Production

Ahmed also warned that climate change, orchard diseases and rising cultivation costs were affecting Pakistan's mango industry.

According to the association, production is approximately **35% below its potential capacity of 1.8 million tonnes**.

The export season ran from June to September. Ahmed said beginning shipments on June 1 helped maintain fruit quality and enabled exporters to come close to their annual target despite difficult market conditions.
