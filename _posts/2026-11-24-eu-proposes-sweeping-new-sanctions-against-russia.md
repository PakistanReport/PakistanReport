**BRUSSELS:** The European Commission has proposed its 22nd package of sanctions against Russia, targeting financial institutions, energy infrastructure, maritime trade and cryptocurrency networks in an effort to restrict Moscow's ability to finance its war in Ukraine.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced the proposed measures on Monday, including transaction restrictions on approximately 60 additional Russian banks and their removal from the SWIFT international financial messaging system.

The package would also target cryptocurrency platforms and payment networks in Russia and third countries accused of facilitating sanctions evasion. The Commission has established a dedicated Crypto Sanctions Task Force to strengthen enforcement and coordinate measures with international partners.

Under the proposed energy restrictions, European companies would be prohibited from assisting Russia in constructing, operating or repairing energy infrastructure, including oil refineries. Restrictions would extend to electricity sector services for the first time.

More than 70 additional vessels associated with Russia's so-called shadow fleet would face sanctions, alongside proposed restrictions on tankers insured in Russia.

The European Union has already imposed restrictions on more than 670 vessels associated with the fleet, which is used to circumvent Western restrictions on Russian oil exports. The EU's price cap on Russian crude oil currently stands at $44 per barrel.

The Commission has also proposed banning entry into the EU for Russian former combatants and preventing European nationals from serving on the governing bodies of Russian companies.

Further measures would prohibit Russian athletes and teams from competing under their national flag at sporting events within the EU and restrict Russian financial sponsorship of European sporting and cultural organisations.

The proposed package requires unanimous approval from all 27 EU member states, with Brussels seeking an agreement in December.
