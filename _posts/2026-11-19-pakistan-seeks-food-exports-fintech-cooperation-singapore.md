**SINGAPORE, 19 November 2026 —** Pakistan is seeking to expand its food and agricultural exports to Singapore while strengthening cooperation in financial technology, artificial intelligence and investment.

The opportunities were discussed during a meeting between Finance Minister **Muhammad Aurangzeb** and Singapore's Deputy Prime Minister **Gan Kim Yong** on Wednesday, on the sidelines of the Singapore FinTech Festival 2026.

Pakistan's High Commissioner to Singapore, Rabia Shafiq, also attended the meeting.

### Expanding Food Exports and Investment

Aurangzeb highlighted the potential to increase the presence of Pakistani food and agricultural products in Singapore's market.

Both sides discussed ways to strengthen bilateral trade and investment, with particular emphasis on direct engagement between businesses.

The finance minister encouraged small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) from both countries to develop commercial partnerships and explore new opportunities.

The discussions also covered cooperation in finance, artificial intelligence and fintech, reflecting Pakistan's efforts to expand its presence in technology-driven industries.

### Focus on Digital Finance and SMEs

During his visit, Aurangzeb addressed the Global SME Finance Forum, where he outlined Pakistan's plans to improve access to financing for smaller businesses.

He said the government aims to increase SME financing to **Rs. 2 trillion by June 2028**, nearly double the current level.

The minister highlighted digital lending, alternative credit assessment and financial technology partnerships as ways to help businesses obtain financing without relying entirely on traditional collateral.

He also visited Pakistan's pavilion at the Singapore FinTech Festival and met representatives of Pakistani technology companies showcasing their products.

### Strengthening Bilateral Economic Relations

The meeting took place as Pakistan and Singapore marked **60 years of diplomatic relations** in 2026.

Aurangzeb welcomed opportunities for closer cooperation and encouraged stronger links between investors and businesses in both countries.

During the three-day visit, which began on 17 November, the finance minister also engaged with Pakistan's business community in Singapore to discuss investment opportunities and economic reforms.

The discussions form part of Pakistan's broader efforts to diversify export markets, attract foreign investment and strengthen international technology partnerships.
