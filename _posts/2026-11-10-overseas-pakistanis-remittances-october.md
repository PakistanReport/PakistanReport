**KARACHI:** Overseas Pakistanis sent $3.89 billion home in October, an increase of 13.8% from a year earlier, according to figures released by the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) on Tuesday.

The inflows were also 8.5% higher than September's $3.59 billion, continuing the rise in money transferred by Pakistani workers abroad.

Remittances during the first four months of the current financial year, from July to October, reached approximately $14.8 billion, up 14% from the corresponding period last year.

Saudi Arabia remained the largest source of remittances, accounting for $981.6 million in October. Pakistanis working in the United Arab Emirates sent another $815.9 million.

Together, the two Gulf countries contributed almost $1.8 billion, or about 46% of the month's total.

The United Kingdom was the third-largest source, with inflows of $592.2 million, followed by the United States at $328.9 million. Remittances from European Union countries amounted to approximately $461 million.

The latest figures come after the central bank raised its projection for remittances during the current financial year to $45 billion, from an earlier estimate of $44 billion.

SBP Governor Jameel Ahmed said last month that inflows had remained resilient during the first quarter of FY2027 and were expected to exceed the bank's previous assessment.

Remittances are an important source of foreign exchange for Pakistan and provide income to households receiving money from relatives working overseas.
