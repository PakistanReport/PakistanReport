**LAHORE, November 13, 2026 —** Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi has proposed holding a referendum on the creation of new provinces in Pakistan, saying the public should decide whether the country's existing administrative structure needs to change.

Speaking to journalists at the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) office in Lahore on Thursday, Naqvi said the outcome of any referendum should be respected, whether voters supported or rejected the proposal.

He also called for consultations with political parties, acknowledging differences over the proposed restructuring.

## PPP Opposes Division of Sindh

The Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP), a key federal coalition partner, has opposed proposals to divide Sindh.

Sindh Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah said his party had no objection to creating additional provinces elsewhere in Pakistan if the people concerned supported such changes. However, he maintained that dividing Sindh was unacceptable.

The disagreement reflects the political challenges surrounding proposals to redraw provincial boundaries.

## Constitutional Approval Required

Prime Minister's Adviser on Political Affairs Rana Sanaullah said Parliament would retain the final authority over creating new provinces, even if a referendum were conducted.

Under Pakistan's Constitution, changes to provincial boundaries require a two-thirds majority in both houses of Parliament and approval by at least two-thirds of the affected provincial assembly's total membership.

Defence Minister Khawaja Asif has also supported considering additional administrative units, suggesting that Pakistan could examine a structure comprising as many as 12 provinces.

Supporters argue that smaller provinces could improve governance and public service delivery, while opponents have raised concerns about provincial boundaries and political representation.
