**KARACHI:** Strong investor demand has created shortages of physical gold bars in Karachi, prompting dealers to charge premiums of between Rs10,000 and Rs30,000 per tola above quoted market rates.

Gold traders say investors have returned to the market following a substantial decline in prices, with demand for bullion exceeding available supplies.

Hamza Shikarpuri, vice president of the All Pakistan Sarafa Gems and Jewellers Association, said premiums of around Rs10,000 per tola were being charged as buyers sought gold bars, including smaller one- and two-tola quantities.

Some investors are reportedly willing to wait one or two days for delivery rather than delay their purchases.

According to association figures, the official price of 24-karat gold reached Rs572,862 per tola on January 29, 2026, although dealers reported transactions as high as Rs585,000 because of additional premiums.

The quoted price has since fallen to approximately Rs376,336 per tola, encouraging both investors and jewellery buyers to return to shops.

Haseen Qureishi, president of the All Karachi Jewellers and Manufacturers Association, said investors currently outnumber jewellery customers, although some remain cautious and expect further price declines.

Lower prices have also revived demand for wedding jewellery, after high rates previously encouraged consumers to consider silver and artificial alternatives.

Traders say uncertainty surrounding international conflicts and future gold prices continues to influence purchasing decisions.
