Pakistan has called for changes to the international financial system to improve access to development financing and strengthen the representation of developing economies, as it prepares to assume the chairmanship of the Group of 24.

Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb presented the proposals at a meeting of G-24 finance ministers and central bank governors on October 13, held alongside the World Bank and International Monetary Fund annual meetings in Bangkok.

Speaking as the group's first vice-chair, Aurangzeb identified disruptions to trade and investment, climate-related losses, restrictive financing conditions and widening funding gaps as major challenges for developing countries.

According to the Ministry of Finance, Pakistan proposed three priorities: restoring development as a central objective of the international financial system, making the World Bank and IMF more representative and responsive, and improving financial arrangements to support long-term development.

The minister also called for timely assistance to countries implementing economic reforms while facing external shocks.

Pakistan is due to take over the G-24 chairmanship and has pledged to work with member countries to strengthen their collective influence in international financial discussions.

The G-24 is a forum through which developing economies coordinate positions on international monetary and development finance issues.

The proposals represent Pakistan's stated priorities for future discussions. No new financing facility or binding reform agreement was announced at the meeting.
