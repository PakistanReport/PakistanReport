**LAHORE, November 7, 2026 —** Senate Chairman Syed Yousaf Raza Gilani has called for greater international promotion of Pakistan's traditional handicrafts, cultural products and skilled workforce to strengthen export opportunities and expand commercial ties with overseas markets.

Speaking at the inauguration of the New York Chamber of Small Business Corporation's office in Lahore on November 6, Gilani said Pakistan's traditional industries had significant potential to attract international buyers through stronger branding and business networks.

## Traditional Products Offer Export Opportunities

Gilani highlighted embroidery, blue pottery, ceramics, khussa footwear and Ajrak as examples of Pakistani products that could gain greater recognition in international markets.

He particularly emphasised the potential of Ajrak-based clothing, including shirts, as a way to introduce traditional Pakistani textiles to a wider audience.

Referring to Multan's historical and cultural significance, the Senate chairman said the city's blue pottery, ceramics and craftsmanship deserved greater international exposure.

He encouraged businesses to make better use of Pakistan's cultural identity when developing products for export markets.

## Overseas Business Networks and Skilled Workforce

Gilani also stressed the importance of engaging Pakistani business associations and communities abroad, particularly in the United States, to build commercial connections and promote locally produced goods.

He said overseas professional networks could help introduce Pakistani products to international markets and support wider trade and investment opportunities.

The Senate chairman identified technical, professional and software-related skills as additional areas in which Pakistani workers could compete internationally.

He called for stronger skills development and international exposure to help young people meet the requirements of overseas employment markets.

## Greater International Engagement

Gilani welcomed the opening of the business organisation's Lahore office, describing international business partnerships as an opportunity to strengthen Pakistan's commercial outreach.

He also acknowledged the contribution of overseas Pakistani professionals and business organisations to the country's international connections.

The remarks highlighted the potential role of traditional industries, skilled workers and diaspora networks in expanding Pakistan's economic engagement with international markets.
