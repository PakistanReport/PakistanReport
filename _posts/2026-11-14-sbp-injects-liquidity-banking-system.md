**KARACHI, November 14, 2026 —** The State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) injected approximately Rs1.05 trillion into the banking system on Friday through short-term open-market operations aimed at maintaining liquidity.

According to reported auction results, the central bank provided Rs854.65 billion through conventional reverse-repurchase operations and Rs195.2 billion through Shariah-compliant financing.

The combined injection amounted to Rs1.049 trillion, with funds offered for seven- and 14-day periods.

Under the conventional operations, the SBP accepted Rs132.65 billion for seven days at 12.02 per cent and Rs722 billion for 14 days at 12.06 per cent.

Islamic banking institutions received Rs42 billion for seven days and Rs153.2 billion for 14 days, at expected returns of 12.06 per cent and 12.04 per cent, respectively.

Open-market operations allow the central bank to provide temporary funding to eligible financial institutions and manage short-term money-market conditions.

Such transactions help banks meet liquidity requirements while supporting the SBP's efforts to keep market interest rates aligned with its monetary policy framework.

The scale of Friday's operation indicates substantial demand for short-term funding, although the amount alone does not establish the reasons behind that demand.

The injection does not represent new government spending or direct lending to businesses and households. The funds are provided temporarily and are repayable at the end of the agreed periods.

The SBP regularly conducts these operations as part of its management of liquidity in Pakistan's banking system.
