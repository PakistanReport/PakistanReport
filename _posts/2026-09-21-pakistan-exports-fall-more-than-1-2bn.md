Pakistan’s export earnings fell by more than $1.2 billion in fiscal year 2025-26 compared with the previous year, according to figures reported on Sunday.

Total exports declined from around $32bn in FY2024-25 to about $30.8bn in FY2025-26.

Rice recorded the largest reported drop, with export earnings falling by around $1.02bn over the period. Textile products, sugar and other agricultural exports also faced pressure.

Officials cited regional tensions as one factor behind the deterioration, saying disruptions to trade routes had affected transportation and logistics costs and complicated the movement of export consignments to regional markets.

Reported data also showed exports to Gulf countries declining by around $100 million, while some Central Asian and Afghan markets recorded much steeper percentage declines.

The figures underline Pakistan’s exposure to external disruptions at a time when export growth remains important for foreign-exchange earnings and the wider external account.

### A wider trade challenge

Regional instability is not the only factor affecting exports, and the reported figures do not establish that the Middle East conflict caused the entire decline. Domestic energy costs, taxation, competitiveness and demand in overseas markets also influence export performance.

The government says it is working to lower costs and support businesses, while exporters continue to face uncertainty over freight, logistics and access to regional markets.
