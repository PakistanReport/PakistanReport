**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan's trade deficit with seven Middle Eastern countries narrowed by 29 per cent to $3.75 billion during the first four months of FY27, compared with $5.27 billion a year earlier, according to State Bank of Pakistan data.

Import payments fell by 22 per cent to $4.92 billion, while export receipts increased by 13 per cent to $1.17 billion.

Qatar and Kuwait accounted for the largest reductions in import payments, with purchases from the two countries collectively declining by $1.21 billion.

The United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia remained Pakistan's largest trading partners within the group, accounting for approximately 75 per cent of imports and 86 per cent of exports.

The country-wise changes during July–October were:

| Country | Export growth | Import growth |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| UAE | +18% | −10% |
| Saudi Arabia | +10% | −19% |
| Qatar | −32% | −73% |
| Kuwait | −9% | −86% |
| Oman | +21% | +79% |
| Bahrain | −6% | −10% |
| Jordan | +1% | +7% |

Oman recorded the largest increase in Pakistan's import payments, which rose by $343 million during the period.

Pakistan has been diversifying its energy supply sources amid disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

On November 22, Pakistan State Oil and Oman's OQ Trading signed a petroleum supply agreement to strengthen bilateral energy cooperation.

QatarEnergy has extended its force majeure notices on liquefied natural gas supplies to Pakistan until the end of December, citing disruptions caused by the Middle East conflict, according to Reuters.

Despite the improvement in trade with the seven Middle Eastern economies, Pakistan's overall merchandise trade deficit increased by 16 per cent to $14.63 billion during July–October FY27, according to separate customs-based figures released by the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics.
