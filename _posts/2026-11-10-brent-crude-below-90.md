**ISLAMABAD:** International oil prices fell sharply on Tuesday, with Brent crude slipping below $90 a barrel for the first time since late August as traders reassessed the risk of prolonged supply disruptions in the Middle East.

Brent fell to around $89.66 during trading before recovering some ground. US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude also declined, trading near $85 a barrel.

The latest fall followed remarks by US President Donald Trump indicating that further military action against Iran might not be necessary. His comments raised expectations of an easing in regional tensions that have kept oil markets volatile.

Brent had already fallen nearly 6 per cent on Monday and has now retreated substantially from the six-month high of $118 a barrel reached on October 30.

Oil supplies remain a concern, particularly around the Strait of Hormuz, where attacks on shipping have continued. However, traders have reduced some of the additional cost they had been attaching to the risk of further disruption.

Higher production has also weighed on prices. A Reuters survey found that output from the Organisation of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) increased in October, with Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Iraq contributing to the rise.

The decline could ease Pakistan's petroleum import bill if lower international prices are sustained.

Domestic petrol and diesel prices have already been reduced several times this month. Petrol is currently priced at Rs346 per litre and high-speed diesel at Rs378 per litre, following the latest revision effective from November 7.

Any further change in local fuel prices will depend on international refined-product prices, the rupee's exchange rate and applicable government levies and margins.
