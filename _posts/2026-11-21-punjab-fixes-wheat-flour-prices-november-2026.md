LAHORE: The Punjab government has fixed the release price of government wheat at Rs4,500 per 40 kilograms and the retail price of a 10kg flour bag produced from government wheat at Rs1,275 under a new distribution policy taking effect on November 21.

The Punjab Wheat Release and Flour Distribution Order 2026 introduces revised procedures for supplying government wheat to flour mills, monitoring stocks and regulating the sale of flour across the province.

Under the order, government wheat will be supplied only to approved operational flour mills and licensed or registered atta chakkis.

Eligible mills and grinding units must sign agreements with their respective district food controllers before receiving wheat. Supplies will require advance payments into designated bank accounts, followed by verification by the relevant authorities.

The government will allocate wheat quotas among districts according to population and requirements, with district food controllers responsible for distribution among eligible mills.

Wheat supplies may include stocks obtained from the Pakistan Agricultural Storage and Services Corporation (PASSCO), aggregators and imported sources.

Flour mills receiving government wheat will also be permitted to process privately purchased stocks. However, they must maintain separate records and submit daily information on wheat purchases, storage, grinding and flour sales through the Food Department Inventory Management System.

The order specifies an extraction ratio of 70% flour, 18% fine flour and 12% bran from government wheat.

Mills will be allowed to transport products manufactured from privately purchased wheat to other provinces, subject to obtaining electronic permits.

Provincial food authorities have been authorised to inspect mills and grinding units to monitor compliance with the distribution rules.

Violations may result in financial recoveries or suspension or cancellation of licences, while mills with outstanding government dues may be denied further wheat supplies.

District administrations will monitor the sale of flour at the notified price.

The new arrangements establish a regulated system for distributing public wheat stocks while introducing digital reporting requirements intended to improve oversight of milling and flour supplies.
