Pakistan's opposition alliance **Tehreek-e-Tahaffuz Ayeen-e-Pakistan (TTAP)** has suspended negotiations with the federal government, saying talks will not resume until arrangements are made for access to imprisoned former prime minister **Imran Khan** and information about his health is provided.

The decision was announced on **15 October 2026** after an alliance leadership meeting. It comes amid heightened tensions following the emergency and governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, which parliament has now endorsed.

### Access and health information at centre of dispute

According to reports of the alliance's announcement, TTAP demanded that Khan be permitted to meet family members and that his doctors be granted access. The alliance said it would not continue discussions without progress on those concerns.

The alliance also assigned authority for future negotiations to its chairman, **Mehmood Khan Achakzai**, according to *Dawn*. The decision sets a clear condition for any renewed formal dialogue.

### A further setback for negotiations

The suspension follows earlier attempts by the government and opposition to find a political settlement. Those discussions had already faced obstacles over protest plans, detainees and the political situation in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

The latest announcement is distinct from previous reports that talks had failed to produce an agreement: TTAP has now explicitly linked any resumption of negotiations to access to Khan and information about his health.

The government's response and whether arrangements for visits can be agreed will help determine if the impasse is temporary or develops into a longer interruption of dialogue. Claims and concerns about Khan's health should not be treated as independently established medical findings without verified information.
