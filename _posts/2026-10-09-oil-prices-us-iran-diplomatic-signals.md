Oil prices eased in early trading on October 9 as markets assessed remarks from US President Donald Trump suggesting possible diplomatic progress with Iran. At approximately 02:20 GMT on October 9, Reuters reported Brent crude down 72 cents at $103.53 a barrel and US West Texas Intermediate down 52 cents at $90.97. These were intraday prices, not final settlement values, and may have changed later in the session. The market reaction followed comments indicating that immediate escalation might be avoided, although the wider Middle East conflict continues to threaten shipping and energy infrastructure. The Strait of Hormuz remains central to the supply outlook because of its importance for oil and fuel shipments. A single morning decline does not establish that disruption has ended. Reuters noted that Brent remained on course for a weekly gain despite the day's fall. For Pakistan, changes in international crude benchmarks can influence import costs, but domestic pump prices also depend on the government's pricing formula, taxes and the timing of reviews. The story should not promise an immediate reduction in retail fuel prices.
