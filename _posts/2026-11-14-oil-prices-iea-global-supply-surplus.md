**LONDON, November 14, 2026 —** Global oil prices ended the week lower as the International Energy Agency (IEA) forecast that worldwide petroleum supplies would exceed demand during the final quarter of 2026, reversing its earlier expectations of a shortage.

Brent crude, the international oil benchmark, settled at $95.84 per barrel on Friday, while US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) closed at $88.56. Both benchmarks recorded weekly losses of approximately 2.5%, marking a second consecutive week of declines.

In its latest monthly Oil Market Report, the IEA projected global oil supplies would average 105 million barrels per day during the October–December quarter, exceeding expected demand by approximately 1.7 million barrels daily.

The revised outlook reflects a faster-than-anticipated recovery in Middle Eastern oil exports, despite continuing disruptions to regional shipping and energy infrastructure.

Global oil production increased by 2.3 million barrels per day in October, reaching 105.4 million barrels daily, following another substantial increase in September.

The agency also reported that observed global oil inventories rose by 52 million barrels in October, the first monthly increase since June.

Meanwhile, the IEA expects worldwide oil demand to decline by 2.7 million barrels per day in 2026 compared with the previous year, reflecting high energy prices and disruptions to economic activity.

Although improving supplies have eased pressure on crude prices, the agency warned that refined petroleum products, particularly diesel, remain in relatively short supply.

Security concerns around the Strait of Hormuz also continue to threaten international oil shipments, creating uncertainty over the sustainability of the recovery.

For oil-importing economies such as Pakistan, lower international crude prices could help ease petroleum import costs if the downward trend continues. However, domestic fuel prices also depend on exchange rates, taxation and government pricing decisions.

The latest forecasts suggest that global oil markets are moving towards a more balanced supply position, although geopolitical tensions and disruptions to shipping remain significant risks.
