**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan's salaried workers paid approximately Rs193 billion in income tax during the first four months of the current financial year, nearly five times the Rs42 billion collected in withholding taxes from property transactions.

The figures for July–October 2026, reported by The Express Tribune, show a widening gap between tax collections from salaried employees and the property sector.

## Property Tax Collections Fall

Withholding tax collections from property transactions dropped from approximately Rs76 billion during the same period last year to Rs42 billion.

The fall followed reductions in advance income tax rates on property purchases and sales introduced in the federal budget.

The advance tax rate on property sales was cut from 5.5% to 2.75%, while the rate on purchases was reduced from 2.5% to 1.25%.

As a result, tax collected on property sales declined from Rs52.7 billion to Rs27.3 billion. Collections from purchases fell from Rs23.1 billion to Rs14.7 billion.

## Salaried Workers' Contributions Rise

In contrast, income tax collected from salaried employees increased from approximately Rs179 billion to Rs193 billion during the four-month period, despite tax relief announced for salaried individuals in the federal budget.

Unlike property transaction taxes, income tax on salaries is generally deducted directly by employers, providing the government with a regular source of revenue.

Retailers and wholesalers together contributed approximately Rs24.7 billion in withholding taxes, recording growth of just 2.5% over the previous year.

The Federal Board of Revenue also faced an estimated Rs95 billion collection shortfall during July–October, with overall revenue growth reported at 6.5%.

The figures come as Pakistan continues efforts to broaden its tax base and reduce reliance on taxpayers whose income is already documented.
