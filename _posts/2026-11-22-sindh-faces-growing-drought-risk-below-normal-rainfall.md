**KARACHI:** Sindh faces an increasing risk of drought after five consecutive months of below-normal rainfall, with the Pakistan Meteorological Department (PMD) warning that dry conditions could persist through the winter.

According to a drought advisory issued by the department's National Drought Monitoring and Early Warning Centre on November 20, rainfall across Sindh remained below normal from July through November.

The province recorded a 61 per cent rainfall deficit in July, 22 per cent in August and 90 per cent in September. The shortfall reached 100 per cent in October and stood at 99 per cent during the first 20 days of November.

The prolonged dry spell has raised concerns about declining soil moisture, agricultural production and water availability, particularly in communities dependent on rain-fed farming and livestock.

Balochistan and southern Punjab are also facing rainfall shortages, with the department warning that continued dry weather could increase pressure on agricultural and domestic water supplies.

The PMD expects normal to below-normal rainfall across the affected regions between December 2026 and February 2027, potentially worsening existing water shortages and affecting winter crops.

Authorities have been advised to strengthen drought monitoring, identify vulnerable communities and prepare precautionary assistance measures.

The department also recommended water conservation, improved irrigation practices and livestock support to reduce the potential impact on rural livelihoods.

The advisory calls for early preparedness against possible mild drought conditions rather than declaring a drought emergency.
