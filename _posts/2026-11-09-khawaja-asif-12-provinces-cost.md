**ISLAMABAD:** Defence Minister Khawaja Asif has supported the creation of up to 12 provinces in Pakistan, saying smaller administrative units could improve governance and make public services more accessible.

Speaking to Geo News in Sialkot on Sunday, Asif said the proposal had been under discussion for the past two months.

He said Pakistan's growing population required a more effective system of administration, with greater authority given to institutions closer to the public.

## Powers Should Move Beyond Provincial Capitals

Asif argued that too much administrative authority remained concentrated in provincial capitals, including Lahore, Karachi, Quetta and Peshawar.

He called for greater powers and resources to be transferred to smaller administrative units and local government institutions.

The minister also questioned the existing distribution of financial resources, saying funds transferred by the federal government remained largely under the control of provincial administrations.

According to Asif, local governments continued to depend on provincial authorities despite being responsible for providing essential services to residents.

## New Provinces Could Add Billions in Annual Costs

A recent study by the Policy Research Institute of Market Economy (PRIME) has raised concerns about the financial cost of establishing additional provinces.

The study estimates that creating two additional provinces in Punjab would increase annual government expenditure by approximately Rs47 billion to Rs56 billion.

A much larger restructuring, involving the conversion of 30 existing administrative divisions into provinces, could cost between Rs609 billion and Rs730 billion annually.

Each new province would require its own governor, chief minister, provincial assembly, secretariat, high court and other government institutions.

The study estimates that running the core administration of a new province could cost between Rs22.4 billion and Rs25.9 billion each year.

PRIME also warned that smaller provinces could spend a substantial portion of their financial allocations on administrative expenses, leaving fewer resources for development and public services.

The estimates cover two hypothetical restructuring plans. A separate cost assessment for a 12-province arrangement has not been presented.

## Debate Over Constitutional Changes

Asif's remarks come amid reports of discussions about reorganising Pakistan into 12 provinces through a possible 28th Constitutional Amendment.

Under one reported proposal, Punjab could be divided into four provinces, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan into three each, and Sindh into two.

However, no formal constitutional proposal or final decision on provincial boundaries has been announced.

Pakistan Peoples Party leader Qamar Zaman Kaira said on Sunday that discussions about a 28th Amendment had not yet resulted in a written proposal.

He said political parties would be able to assess any proposed changes once the government presented a formal draft.

## MQM-P Renews Demand for New Administrative Units

Meanwhile, the Muttahida Qaumi Movement-Pakistan (MQM-P) has renewed its demand for new administrative units, particularly in urban Sindh.

MQM-P chairman Khalid Maqbool Siddiqui said the party would continue pressing for stronger local representation and greater authority for urban communities.

The party has linked its demand to public services, development spending and the distribution of resources.

Creating new provinces would require constitutional changes and political agreement. No government decision has been announced.
