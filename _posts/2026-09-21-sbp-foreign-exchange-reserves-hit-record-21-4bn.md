The State Bank of Pakistan's foreign exchange reserves rose to a record $21.39 billion in the week ended September 11 after Pakistan received proceeds from its Eurobond issuance, strengthening the country's external liquidity position.

Official SBP data show the central bank's reserves increased by $3.061 billion during the week, from about $18.33 billion to $21.389 billion. Commercial banks held a further $5.402 billion, taking Pakistan's total liquid foreign exchange reserves to $26.791 billion.

The distinction is important: the record $21.4 billion figure refers to reserves held by the State Bank, not Pakistan's combined liquid foreign exchange reserves.

The SBP attributed the sharp weekly increase to receipt of Eurobond proceeds. The rise was the largest weekly increase in the central bank's reserves since June 2025, according to published reporting on the latest data.

## External buffer strengthens

The increase gives Pakistan a substantially larger external buffer than it held during the severe foreign-exchange shortage of 2023, when SBP reserves at one point fell below $3 billion.

Foreign-exchange reserves are used to meet external payment needs, including imports and debt-related payments, and are closely watched because Pakistan depends on imported fuel, machinery and other goods paid for in foreign currencies.

Pakistan's total liquid reserves of $26.79 billion are also at their highest level in several years, although they remain below the roughly $27.1 billion recorded in September 2021.

Market analysis cited in local reporting put the latest reserve stock at the equivalent of just over three months of import cover, the first clear move above that benchmark since 2020.

The improvement comes as Pakistan has returned to international capital markets. The government has presented that return, alongside stronger remittances and services exports, as evidence of an improving external position.

## Government welcomes record level

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif welcomed the increase on September 21, describing the SBP's $21.4 billion reserve level as a historic high.

A statement issued by the Prime Minister's Office credited improved remittances, services exports, the current-account position and fiscal discipline with strengthening reserves. It also pointed to Pakistan's return to international capital markets as a sign of renewed investor confidence.

The statement said the higher reserve level would improve Pakistan's capacity to meet external payment obligations and provide greater protection against global economic shocks.

There is, however, an important distinction between the government's broader explanation of the improvement in reserves and the immediate reason for the latest weekly increase.

The Prime Minister's Office said the latest improvement had primarily been supported by the SBP's purchases of foreign exchange and was not driven by an increase in external borrowing. The State Bank's own weekly reserve announcement, meanwhile, said the $3.06 billion increase during the week ended September 11 followed receipt of proceeds from Pakistan's Eurobond issuance.

Both points can apply to different parts of the reserve build-up: the longer-term increase can reflect foreign-exchange purchases and other inflows, while the unusually large one-week jump was directly linked by the central bank to the bond proceeds.

## What the numbers show

The latest data therefore show a stronger reserve position, but also underline the importance of separating different measures of Pakistan's external finances.

SBP-held reserves stand at $21.389 billion. Commercial-bank reserves add $5.402 billion, producing the $26.791 billion total.

A larger reserve stock reduces immediate external financing pressure and gives the central bank more room to manage foreign-currency payment requirements. It does not by itself remove Pakistan's external vulnerabilities, which continue to depend on the trade balance, remittances, exports, debt repayments, energy prices and future financing flows.

The next reserve releases will show whether the record SBP level is sustained after the large Eurobond-related inflow.
