**RAWALPINDI —** The National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency has arrested 61 people in a raid on a call centre in Rawalpindi’s Satellite Town over allegations that operators were threatening foreign nationals while posing as loan-recovery agents.

The NCCIA said its Rawalpindi Circle carried out the operation in Commercial Market after receiving information about an allegedly illegal call centre. Officers seized 155 laptops and 71 mobile phones, with the devices and associated data taken for forensic examination.

A case has been registered and the investigation remains under way. The allegations against those arrested have not been established by a court, and the agency’s account represents the law-enforcement version at this stage of proceedings.

### Agency alleges foreign loan-recovery scheme

According to the NCCIA, the call centre was allegedly being used to contact foreign nationals in the name of recovering loans. The agency’s preliminary account says operators posed as recovery agents and used threats or intimidation to obtain money.

The NCCIA has not publicly set out individual allegations against each of the 61 people arrested. That distinction matters in a large workplace raid because the presence of a person at a site does not, by itself, establish their role or criminal liability.

Investigators are now expected to examine the seized computers, phones and data to determine how the operation functioned, identify communications and financial trails, and establish the roles of individual suspects.

### Digital devices sent for forensic analysis

The volume of equipment seized makes digital forensics a central part of the case. Investigators can use device records, communications and account data to test the agency’s initial allegations and determine whether activity can be linked to specific individuals.

The Rawalpindi raid followed other recent NCCIA operations against call centres accused of targeting people overseas through fraudulent financial schemes. Such cases have placed greater attention on Pakistan-based operations that use internet calling, digital payments and remote sales or support infrastructure to reach victims outside the country.

NCCIA Director General Syed Ali Nasir Rizvi said the agency would continue action against illegal call centres and cyber fraud. That statement reflects the agency’s enforcement position; the legal outcome of the Rawalpindi case will depend on the evidence collected and subsequent judicial proceedings.

### Investigation is continuing

For now, the confirmed procedural developments are the raid, 61 arrests, seizure of 155 laptops and 71 phones, registration of a case and continuing technical analysis.

Further details may emerge as investigators examine the devices and produce evidence for prosecution. Until then, claims that the arrested individuals operated an extortion or fraudulent loan-recovery scheme remain allegations attributed to the NCCIA rather than established findings of guilt.
