Pakistan's rupee remained near Rs277 to the US dollar in early October 2026, despite pressure from higher oil prices and the country's import requirements.

The exchange rate stood at approximately Rs277.1 on September 29, compared with around Rs281.4 a year earlier.

State Bank of Pakistan data recorded a September 29 revaluation rate of Rs277.1214 per dollar.

The currency's relative stability has coincided with stronger foreign exchange reserves and remittance inflows. State Bank reserves stood at $21.439 billion on September 25.

For Pakistan, a stable exchange rate can make it easier for importers and businesses to plan payments in foreign currency. It may also limit sudden increases in the rupee cost of imported goods.

However, the outlook remains exposed to global energy markets.

Pakistan imports significant quantities of fuel, so higher international oil prices can increase demand for dollars and raise the cost of transport and production.

A stronger reserve position provides some protection against external pressures, but it cannot eliminate the effects of expensive imports or future debt repayments.

The September comparison shows the rupee was modestly stronger than a year earlier. It does not guarantee the exchange rate will remain unchanged.

Future movements will depend on trade flows, remittances, international energy prices and broader market conditions.

For now, the available figures show a relatively steady currency despite continuing risks from the import bill. The stability is welcome for planning, but businesses still face uncertainty over future energy costs and foreign currency demand.
