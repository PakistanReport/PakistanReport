Pakistan's rupee has remained near Rs277 to the US dollar despite pressure from higher oil prices and a rising import bill.

The exchange rate stood at about Rs277.1 per dollar on September 29, compared with roughly Rs281.4 a year earlier, leaving the rupee about 1.5% stronger year-on-year.

State Bank of Pakistan data independently put the September 29 M2M revaluation rate at Rs277.1214 per dollar. The currency remained close to that level in early October.

The relative stability has been supported by stronger foreign-exchange reserves and remittance inflows, according to current reporting. SBP's foreign-exchange reserves stood at $21.439 billion on September 25.

Risks remain. Pakistan is heavily dependent on imported energy, so a sustained increase in international oil prices can raise the country's import bill and increase demand for foreign currency. Continuing Middle East tensions therefore remain a potential source of pressure rather than proof that the rupee will depreciate.
