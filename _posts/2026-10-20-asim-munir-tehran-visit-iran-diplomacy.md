Pakistan's Chief of Defence Forces and Chief of Army Staff, Field Marshal Asim Munir, is due to visit Tehran on Tuesday, October 20, for discussions with Iranian officials as Pakistan continues its diplomatic efforts amid tensions involving Iran and the United States.

Reuters reported the planned visit, citing a Pakistani official and Iranian media.

The trip follows earlier contacts between Munir and Iranian leaders concerning regional de-escalation and the Strait of Hormuz, a major shipping route for oil and gas exports.

## Why the visit matters

Pakistan shares a border with Iran and has direct interests in regional stability, including border security, energy supplies and cross-border trade.

Tensions involving Iran can affect international oil markets and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

Pakistan relies heavily on imported petroleum, making disruptions to energy supplies and transport routes an economic concern as well as a diplomatic one.

## Pakistan's diplomatic role

Pakistan has been involved in efforts to encourage dialogue between Iran and the United States during the regional conflict.

Earlier meetings between Pakistani and Iranian officials addressed measures to prevent further escalation, reopen the Strait of Hormuz and advance negotiations.

Iranian media reported that the latest visit would include talks on bilateral cooperation, regional developments and Pakistan's mediation efforts.

The precise agenda for Tuesday's meetings has not been publicly released.

## Security and regional cooperation

Talks involving senior military officials can cover border stability, regional threats and efforts to reduce the risk of escalation.

Pakistan and Iran also have longstanding interests in border management and trade, alongside their broader diplomatic relations.

For Islamabad, reduced regional tensions could ease uncertainty around energy costs and international trade routes while improving conditions for cooperation with its western neighbour.

## What comes next?

Official accounts of the visit will establish which Iranian officials Munir meets and whether the discussions produce further diplomatic steps.

At the time of this report, the visit had been reported as scheduled; no October 20 meeting outcome had been confirmed by the sources reviewed.
