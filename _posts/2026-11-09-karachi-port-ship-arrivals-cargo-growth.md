**KARACHI:** Karachi Port recorded a 13.7% increase in ship arrivals during the first four months of the current financial year, as higher cargo volumes and new international shipping connections boosted activity at the country's main seaport.

According to Karachi Port Trust (KPT) figures reported by Dawn, 764 vessels called at the port between July and October 2026, compared with 672 during the same period last year.

Total cargo handling increased by 3.8% to 19.286 million tonnes, up from 18.581 million tonnes a year earlier. Export cargo grew by 10.5%, while dry bulk cargo handling rose by 26.1%.

## New Shipping Services Strengthen Connections

The increase comes as international shipping companies adjust their routes amid disruptions in the Middle East.

Mediterranean Shipping Company (MSC) introduced a weekly direct service between Karachi and Europe in October through Karachi Gateway Terminal Limited.

A separate weekly feeder service connecting Karachi with Khor Fakkan in the United Arab Emirates has also improved the port's access to Gulf shipping networks.

These connections provide additional options for businesses moving goods between Pakistan and overseas markets.

The government has also introduced measures to attract transshipment cargo, which is unloaded at one port before being transferred to another vessel for delivery elsewhere.

## Lower Charges for Shipping Companies

In August, the Ministry of Maritime Affairs announced a package of concessions for shipping companies using Karachi Port and Port Qasim.

The measures include discounts of 20% to 80% on certain port charges, depending on the amount of transshipment cargo carried.

Karachi Port also offers up to 14 days of free terminal storage under the scheme, while private container terminal operators have reduced handling charges by as much as 25%.

The government has introduced changes to customs procedures, including faster clearance arrangements and permission for additional types of cargo to be transferred through off-dock facilities.

## Damaged Roads Remain a Problem

Despite the increase in shipping activity, poor road conditions around Karachi's port areas continue to create difficulties for transport companies.

KPT Chairman Rear Admiral (retd) Shahid Ahmed has raised concerns about damaged roads connecting the port with industrial areas and major highways.

He said delays in moving cargo were increasing transport costs and called for a portion of the Sindh Infrastructure Development Cess to be used for improving port access roads.

The Sindh government collects approximately Rs160 billion annually through the levy on goods entering the province through Karachi Port and Port Qasim.

Ahmed has proposed allocating half of that amount to road maintenance and improvements through cooperation between federal and provincial authorities.

## Port Expansion and Modernisation

Karachi Port has also improved its position in international performance rankings, moving up 30 places to 69th in the Container Port Performance Index for 2025.

KPT is also working to deepen the shipping channel leading into Karachi Port, allowing larger vessels to enter safely. The project is expected to be completed by December 2026.

Other plans include terminal expansion, improved cargo clearance systems and stronger road and rail connections.

During the 2025–26 financial year, Karachi Port handled a record 55.438 million tonnes of cargo and 2.749 million standard container units, with more than 2,100 vessel calls.
