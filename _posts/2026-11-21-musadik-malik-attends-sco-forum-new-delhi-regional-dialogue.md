ISLAMABAD: Federal Minister for Climate Change and Environmental Coordination Musadik Malik attended the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) Civilisational Dialogue Forum in New Delhi on Friday, calling for greater regional engagement and understanding through multilateral cooperation.

The visit marked the first by a Pakistani federal minister to India since former foreign minister Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari attended an SCO meeting in Goa in May 2023.

Speaking to reporters at the forum, Malik welcomed the opportunity for dialogue on the region's shared history, culture and civilisational heritage.

He expressed hope that relations among regional countries would improve despite continuing political tensions, emphasising the importance of cooperation on issues affecting the wider region.

Responding to questions about terrorism, Malik called for transparent, evidence-based mechanisms to address security concerns and build mutual trust.

However, he stressed that the forum was a multilateral gathering rather than a platform for bilateral negotiations between Pakistan and India.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the three-day event, scheduled for November 20–22, highlighting the role of cultural exchanges in promoting international understanding.

The gathering brings together representatives of SCO member states for discussions on civilisational heritage, cultural cooperation and people-to-people contacts.

Pakistan's Foreign Office said the country's participation reflected its commitment to constructive engagement within the SCO framework.

The visit comes amid strained relations between Islamabad and New Delhi following their military confrontation in May 2025. India has separately reiterated that its position on the Indus Waters Treaty remains unchanged.

Pakistan currently holds the rotating chairmanship of the SCO Council of Heads of State for 2026–27 and is scheduled to host the organisation's next summit in Islamabad in August 2027.

The New Delhi forum provides an opportunity for continued multilateral engagement, although no change in bilateral relations has been announced.
