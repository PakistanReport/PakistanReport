The State Bank of Pakistan has introduced new limits on the use of Tawarruq, a form of Islamic financing that allows customers to obtain cash through commodity transactions.

Under the rules, Tawarruq transactions generally cannot exceed **5% of an Islamic financial institution's total financing**. Tawarruq-based consumer financing has a separate ceiling of **10% of the institution's consumer financing portfolio**, with an exception for credit-card products issued under Tawarruq.

The rules took effect immediately under an [SBP circular issued on October 16](https://www.sbp.org.pk/circulars/ifpd-circular-no-03-of-2026).

## What Tawarruq means

In a typical Tawarruq transaction, a customer buys a commodity on deferred payment terms and then sells it to another buyer for immediate cash. The central bank wants these transactions to involve real commodities and to be used only when there is a genuine need.

The new limits apply to banks' financing portfolios. **They are not interest rates, fees or limits on an individual customer's loan.** One-time conversions of conventional loans into Islamic financing are exempt from the stated ceilings.

## Banks must show why the financing is needed

Institutions must document why other practical Shariah-compliant financing options cannot meet a customer's needs. They must explain alternatives and costs, keep written assessments and obtain evidence of how the funds were used.

The guidelines say Tawarruq cannot become a routine way to increase profits. They also bar its use simply to extend an existing financing arrangement or direct money into interest-bearing activities.

The commodity transaction itself must be genuine: the goods must be identifiable, ownership and possession requirements must be met, and arrangements that improperly return the commodity to its original seller are restricted.

## Oversight and penalties

Banks must adopt board-approved policies and obtain oversight from their Shariah boards or advisers. Management must report Tawarruq exposure to the board twice a year, while Shariah compliance reporting and internal audits are required quarterly.

The framework applies to Islamic banks and Islamic banking operations within conventional banks, with additional operational guidance for other Islamic finance providers.

SBP says institutions that breach the requirements may face penalties under banking law.

The detailed requirements are set out in the central bank's [Appendix A](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/documents/circulars/IFPD-2026-C3-Appendix-A.pdf) and [Annexure I](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/documents/circulars/IFPD-2026-C3-Annexure-I.pdf).
