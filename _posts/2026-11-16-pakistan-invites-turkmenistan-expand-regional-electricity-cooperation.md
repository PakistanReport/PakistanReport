**ISLAMABAD, 16 November 2026 —** Pakistan has invited Turkmenistan to participate in the CASA-1000 regional electricity project and called for faster progress on a separate transmission link connecting the two countries through Afghanistan.

The proposals were discussed during a meeting between Federal Power Minister Sardar Awais Ahmad Khan Leghari and Turkmenistan's Energy Minister Annageldi Saparov in Islamabad on Monday.

According to the Power Division, Pakistan has encouraged Tajikistan and Kyrgyzstan to explore extending the CASA-1000 network to Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan.

CASA-1000 is designed to facilitate electricity trade between Central and South Asia, including the transmission of surplus hydropower to Pakistan.

### Two-way electricity trade proposed

Leghari suggested examining whether CASA-1000 could support electricity transmission in both directions rather than only from Central Asia towards South Asia.

He said reciprocal electricity flows could allow participating countries to exchange power according to their requirements and improve the project's commercial viability.

The minister also raised Gwadar's future electricity needs, saying increased industrial and commercial activity would require additional supplies.

Pakistan proposed exploring higher electricity transmission capacity to Gwadar through Iran's grid, with technical experts examining possible arrangements involving Turkmenistan.

### TAP project faces technical work

The ministers separately reviewed the Turkmenistan-Afghanistan-Pakistan (TAP) electricity transmission project.

Leghari said seven essential sets of technical information were required to advance the scheme. Pakistan has already supplied three, while further information from Turkmenistan is awaited.

Saparov reaffirmed his government's commitment to the project and said Turkmenistan had completed its transmission line up to Afghanistan.

The remaining work includes feasibility assessments for a proposed 1,150-kilometre, 500-kilovolt transmission link from Afghanistan towards Quetta, designed for a capacity of 1,200 megawatts.

Both countries agreed to accelerate technical consultations and maintain regular contact to address outstanding issues.

The discussions form part of efforts to strengthen regional electricity trade and develop additional routes for supplying Pakistan's future energy needs.
