Pakistan has sought potential financing support from the US Export-Import Bank for Pakistan International Airlines' aircraft plans, including interest in Boeing 787 Dreamliners, as Islamabad explores funding for aviation and refinery projects.

Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb discussed the proposals with US EXIM Chairman John Jovanovich in New York. According to an official Pakistani statement, PIA's requirements include a possible financing package covering aircraft and engines.

The talks also covered spare parts needed to return grounded Boeing 777 aircraft to service and possible financing for pre-delivery payments. Refinery upgrades were identified as another area where US EXIM support could potentially be considered.

The discussions do not amount to a completed Boeing 787 order or an approved EXIM financing agreement. The two sides agreed to continue work on a broader cooperation framework while prioritising specific potential transactions.

Aurangzeb had separately met Boeing Global President Brendan Nelson earlier in New York, where PIA fleet modernisation, aircraft availability, leasing options, engines, spare parts and maintenance were discussed.

Any eventual aircraft acquisition or financing package would depend on further commercial and financing negotiations.
