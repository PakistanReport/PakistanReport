**ISLAMABAD, November 14, 2026 —** The federal government has set December 31 as the target for making Islamabad Pakistan's first fully cashless city, with approximately 26,000 merchants already connected to digital payment facilities.

The initiative was reviewed at a meeting chaired by Minister of State for Interior Talal Chaudhry, who directed authorities to accelerate the transition across government departments, commercial markets and public services.

Under the announced timetable, departments operating under the Islamabad Capital Territory administration and Capital Development Authority (CDA) are to stop accepting cash payments from December 1.

The December 31 target focuses on extending digital payment facilities to businesses throughout the capital, including shops, restaurants, petrol stations, commercial centres and street vendors.

Authorities are promoting QR-code payments and other electronic transaction methods to reduce dependence on cash and improve financial transparency.

Digital payment facilities are also being expanded at the CDA's One-Window Facilitation Centre, weekly bazaars and public transport services, including metro and electric buses.

The initiative forms part of the wider Cashless Pakistan programme, which aims to increase digital transactions and improve the documentation of economic activity.

According to figures reported during the programme's implementation, the number of active digital-payment merchants nationwide has increased from approximately 500,000 to 2.03 million.

Officials are also working with banks and payment service providers to improve access to electronic transactions and encourage adoption among smaller businesses.

However, reliable internet connectivity, access to banking services and the ability of small traders to use digital payment systems remain important implementation challenges.

The government's announcement distinguishes between ending cash acceptance at designated public offices and ensuring that private merchants can offer digital payment options.

The coming weeks will determine how effectively the capital's businesses and public institutions meet the announced deadlines.
