**KARACHI:** The Pakistan Petroleum Dealers Association (PPDA) has threatened a nationwide shutdown of petrol stations unless the federal government addresses its concerns over fuel pricing and retail margins within 15 days.

Speaking at the Karachi Press Club on Monday, PPDA Chairman Malik Khuda Bakhsh called for petroleum prices to remain fixed for 30 days at a time, replacing the daily pricing mechanism introduced in July.

The association argues that frequent downward price revisions are causing financial losses for retailers that purchase fuel at higher rates but must subsequently sell their remaining stocks at reduced government-notified prices.

Dealers have also demanded an increase in their retail margin to 8 per cent of the fuel selling price and called for the government to retain control over petroleum pricing rather than deregulate the market.

They have urged officials to consult industry representatives before introducing further petroleum market deregulation measures.

Pakistan adopted daily petroleum price reviews in July following disruptions to international energy markets caused by the US-Iran conflict. The mechanism was intended to allow domestic prices to respond more quickly to global market movements.

In August, the Economic Coordination Committee approved an increase in dealers' margins from Rs8.64 to Rs9.98 per litre, effective from September 1, following an earlier strike warning.

However, the increase fell short of the association's demand for a percentage-based margin.

PPDA representatives also claimed that reduced fuel supplies from some refineries were creating additional difficulties for filling stations.

The association says it represents approximately 14,000 petrol stations nationwide.

Its leadership has called for negotiations with the government before the deadline expires, warning that unresolved concerns could lead to the closure of filling stations across Pakistan.
