**October 31, 2026** — Global oil prices have climbed to their highest levels in six months as renewed security threats in the Middle East raise concerns about disruptions to energy supplies.

Brent crude futures for December delivery settled at $117.64 per barrel on October 30, an increase of 2.4% during the trading session.

US West Texas Intermediate crude rose 2.08% to $104.12 per barrel.

Both benchmarks reached their highest closing levels since late April, reflecting growing uncertainty over shipping routes and regional energy infrastructure.

The latest increase followed reports that an oil tanker was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz, one of the world's most important routes for transporting crude oil.

The incident added to concerns about the security of commercial shipping through the Gulf.

There have also been reported attacks and threats involving energy facilities in Saudi Arabia, increasing fears of further disruption.

During October, Brent crude gained 13.6%, while US crude prices increased by 15.2%.

Higher oil prices can increase fuel and transport costs worldwide, particularly in countries that depend heavily on imported petroleum.

For Pakistan, sustained increases in international crude prices could put additional pressure on fuel prices, import payments and inflation.

However, the impact on domestic consumers will depend on exchange rates, government taxes and future fuel pricing decisions.

Energy markets are expected to remain sensitive to developments in the Middle East, with traders closely watching shipping security and the possibility of further escalation.

The direction of prices will also depend on whether regional producers can maintain exports despite the heightened risks.
