Pakistan’s economy could grow by more than 4% in the current fiscal year despite higher global oil prices, Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb told Reuters during the IMF and World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok on Monday.

Aurangzeb said Pakistan had secured adequate fuel supplies despite disruptions linked to the Middle East conflict and that agriculture remained resilient. His growth estimate is a forecast, not a final official GDP figure.

The minister said the government aims to privatise five or six state-owned entities during the fiscal year, potentially raising more than $1 billion. Power distribution companies and airport operations are among the areas under consideration.

Pakistan remains vulnerable to higher energy import costs, inflation and financing pressures. The timing and proceeds of individual privatisations are uncertain, and the government’s economic outlook depends on conditions over the remainder of the year.
