**KARACHI:** The European Union has urged Pakistan to show measurable progress in implementing commitments associated with the Generalised Scheme of Preferences Plus (GSP+), underscoring the importance of governance and rights-related reforms to preferential trade access.

EU Ambassador Raimundas Karoblis discussed the matter with the Pakistan Textile Council, focusing on how Pakistan's performance is assessed and what will matter in future reviews. The issue is especially consequential for the country's textile and clothing exporters.

## Implementation, not merely legislation

The ambassador stressed that the EU is seeking effective implementation of obligations Pakistan has already accepted under international conventions, including commitments related to human rights and the rule of law. Ratifying conventions or passing laws establishes formal obligations; assessments also consider their practical enforcement. A credible response requires institutions to document improvements and coordinate between federal and provincial authorities.

## Why GSP+ matters

GSP+ grants qualifying countries preferential access to the EU market for numerous products, subject to specified conditions. Textiles and apparel reportedly account for approximately 70% to 76% of Pakistan's exports to the bloc. Tariff preferences can influence buyers' sourcing decisions in a highly competitive industry, affecting factories, workers and associated businesses.

## A more demanding review

The EU has called for verifiable improvements and an action plan containing measures, deadlines and indicators. Exporters need predictability because orders and sourcing arrangements are negotiated months in advance. Uncertainty about future tariff treatment can influence commercial decisions even before any formal change.

The ambassador's comments **do not mean that Pakistan has lost GSP+ status**. No withdrawal was announced in the reported discussions. The question is whether Pakistan can demonstrate sufficient implementation to preserve its position under the evolving framework.

## What comes next

Government departments and industry representatives must turn commitments into evidence of progress. For businesses, monitoring the review process and preparing for closer scrutiny from customers will be important. The central commercial message is that future access will increasingly depend on results rather than assurances.
