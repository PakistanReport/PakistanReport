Pakistan’s textile and clothing exports increased by 5.26% in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026–27, but exporters have warned that higher production costs and disrupted regional trade could weaken shipments in the months ahead.

According to Pakistan Bureau of Statistics figures released on 15 October, textile and clothing exports totalled **$5.026 billion** during July–September 2026, compared with **$4.775 billion** in the same period a year earlier. The increase was approximately $251 million.

### Garments lead the increase

Ready-made garment exports rose **15.69% by value**, while knitwear exports increased **4.14%**. Cotton yarn exports grew **24.47%**, and towel exports rose by around **10%**, according to figures reported by *Business Recorder*. Bedwear exports, by contrast, slipped slightly in value.

These differences matter because stronger sales in some finished-garment categories do not necessarily indicate that every part of the textile supply chain is recovering at the same pace.

### Costs and disruptions remain a concern

Exporters have cited expensive inputs and logistical uncertainty as constraints on competitiveness. The suspension of trade with Afghanistan and weaker demand in parts of the Middle East amid regional conflict have added pressure to an industry that depends on reliable access to overseas markets.

The sector remains a major source of manufacturing employment and foreign-exchange earnings. Its performance over the next quarter will help show whether the first-quarter increase can be sustained or whether the disruption to regional trade is beginning to outweigh gains in higher-value garments.
