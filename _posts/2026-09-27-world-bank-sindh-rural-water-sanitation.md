**ISLAMABAD —** The World Bank has proposed a $540.25 million rural water, sanitation and hygiene programme for Sindh aimed at expanding access to safer water and sanitation services for about 1.5 million people, according to project documents and development-bank disclosures.

The Sindh Transformational Accelerated Rural Water Supply, Sanitation and Hygiene Services Phase 1 project, known as STAR-WASH-1, remains at the proposal stage. The financing plan includes $300 million from the World Bank, $150 million in co-financing from the Asian Development Bank and $90 million in counterpart funding, with a small remaining financing gap.

ADB lists the project as proposed and says it is intended to increase access to safely managed water supply and sanitation services in rural areas across 16 of Sindh’s 23 districts. World Bank documentation identifies the programme as an investment project focused on water, sanitation and waste management.

The proposal comes against persistent gaps in rural water and sanitation coverage. World Bank project material cited in reporting says only 29% of Sindh’s rural population has access to safely managed water, while open defecation and unsafe sanitation remain significant public-health concerns.

Under the proposed programme, about 1.5 million people are expected to gain at least basic water services, including roughly 750,000 people receiving safely managed water. The programme also targets safely managed sanitation services for around 1.5 million people and a reduction in point-of-use water contamination.

The largest component would finance water-supply, sanitation and hygiene infrastructure, including single- and multi-village water schemes, household connections, drainage, wastewater and fecal-sludge management, and solarisation. Project plans envisage relying mainly on canal water and freshwater sources near perennial canals, with reverse osmosis used selectively where water quality requires it.

The programme also includes institutional reforms intended to strengthen long-term operation and maintenance of rural water systems. These include plans for a provincial water-quality surveillance programme, a user-charge policy and a dedicated operations and maintenance fund.

The project has not yet received final approval. ADB continues to classify STAR-WASH-1 as proposed, while World Bank project reporting has indicated appraisal and approval steps are still to follow. Any financing, implementation timetable and final project scope therefore remain subject to the relevant approval processes.
