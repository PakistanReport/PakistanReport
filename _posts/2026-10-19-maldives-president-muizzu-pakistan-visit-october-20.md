Maldives President Mohamed Muizzu will begin a three-day state visit to Pakistan on Tuesday, October 20, with discussions expected to focus on trade, investment and broader bilateral cooperation.

The visit, taking place at the invitation of Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, will continue until October 22 and marks the first trip to Pakistan by a Maldivian president in more than a decade.

Muizzu is scheduled to hold talks with Sharif and meet President Asif Ali Zardari. He will be accompanied by First Lady Sajidha Mohamed, cabinet ministers and senior officials.

The visit comes as Pakistan and the Maldives mark 60 years of diplomatic relations, providing an opportunity for both governments to review their longstanding ties and identify areas for closer engagement.

## Economic cooperation in focus

According to Pakistan's Foreign Office, discussions will cover trade, investment, tourism, education, healthcare, agriculture, defence, maritime security and climate change.

A Pakistan-Maldives Business Forum is also expected to take place during the visit, bringing together representatives of the two countries' business communities.

Several agreements and memorandums of understanding are anticipated, although their scope and final terms have not yet been made public.

The meetings are also expected to include an exchange of views on regional and international developments.

For Pakistan, the visit offers an opportunity to strengthen its diplomatic and commercial engagement with a fellow South Asian country situated along important Indian Ocean routes.

The Maldives, whose economy is closely tied to tourism, has an interest in expanding its international partnerships and cooperation in sectors such as education, healthcare and trade.

## Renewed high-level engagement

Pakistan and the Maldives established diplomatic relations in 1966 and have maintained cooperation through bilateral exchanges and multilateral organisations.

However, visits at the presidential level have been infrequent. The last Maldivian president to travel to Pakistan was Abdulla Yameen Abdul Gayoom in 2015.

Muizzu's arrival will therefore represent a renewed period of high-level engagement between the two countries.

While the visit is expected to produce new cooperation agreements, its longer-term significance will depend on how far the commitments translate into practical economic and institutional partnerships.

Further details are expected following the leaders' meetings and the formal announcement of any agreements concluded during the visit.
