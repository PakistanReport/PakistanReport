**DUBAI:** A tanker carrying liquefied natural gas (LNG) struck a naval mine and suffered an explosion in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday, according to Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy, adding to a series of incidents affecting commercial shipping in the strategic waterway.

Iranian state media said the vessel had entered a route described by Iranian authorities as unauthorised and dangerous. The tanker was not immediately identified, and details about possible casualties or the extent of the damage were unavailable.

The latest incident followed reports from Iran's military on Thursday that four oil tankers had been hit by explosions in the strait.

Iranian authorities said two supertankers were disabled on Wednesday after allegedly ignoring navigation warnings. Two additional tankers reportedly caught fire on Thursday after entering restricted waters.

Iran attributed the incidents to naval mines, although the accounts have not been independently verified.

Separately, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported that a commercial vessel had been struck by an unidentified projectile northwest of Ras Al-Khaimah in the United Arab Emirates. The incident caused an engine-room fire, but the crew was evacuated.

Shipping incidents have continued despite expectations of reduced tensions between Iran and the United States.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a crucial passage for international oil and gas shipments. Continued disruption could increase transport risks and create uncertainty for energy-importing countries, including Pakistan.
