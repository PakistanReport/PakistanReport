Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky pressed European leaders for additional military and financial support at a Brussels summit on Friday, October 16, as disagreements over a United States–Russia diesel arrangement complicated Western efforts to maintain pressure on Moscow.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the European Union would maintain sanctions against Russia until the war ends. She said officials were working on a 22nd package of sanctions and a fresh winter support package for Ukraine. The stance contrasted with Washington's decision to ease restrictions linked to Russian diesel supplies.

## A dispute over energy sanctions

The US arrangement, announced after contacts between President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin, has prompted questions in Kyiv and European capitals about the effect of sanctions relief on Russia's energy income. Zelensky had criticised the decision in an interview earlier in October, arguing that easing restrictions without corresponding Russian concessions was unfair to Ukraine.

At the Brussels summit, the Ukrainian president emphasised that Kyiv had seen no convincing sign that Moscow was ready to end the war. He said the coming winter required preparations not only for energy supplies but also for air defence and defence financing.

EU leaders expressed political support for Ukraine, although public reporting on Friday did not establish a new, collectively approved funding commitment from the summit. That distinction matters: expressions of solidarity do not automatically resolve Kyiv's financing requirements.

## Europe's position and the winter ahead

European leaders are trying to sustain support for Ukraine while managing strains in transatlantic relations. Washington's energy policy has raised concern among some European officials, who argue that restrictions on Russian energy revenues are a key element of economic pressure on Moscow.

The EU's position is also shaped by continued Russian attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure. European officials have warned that power and water systems remain vulnerable as colder weather approaches.

For Kyiv, the immediate test is whether the diplomatic backing expressed in Brussels translates into timely funding, air-defence equipment and sustained sanctions enforcement. The summit demonstrated continuing European support, but also highlighted disagreements among Ukraine's partners over the use of energy restrictions.
