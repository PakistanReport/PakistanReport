**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan State Oil (PSO) and SSGC LPG Limited (SLL) face a potential loss of Rs1 billion on a 20,000-tonne liquefied petroleum gas shipment as high import costs, weak buyer demand and unloading delays increase financial pressure.

The cargo arrived from Abu Dhabi at Engro Vopak Terminal in Port Qasim on November 18, following changes to the government's LPG import and pricing policy earlier this month.

SLL holds 15,000 tonnes of the shipment, while PSO accounts for the remaining 5,000 tonnes.

According to shipping and customs details reported by *Business Recorder*, the gas was purchased at $1,235 per tonne, including a $485 premium over the Saudi Aramco Contract Price.

Its estimated landed cost is Rs465,000 per tonne, compared with domestic wholesale market rates that had fallen below Rs400,000 per tonne, making the imported cargo difficult to sell profitably.

The vessel was also too large to use the importer's own terminal, requiring unloading at Engro Vopak at an additional handling cost of $38 per tonne.

Of SLL's allocation, only 6,000 tonnes had been discharged, leaving 9,000 tonnes aboard because of insufficient onshore storage capacity.

Further delays could trigger demurrage charges if the vessel remains at port beyond its permitted unloading period.

SLL has separately warned customers that future LPG allocations will favour buyers who honour their purchasing commitments. The company said some customers withdrew from agreed purchases or failed to collect confirmed quantities as market conditions changed.

PSO has set its LPG selling price at Rs509.86 per kilogram, excluding applicable freight charges.

The estimated Rs1 billion exposure remains a potential loss rather than a confirmed financial outcome, with the eventual impact depending on sales, storage arrangements and additional port costs.
