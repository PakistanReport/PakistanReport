Oil prices are heading for a roughly 14% gain in September as stalled US-Iran diplomacy keeps geopolitical risk in focus, even as crude export flows from the Gulf have improved.

Brent crude was trading around $103 a barrel on Wednesday, according to Reuters, leaving the international benchmark on course for a substantial monthly increase.

The move illustrates the competing forces shaping the oil market: physical export flows have recovered from earlier disruption, but traders continue to price risks linked to the Middle East and uncertainty over the diplomatic track between Washington and Tehran.

## Gulf exports improve

Crude movements from the region have strengthened, easing some immediate concerns about the availability of physical supply.

Improved flows would normally reduce some of the risk premium attached to oil. September's price performance, however, shows that the market is also responding to the possibility of renewed disruption and uncertainty surrounding US-Iran relations.

## US-Iran talks remain in focus

Diplomatic efforts between the United States and Iran have not produced a breakthrough, leaving sanctions and regional security questions unresolved.

Those negotiations are relevant to energy markets because Iran is a major oil producer and tensions involving Tehran can affect expectations for regional production and shipping.

The diplomatic situation is one factor among several influencing prices. Oil markets also respond to global demand expectations, inventories, production policy, currency movements and economic data, making it misleading to attribute the monthly rise to a single cause.

## Why a 14% monthly rise matters

A gain of around 14% in one month is large enough to affect businesses and governments well beyond oil-producing states.

For Pakistan, movements in global petroleum prices matter because imported crude and refined products form an important part of the country's external energy bill. Domestic pump prices, however, are not determined by Brent alone and also reflect exchange rates, taxes, levies and the government's pricing calculations.

The September rally therefore does not automatically translate into an equivalent change at Pakistani fuel stations. As the month closes, Gulf export flows have improved, but geopolitical and diplomatic uncertainty continues to influence crude prices.
