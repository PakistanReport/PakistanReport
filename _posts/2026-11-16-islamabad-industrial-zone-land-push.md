**ISLAMABAD, 16 November 2026 —** Islamabad's business community has renewed its demand for up to 1,000 acres of land for a proposed special economic zone, bringing attention to a government-backed industrial project that has remained under development for several years.

The Islamabad Chamber of Commerce and Industry (ICCI) has approached the federal and Punjab governments for 800–1,000 acres to establish the Islamabad Model Special Economic Zone.

ICCI President Muhammad Zikria Akbar Zia said the capital's existing industrial areas, developed around five decades ago, had limited capacity to accommodate new factories and expanding businesses.

He said the chamber had taken up the matter with Special Investment Facilitation Council (SIFC) National Coordinator Lieutenant General Asad ur Rehman Afzal Cheema and Punjab Industries Minister Chaudhry Shafay Hussain.

The proposal is not new.

Government planning records show that the Central Development Working Party approved Rs500 million in December 2022 for feasibility studies and land acquisition associated with a proposed 1,000-acre Islamabad Model Special Economic Zone.

A Ministry of Finance performance report subsequently recorded that land acquisition proceedings had been initiated in July 2023 and that a groundbreaking ceremony took place the same month.

However, the latest discussions indicate that securing suitable land remains an unresolved issue.

The federal government's budget for FY2026–27 allocates Rs20.95 million to the project's feasibility and land-acquisition programme, according to official budget documents.

In a meeting with ICCI representatives late last month, the SIFC national coordinator said consultations with the Punjab government were underway to identify an appropriate location.

Punjab Governor Sardar Saleem Haider Khan also assured the chamber that he would raise its land-allocation request with the relevant authorities.

Business representatives say a modern industrial zone could provide space for small and medium-sized enterprises, attract domestic and foreign investment and reduce the movement of industrial activity away from the capital.

Zia has also called for a consistent economic policy framework extending at least ten years to encourage long-term investment.

The proposal comes as the federal government continues broader reforms to special economic zones. In October, the Board of Approvals authorised Karachi Industrial Park as a federal special economic zone and approved a land-leasing policy for public-sector industrial estates in Punjab.

Those decisions do not amount to approval of the Islamabad project.

Despite earlier planning work and budget allocations, no fresh official confirmation has been identified of a final site allocation, completed land acquisition or a construction timetable for the Islamabad Model Special Economic Zone.
