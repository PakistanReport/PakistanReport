Pakistan’s total public debt stood at **Rs86.7 trillion at the end of June 2026**, as the government set out a borrowing plan for the current fiscal year that puts gross financing requirements at **Rs28.647 trillion**.

The Finance Division published its Annual Borrowing Plan for FY2026-27 on September 29, setting out how the government intends to finance the federal deficit and refinance debt falling due during the year.

The debt stock at end-June comprised **Rs59.4 trillion in domestic debt** and **Rs27.3 trillion equivalent in external debt**, according to figures reported from the plan.

## Rs6.86 trillion in net additional borrowing planned

The government plans net additional borrowing of about **Rs6.86 trillion** during FY27. That consists of **Rs6.046 trillion in net domestic borrowing** and **Rs813 billion in net external financing**.

That figure is different from the much larger Rs28.647 trillion gross financing requirement. Gross financing requirements include both financing for the fiscal deficit and the refinancing of debt that matures during the year. They therefore should not be read as the amount by which Pakistan’s debt stock is expected to increase.

The federal fiscal deficit is projected at **Rs7.020 trillion**. After domestic and external maturities are included, the annual borrowing plan puts total gross financing needs at approximately 20% of GDP for FY27.

## Government targets longer-term domestic debt

The borrowing plan also sets out changes in the composition of domestic financing. The government intends to reduce reliance on short-term Treasury bills on a net issuance basis and increase the use of medium- and longer-term instruments.

Net issuance of Pakistan Investment Bonds is planned at **Rs4.58 trillion**, with fixed-rate PIBs targeted to account for more than half of new issuance. The government also plans Ijara Sukuk and Bai Muajjal transactions, while continuing liability-management operations such as debt buybacks and exchanges when market conditions and fiscal space allow.

The strategy is intended to reduce refinancing risk by limiting dependence on short-maturity borrowing, although longer maturities do not remove the government’s overall financing burden.

## External financing remains part of the plan

Net external financing is projected at **Rs813 billion**, equivalent to about **$2.8 billion** in the borrowing plan. Multilateral lenders are expected to remain an important source of external funding, while the government also intends to use international capital markets when conditions permit.

The plan therefore combines new financing with the refinancing of existing obligations and changes to the maturity profile of domestic debt.

For readers, the central distinction is between Pakistan’s **Rs86.7 trillion existing public-debt stock**, the **Rs28.647 trillion gross financing requirement** for FY27 and the roughly **Rs6.86 trillion in planned net additional borrowing**. They measure different parts of the government’s debt and financing position and should not be treated as interchangeable figures.
