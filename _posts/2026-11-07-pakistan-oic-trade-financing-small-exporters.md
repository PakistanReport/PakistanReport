**ISLAMABAD, November 7, 2026 —** Federal Minister for Commerce Jam Kamal Khan has urged member countries of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) to improve access to trade financing, simplify cross-border transactions and strengthen support for smaller exporters.

Speaking on behalf of OIC member states at the closing session of the 42nd Ministerial Session of the Standing Committee for Economic and Commercial Cooperation (COMCEC) in Istanbul on November 6, he outlined measures to address financial and administrative barriers affecting international trade.

## Financing Barriers Affect Small Businesses

Jam Kamal said micro, small and medium-sized enterprises faced difficulties obtaining affordable financing because of high collateral requirements, regulatory differences, transaction costs and lengthy procedures.

He called for closer alignment of national regulatory frameworks, stronger export credit guarantees and financing products designed for smaller businesses.

The minister also encouraged greater use of Islamic financial instruments, including risk-sharing arrangements and Shariah-compliant export credit insurance.

## Digital Systems Could Reduce Trade Costs

The commerce minister proposed closer integration of customs and banking systems to simplify cross-border payments and documentation.

He highlighted electronic trade documents and single-window platforms as ways to reduce processing costs and administrative delays.

Jam Kamal also called for targeted financing, technical assistance and financial literacy programmes for businesses led by women and young entrepreneurs.

## Wider Economic Cooperation

The COMCEC discussions covered transport connectivity, agricultural productivity, food security, sustainable tourism and digital transformation.

Jam Kamal urged member countries to strengthen agricultural research, technology transfer and investment in more resilient food supply chains.

He also identified private-sector partnerships as important for expanding investment, industrial development and employment.

The minister called for COMCEC recommendations to be translated into measurable projects through institutional cooperation and implementation mechanisms.
