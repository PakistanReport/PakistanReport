The Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Bar Council has announced a province-wide lawyers' strike for Monday, October 19, and plans to challenge the imposition of emergency measures and governor's rule in the Peshawar High Court. The announcement adds a legal dimension to the dispute over federal intervention in the province.

Bar council representatives said at a press conference following a general body meeting that lawyers would stay away from court appearances on Monday. They described the emergency and governor's rule as unconstitutional and undemocratic, allegations that reflect the council's position rather than a judicial ruling.

The council said it had formed a legal panel to prepare a constitutional petition. Its representatives indicated they wanted the matter heard alongside related challenges on October 20. Reporting also described a separate petition by PTI chairman Barrister Gohar Ali Khan scheduled for preliminary hearing on October 20; the hearing status should be rechecked before publication.

The council also called for the production before a court, or release, of former chief minister Sohail Afridi and provincial assembly speaker Babar Saleem Swati. It raised concerns about the reported detention of lawyer Ameer Ijaz and announced a committee to address alleged harassment of legal professionals. These allegations require independent adjudication.

The federal government imposed emergency measures and governor's rule on October 11, citing security conditions and the provincial administration's alleged failure to cooperate sufficiently in counterterrorism efforts. PTI and other critics dispute the justification and have challenged the move politically and legally.

The dispute raises questions about the constitutional requirements for emergency administration, provincial autonomy and the courts' role in reviewing federal action. A planned legal challenge should not be confused with a court decision invalidating the measures.

The bar council also announced a meeting of district and tehsil bar association presidents for October 24 to consider further steps. As of October 18, the October 19 strike was **announced but had not yet taken place**.
