**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan has announced plans to increase the share of goods transported by rail to 20%, as it seeks to reduce its heavy dependence on road freight and develop cleaner transport links.

Federal Minister for Communications Abdul Aleem Khan outlined the government's plans at a ministerial meeting on green freight corridors during the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Türkiye, on Tuesday.

According to the minister, about 96% of domestic freight in Pakistan is currently carried by road. The government intends to increase rail's share through its Railway Plan 2026, including the proposed upgrade of the Main Line-1 railway.

Khan also highlighted plans under the National Electric Vehicle Policy 2025–30 to establish 3,000 charging stations along major transport routes.

Pakistan formally endorsed the Global Green Freight Corridors Initiative at the meeting, which was co-hosted by Türkiye's transport minister, Abdulkadir Uraloğlu, and two United Nations regional commissions.

The initiative seeks to encourage cleaner freight technology, improved route planning, digital transport systems and access to climate financing.

Khan said Pakistan's road network extends over 500,000 kilometres, while its railway network covers approximately 7,800 kilometres. He pointed to the country's position between China, Central Asia and the Middle East as an opportunity to develop regional cargo routes.

The minister also reported a 200% increase in international road transit movements under the TIR system, which allows goods to move across borders under internationally recognised customs procedures.

Pakistan is working to link advance cargo information with the Pakistan Single Window system and introduce electronic TIR documentation to reduce paperwork at border crossings.

Khan said the government was looking to public funding, multilateral lenders and private investment to support transport projects, including the M-6 motorway and the ML-1 railway upgrade.

Pakistan also expressed support for working with international partners on pilot green freight corridors.
