**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan spent just over Rs104 billion on federal development projects during the first four months of the current financial year, accounting for only 10.4% of the government's Rs1 trillion annual development budget.

Figures released by the Ministry of Planning, Development and Special Initiatives show that Rs412 billion had been authorised for development activities between July and October 2026, but actual expenditure remained considerably lower.

The authorised amount represented approximately 41% of the annual Public Sector Development Programme (PSDP), while spending accounted for only about a quarter of the funds authorised.

The figures highlight a substantial difference between the funding approved for development projects and the money actually spent on implementation.

The federal development programme for 2026–27 includes Rs682 billion for ministries and divisions, Rs313 billion for government corporations, Rs4 billion for project liabilities and Rs1 billion for new initiatives under the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor's second phase.

Water resources received the largest authorisation among federal ministries, with approximately Rs91 billion approved against an annual allocation of Rs103 billion.

However, the Water Resources Division spent less than Rs19 billion during the four-month period.

The Cabinet Division was authorised to use more than Rs60 billion from its Rs64 billion allocation, but reported expenditure of only about Rs60 million.

Development spending in the transport sector also remained limited.

The National Highway Authority spent approximately Rs13 billion against its annual allocation of Rs225 billion, despite receiving authorisation for nearly Rs79 billion.

The Railways Division utilised around Rs5 billion from an annual development allocation of Rs41 billion.

In the power sector, entities including the National Transmission and Despatch Company and Pakistan Electric Power Company spent approximately Rs16 billion against an annual allocation of Rs88 billion.

Education and healthcare projects recorded varying levels of expenditure.

The Higher Education Commission spent approximately Rs5 billion from its Rs46 billion annual allocation, while the Federal Education and Professional Training Division utilised nearly Rs7 billion.

The National Health Services Division also spent approximately Rs7 billion against its annual allocation of Rs16 billion.

For development programmes involving provinces and special areas, approximately Rs24 billion was spent from an annual allocation of Rs233 billion.

The Information Technology and Telecommunication Division reported expenditure of about Rs1.5 billion against its Rs20 billion allocation.

Pakistan's development budget finances infrastructure, public services and other government projects, including roads, water systems, educational facilities and energy networks.

Slow utilisation can delay project implementation, although the published figures do not establish the reasons for the underspending in individual departments.

The latest spending figures come as the government seeks to manage public finances while maintaining funding for development priorities.

Under the federal government's expenditure arrangements, development funds are released in stages throughout the financial year, with the largest share scheduled for the final quarter.
