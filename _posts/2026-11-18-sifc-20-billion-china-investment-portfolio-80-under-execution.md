**ISLAMABAD, 18 November 2026 —** Pakistan's Special Investment Facilitation Council (SIFC) has reported a China-linked investment portfolio worth approximately $20 billion, while disclosing that only 80 of 553 investment commitments mapped across government departments are under execution.

According to an SIFC presentation prepared for the Senate, the 80 commitments represent 14.47% of the total and carry an indicative execution value of $2.64 billion.

### China-linked projects span multiple sectors

The council's China investment programme covers manufacturing, agriculture, energy storage, pharmaceuticals and technology.

A Pakistan-China business meeting in Beijing in September 2025 generated 129 memorandums of understanding and joint ventures worth $9.3 billion, with $1.2 billion subsequently classified as under execution.

An agriculture investment conference in Islamabad in January 2026 produced 99 agreements valued at $2.1 billion, of which $56 million was reported under execution.

Similarly, a battery energy storage forum in Lahore generated commitments worth $636 million, including $136 million under execution.

A separate investment roundtable in Hangzhou identified a $6.6 billion pipeline, with $1.2 billion reported under execution.

Among the projects identified were a proposed $1.2 billion fertiliser facility involving Fauji Fertilizer Company and China's Hualu Engineering, a $240 million electric vehicle and solar manufacturing initiative, and a $120 million data centre.

Other proposals cover lithium battery assembly, artificial intelligence infrastructure and automated distribution facilities.

### Saudi and European investment engagement

The SIFC presentation also reported approximately $2.3 billion in inflows associated with agreements between Pakistani and Saudi businesses.

The 34 agreements signed in 2024 included 13 investment-related arrangements, 14 trade agreements and seven linked to remittances.

Because these figures cover investment, trade and remittances, the reported $2.3 billion should not be treated entirely as foreign direct investment.

Separately, the council highlighted the EU-Pakistan Business Forum held in Islamabad in April 2026, where the European Investment Bank signed financing agreements worth €160 million for two projects.

### Focus turns to implementation

The SIFC has also identified 38 investment opportunities across 11 sectors, with an indicative value exceeding $21 billion.

A comprehensive annual performance report is being prepared to provide greater clarity on investment commitments, project execution and actual capital deployment. According to reporting on the government's reform timetable, a draft is expected in December 2026, followed by a final report in March 2027.

The disclosures underline the scale of Pakistan's investment ambitions, but the conversion of commitments into funded and operational projects remains the central measure of progress.
