**ISLAMABAD, November 14, 2026 —** Pakistan has nominated Federal Minister for Climate Change and Environmental Coordination Musadik Masood Malik to attend a regional forum in New Delhi next week, in what would be the first visit to India by a Pakistani federal minister in more than three years.

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs confirmed on Saturday that Malik would represent Pakistan at the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) Civilisational Dialogue, scheduled for November 20–22.

The three-day gathering will bring together representatives from SCO member countries to promote cultural understanding, preserve shared heritage and encourage exchanges between their peoples.

Pakistan's Foreign Office said Islamabad remained committed to dialogue and people-to-people engagement within the regional organisation.

The announcement follows India's invitation to SCO member states for the inaugural forum, which is being organised by its ministries of Culture and External Affairs.

Indian media had previously reported that Pakistan's Minister for National Heritage and Culture, Aurangzeb Khan Khichi, was invited to participate. Islamabad has instead selected Malik to lead its representation.

The planned visit is notable because ministerial exchanges between Pakistan and India have remained limited amid longstanding disagreements and a further deterioration in relations following their military confrontation in May 2025.

The last visit by a Pakistani federal minister to India took place in May 2023, when then Foreign Minister Bilawal Bhutto Zardari attended an SCO meeting in Goa.

India's External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar subsequently travelled to Islamabad in October 2024 for an SCO heads of government meeting.

Despite continuing bilateral tensions, both countries have maintained contact through the regional organisation. An Indian SCO coordinator also visited Islamabad recently as preparations continued for Pakistan's hosting of the organisation's heads of state summit in August 2027.

The New Delhi forum is intended to encourage cultural exchanges rather than address bilateral disputes.

Neither Islamabad nor New Delhi has announced plans for direct talks between Malik and Indian officials during the event.

Nevertheless, Pakistan's decision to send a federal minister demonstrates the continuing role of multilateral gatherings in maintaining official engagement between the neighbouring countries, even when broader diplomatic relations remain strained.
