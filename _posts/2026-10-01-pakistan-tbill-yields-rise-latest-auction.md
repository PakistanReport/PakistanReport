KARACHI: Pakistan raised Rs880 billion in its latest Treasury bill auction as cut-off yields increased across all three maturities, with the six-month rate recording the largest rise at 75 basis points.

The six-month T-bill yield climbed to 12.45 per cent from 11.70 per cent in the previous auction on September 16. The government raised Rs88.3 billion through six-month bills.

The three-month cut-off yield rose by 61 basis points to 11.99 per cent from 11.38 per cent. The government raised Rs143.86 billion through that tenor.

The 12-month yield increased by 45 basis points to 12.49 per cent. The amount raised through one-year bills was Rs29.66 billion.

Total bids received in the auction reached Rs2.726 trillion. Overall borrowing amounted to Rs880 billion, including Rs400.25 billion through competitive bids and Rs479.8 billion through non-competitive bids.

The higher cut-off yields mean the government accepted more expensive short-term borrowing across each of the three maturities compared with the previous auction.

The latest rates also sit above the State Bank of Pakistan’s current 11.5 per cent policy rate. The 12-month T-bill yield, at 12.49 per cent, is 99 basis points above that benchmark.

Market participants are watching the gap between government-security yields and the policy rate as inflation and energy costs remain elevated. However, movements in an individual T-bill auction do not by themselves determine the State Bank’s next monetary-policy decision.

The Monetary Policy Committee considers a broader set of inflation, growth, external-sector and financial conditions when setting the policy rate. Market expectations can change before a scheduled policy review as new economic data become available.

Treasury bills are short-term government securities and are a central part of Pakistan’s domestic borrowing programme. Their auction yields influence returns available to banks and other investors in government debt and can affect pricing across the wider fixed-income market.

The scale of bids — more than three times the Rs880 billion ultimately raised — indicates substantial demand for government paper, but the key development in this auction was the repricing of yields across all three tenors.

For investors and borrowers, the next auctions will show whether the increase persists or was concentrated in this particular round. The current confirmed cut-off rates are 11.99 per cent for three months, 12.45 per cent for six months and 12.49 per cent for 12 months.
