**ISLAMABAD:** Chinese technology group Alibaba is developing a super app to provide access to Pakistan's government services through a single digital platform, the Prime Minister's Office said on Wednesday.

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif was briefed on the application during a meeting to review the performance of the Ministry of Information Technology and Telecommunication.

The application is being developed under the Strategic Transformative Framework agreed between Pakistan and Alibaba. The government has yet to announce a launch date or specify which services will be available through the platform.

Sharif visited Alibaba's headquarters in Hangzhou on May 24, where he met group chairman Joe Tsai and discussed cooperation in artificial intelligence, financial technology and electronic commerce.

Agreements announced during the visit included cooperation between Ignite and Alibaba Cloud on developing AI models supporting Urdu and regional languages, alongside a target of providing digital skills training to 500,000 people.

The Small and Medium Enterprises Development Authority (SMEDA) also entered into an agreement with Alibaba to bring at least 2,000 Pakistani businesses onto its international e-commerce platform.

During Wednesday's meeting, officials informed the prime minister that telecommunications operators had completed network upgrades in 86 cities in preparation for the introduction of 5G services.

Of Pakistan's 32 special technology zones, 21 are operational and house around 300 technology companies. Investment in the zones has reached Rs100 billion, with further proposals worth Rs105 billion under consideration.

The prime minister directed authorities to conduct independent audits of operational technology parks and review the legislation governing companies operating within them.

Pakistan's IT exports stood at $4.6 billion in FY26, while export earnings during the July–September quarter reached $1.24 billion.
