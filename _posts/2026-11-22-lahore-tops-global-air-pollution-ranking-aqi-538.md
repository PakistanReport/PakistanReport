**LAHORE:** Lahore again ranked as the world's most polluted major city on Sunday morning, with its air quality index (AQI) reaching 538 amid persistent smog across Punjab.

According to Swiss monitoring platform IQAir, the reading was recorded between 9am and 10am on November 22, placing the city in the hazardous category, which indicates serious health risks.

Delhi ranked second among major cities with an AQI of 207, followed by Karachi at 180, Mumbai at 177 and Cairo at 162.

Pollution was also severe elsewhere in Punjab. IQAir reported readings of 536 in Bahawalpur, 392 in Sargodha and 347 in Gujranwala during its Sunday morning monitoring.

The worsening conditions come despite enforcement measures targeting smoke-emitting vehicles and polluting brick kilns.

Lahore Traffic Police reported issuing fines to 14,653 smoke-emitting vehicles and taking enforcement action against more than 31,000 vehicles deemed unfit for the road.

Punjab's Environment Protection and Climate Change Department reported conducting more than 84,000 brick-kiln inspections between January 1 and November 18, demolishing 1,429 kilns and sealing 968.

Smog has also reduced visibility across the province, affecting motorway travel and prompting warnings to motorists.

Vehicle emissions, industrial activity, construction dust and crop-residue burning contribute to winter pollution, while cooler temperatures and weak winds can trap pollutants close to the ground.
