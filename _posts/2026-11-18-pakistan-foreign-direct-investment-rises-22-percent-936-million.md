**KARACHI, 18 November 2026 —** Pakistan's net foreign direct investment (FDI) increased 22.4% year-on-year to $935.7 million during July–October of the current financial year, compared with $764.4 million in the corresponding period last year, according to State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) data.

In October alone, net FDI reached $216 million, up 31.7% from $164 million a year earlier, although it declined slightly from September's $219.5 million.

### Canada and China lead investment inflows

Canada was the largest source of net FDI during the four-month period, contributing approximately $318 million, followed by China with $237 million and the United Kingdom with $130 million.

Financial businesses attracted $260.2 million, up from $191.5 million a year earlier. Investment in mining, however, declined to $169.2 million from $266.3 million.

Gross FDI inflows increased to $1.27 billion from $1.07 billion, while investment outflows rose to approximately $330 million from $309 million.

### Recovery remains concentrated

Despite the increase, investment remains concentrated in a limited number of countries and sectors.

Saad Hanif, head of research at Ismail Iqbal Securities, attributed the improvement to stronger fresh inflows rather than reduced investment repatriation.

However, analysts cautioned that a sustained recovery would require broader investment in manufacturing, technology and energy exploration.

The figures indicate improving foreign investor interest, although the relatively narrow investment base leaves overall inflows vulnerable to changes in a few major sectors and source countries.
