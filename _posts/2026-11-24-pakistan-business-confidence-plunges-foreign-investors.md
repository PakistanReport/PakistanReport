**KARACHI:** Business confidence in Pakistan has fallen sharply, with established foreign investors expressing greater pessimism than the wider business community amid rising operating costs, economic uncertainty and geopolitical tensions.

The latest Business Confidence Survey by the Overseas Investors Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OICCI), conducted during September and October 2026, recorded a decline in the overall confidence index from positive 13 per cent to negative 23 per cent.

Among OICCI member companies, which represent established foreign investors operating in Pakistan, business confidence fell more sharply, declining by 55 percentage points from positive 28 per cent to negative 27 per cent.

The nationwide survey covered sectors collectively accounting for nearly 80 per cent of Pakistan's gross domestic product.

Confidence turned negative across all three major sectors, with manufacturing and wholesale and retail trade recording negative 25 per cent, while services fell to negative 17 per cent.

Capital investment expectations declined from positive 2 per cent to negative 14 per cent, while employment expectations dropped from positive 17 per cent to negative 6 per cent.

Rising inflation and high taxation were identified as major threats to business growth by 84 per cent and 81 per cent of respondents, respectively. Energy costs, security concerns and policy uncertainty also contributed to deteriorating sentiment.

Approximately 44 per cent of respondents expect business conditions to worsen over the next six months, while only 23 per cent anticipate improvement, compared with 47 per cent in May.

The deterioration in confidence comes despite net foreign direct investment increasing from approximately $600 million to $720 million during July–September compared with the corresponding period last year.

OICCI President Umar Ahsan Khan called for greater policy consistency, competitive taxation and affordable energy to support investment, business expansion and employment.
