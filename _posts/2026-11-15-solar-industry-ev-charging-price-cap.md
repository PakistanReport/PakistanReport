**ISLAMABAD, 15 November 2026 —** The Pakistan Solar Association has called for nationwide rules governing electric vehicle charging prices, warning that inconsistent charges could discourage motorists from switching to electric transport.

In a communication to federal ministries, the association proposed a uniform pricing framework that would establish a maximum consumer rate for comparable charging services.

The request follows reports of some charging stations, particularly along highways and motorways, charging between Rs100 and Rs150 per kilowatt-hour.

The association contrasted those reported prices with the government's concessional electricity rate for EV charging operators, cited at approximately Rs39.70 per kilowatt-hour.

It questioned whether the benefits of lower electricity costs were reaching drivers, while acknowledging that charging businesses must also recover expenses associated with equipment, installation, maintenance, premises and operations.

Rather than requiring every station to charge an identical amount, the proposed framework would set clearer limits and improve transparency over how consumer prices are calculated.

The association recommended that operators display the complete charging price before a session begins and provide itemised receipts showing electricity supplied, applicable rates, taxes and any additional authorised charges.

It also proposed a dedicated consumer complaints system and regular checks on compliance with pricing requirements.

The suggested consultations would involve the Power Division, relevant federal ministries, the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority, the National Energy Efficiency and Conservation Authority, electricity distribution companies and industry representatives.

The proposals come as Pakistan seeks to encourage electric mobility and expand the infrastructure needed to support it.

Charging costs are an important consideration for drivers weighing the operating expenses of electric vehicles against conventional petrol-powered transport.

The association's recommendations have been forwarded to the relevant ministries. A uniform national consumer price cap has **not** been announced as an approved government policy.

Any eventual framework would need to balance affordability for motorists with the investment required to build and maintain charging facilities.
