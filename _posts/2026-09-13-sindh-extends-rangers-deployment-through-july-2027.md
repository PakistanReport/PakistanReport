The Sindh Assembly has unanimously approved another year of Pakistan Rangers' deployment in the province, authorising the paramilitary force to assist the civil administration until July 19, 2027.

The resolution renews the arrangement for the period from July 20, 2026, to July 19, 2027, under Article 147 of the Constitution. The assembly cited continuing security threats and the law-and-order situation in support of the decision.

Lawmakers credited Rangers operations with helping reduce targeted killings and street crime. The resolution also referred to the threat posed by banned organisations and suspects wanted by law-enforcement agencies.

Rangers have played a prominent security role in Sindh for years, particularly in Karachi, where the force has worked alongside provincial police. The latest extension maintains that arrangement, allowing Rangers to continue supporting civilian authorities in security operations.

Article 147 provides a constitutional framework for one government to entrust functions to another government or its officers with the required consent. The assembly's resolution addresses the continuation of the deployment; it does not replace the province's police or transfer responsibility for everyday policing away from civilian authorities.

The unanimous vote signals broad support among lawmakers for maintaining the force's presence. The provincial government will remain responsible for assessing security needs and coordinating the work of Rangers and Sindh Police.

The renewed authorisation runs until July 19, 2027, unless the arrangement is changed earlier.
