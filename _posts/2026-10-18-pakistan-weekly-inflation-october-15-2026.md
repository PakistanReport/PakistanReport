Pakistan's headline weekly price indicator edged down 0.04% in the week ended October 15, but the majority of items that changed price became more expensive, according to Pakistan Report's examination of the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics' original four-page weekly report.

The combined Sensitive Price Indicator (SPI) stood at **372.43**, compared with **372.58** a week earlier and **334.39** in the corresponding week of 2025. That puts annual SPI inflation at **11.38%**, despite the small weekly decline. The 334.39 figure is the year-earlier comparison, **not** the current index level; the PBS webpage's summary is potentially misleading on this point, so this report uses the detailed official PDF table.

## More items rose than fell

Of the 51 tracked essentials, **23 increased**, **seven decreased** and **21 were unchanged**. In other words, among the 30 items that moved, roughly **77% became more expensive** (23 divided by 30; Pakistan Report calculation). The combined index nevertheless slipped because it reflects weighted price movements, not a simple count of items.

The largest weekly increases included gram pulses (**4.97%**), a 20kg wheat-flour bag (**3.36%**) and live broiler chicken (**3.31%**). The biggest declines included tomatoes (**23.05%**), onions (**4.90%**) and bananas (**2.59%**). These are national average price movements in the PBS survey, not guaranteed prices in any individual market.

## Why the weekly drop may feel different at the market

PBS lists the average price of a 20kg wheat-flour bag at **Rs2,946.72**, compared with **Rs2,851.04** a week earlier: a rise of **Rs95.68** per bag (Pakistan Report subtraction of the official averages). Chicken rose from **Rs383.31** to **Rs395.98** per kilogram, or **Rs12.67**. Meanwhile, tomatoes fell from **Rs155.21** to **Rs119.43** per kilogram, a **Rs35.78** reduction. Households purchasing different combinations of these goods will experience different changes.

The indicator covers 51 essentials in 50 markets across 17 cities. It is a short-term basket, not a full household cost-of-living measure. Its expenditure groups also differed: annual SPI inflation was **8.19%** for the lowest expenditure quintile and **12.09%** for the highest, according to PBS.

**Reporting method:** Pakistan Report read the original PBS executive summary and price-comparison tables, checked the current and comparison SPI levels, counted rising versus falling items using the published totals and calculated the simple rupee differences shown above. No interviews or market visits are claimed.
