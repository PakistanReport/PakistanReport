**LAHORE, 19 November 2026 —** The Pakistan Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission (SUPARCO) has warned that smog conditions could deteriorate across Punjab, with seven districts already classified as experiencing high or severe pollution.

The warning follows a satellite assessment dated 17 November, which identified **690 active fire detections** across Punjab and adjoining transboundary areas during the preceding 24 hours.

Of these, 386 were recorded within Punjab and 304 in neighbouring areas.

### Seven Districts Face High or Severe Smog

SUPARCO identified **Jhang, Narowal, Sheikhupura, Sialkot, Hafizabad, Gujranwala and Dera Ghazi Khan** as the seven districts facing high or severe smog conditions.

Its assessment covered 36 Punjab districts using satellite observations to monitor airborne particles and fire activity.

Forecasts from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service indicated that concentrations of fine particulate matter, known as PM2.5, could range from **51 to 71 micrograms per cubic metre** during the three-day outlook.

SUPARCO warned that poor atmospheric circulation could trap pollutants close to the ground, while modelled pollution levels may be lower than readings from ground monitoring stations.

### Lahore and Faisalabad Record Hazardous Air Quality

Lahore topped the global ranking of the most polluted major cities on Wednesday morning, recording an Air Quality Index (AQI) of **339** between 10am and 11am, according to figures reported from IQAir.

Separate city readings showed even higher pollution levels elsewhere in Punjab, with Faisalabad recording an AQI of **686**, followed by Gujranwala at 550 and Multan at 437.

AQI readings above 300 fall within the hazardous category.

The Pakistan Meteorological Department has also forecast fog and smog in parts of Punjab during morning and nighttime hours, with dry weather expected across most districts.

### Authorities Issue Health and Traffic Warnings

The National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) issued a further alert on Thursday, warning that smog could intensify in Lahore, Kasur, Gujranwala, Hafizabad, Sheikhupura, Sialkot, Narowal and several other districts over the following two days.

The authority cautioned that reduced visibility could disrupt traffic, particularly at night and early in the morning.

Children, older people and those with respiratory conditions were advised to limit unnecessary outdoor exposure, while motorists were urged to follow traffic advisories.

Meanwhile, Punjab's Environment Protection Agency deployed five senior officials to supervise operations against crop-residue burning and coordinate enforcement with district administrations.

In Multan, local authorities also introduced two vehicles equipped with water-spraying systems as part of their anti-smog measures.

With limited atmospheric dispersion and no significant rainfall forecast, authorities are continuing to monitor air quality and fire activity across the province.
