The Federal Board of Revenue has issued final rules for a system that routes specified income-tax audits and assessments through its National Faceless Centre, reducing direct contact between taxpayers and officers handling their cases.

The FBR lists SRO 1665(I)/2026 as the notification setting out the final Faceless Audit and Assessment Rules.

The rules follow the establishment of the National Faceless Centre in Islamabad on September 25.

### How the system works

Under the model described by the FBR, cases can be selected through a computerised, risk-based process and assigned automatically to officers working in different parts of Pakistan.

A taxpayer would not ordinarily know which officer has been assigned the case, while officers would not select their own cases.

The system is designed to separate stages such as audit, assessment and quality control. Communication is intended to take place electronically through the IRIS tax platform rather than through routine meetings with a particular field officer.

According to reporting on the rules, the covered proceedings can include audits under sections 177 and 214C of the Income Tax Ordinance and cases involving unexplained income or assets under section 111.

### Why the change matters

FBR says the approach is intended to improve transparency and reduce opportunities for discretionary dealings.

Automatic case allocation could make it harder for individual officers to choose which taxpayers they examine. Electronic records may also help document communications and decisions.

However, the reform does not mean that every income-tax matter automatically moves to the centre.

The applicable legal framework determines which taxpayers, categories of cases or proceedings are assigned to the National Faceless Centre.

### Implementation will be the test

For affected taxpayers, the practical change is a shift from dealing directly with an assigned field officer to communicating through a centralised digital process.

The success of the system will depend on reliable case allocation, clear notices, timely responses and effective review of assessments.

The final rules establish the framework, but the quality of implementation will determine whether the promised transparency and efficiency are realised.
