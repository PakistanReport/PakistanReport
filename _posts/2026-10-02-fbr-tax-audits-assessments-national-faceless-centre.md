The Federal Board of Revenue has issued final rules for its new faceless audit and assessment system, moving specified tax proceedings to the National Faceless Centre and reducing the role of direct interaction between taxpayers and field officers.

The FBR lists SRO 1665(I)/2026 among its latest notifications as the final rules for Faceless Audit and Assessment. The rules follow the establishment of the National Faceless Centre in Islamabad on September 25 as part of a wider change in the way income tax audits and assessments are handled.

Under the new framework, cases assigned to the centre can include audits under sections 177 and 214C of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001, as well as proceedings involving unexplained income or assets under section 111, according to current reporting on the final rules.

Business Recorder reported that the changes remove the ability of Commissioners Inland Revenue to unilaterally carry out the covered audits and assessments through the previous direct-interaction model where cases have been assigned to the National Faceless Centre under the relevant provisions.

The reform is intended to separate taxpayers from the officers handling their cases and reduce discretionary case allocation.

When announcing the centre on September 25, the FBR said audit cases would be selected through a computerised, risk-based system rather than by an individual officer. Cases would then be allocated automatically to officers who could be based anywhere in Pakistan.

Under that model, the taxpayer would not know the identity of the officer handling the case, while the officer would have no role in choosing which case was assigned.

The FBR said the new structure was designed so that different stages of a case could be handled separately, including audit, assessment and quality control. Proceedings are to be conducted electronically through the IRIS tax system rather than through routine face-to-face contact with a particular field officer.

The final rules therefore turn the National Faceless Centre from an announced institutional reform into an operating framework for specified tax proceedings.

The shift is significant for taxpayers because the traditional relationship between a taxpayer and a particular assessing or audit officer is being replaced, for cases routed through the centre, by computerised selection, automatic assignment and electronic communication.

The FBR has presented faceless administration as part of an effort to improve transparency and reduce opportunities for discretionary interaction in the tax system.

The system does not mean that all tax proceedings automatically move to the centre. Current reporting on the rules states that a case is assigned where the Board has placed the relevant taxpayer, class of taxpayers, income, class of income, case or class of cases under the National Faceless Centre through the applicable legal mechanism.

The latest rules come as the FBR expands the use of technology across tax administration, including electronic scrutiny, digital invoicing and electronic monitoring systems.

Implementation of the faceless system will now be important in determining how effectively the new separation between taxpayers and case officers works in practice, particularly for audits and assessments that previously involved direct dealings with field formations.
