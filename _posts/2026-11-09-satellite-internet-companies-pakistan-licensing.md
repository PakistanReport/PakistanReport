**ISLAMABAD, November 9, 2026 —** Several companies, including Starlink, are pursuing opportunities in Pakistan's satellite internet market, but corporate registration does not by itself authorise them to provide commercial broadband services.

Recent reports citing a Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) submission to a Senate committee identify six registered companies with satellite communications or related business interests.

The Senate's published account of its October 28 meeting, however, states that SECP briefed lawmakers on five registered companies. The difference between the two figures has not been clarified in the publicly available committee summary.

## Companies Seeking Market Entry

Reports citing the SECP submission identify the following companies:

- Aether One (SMC-Private) Limited
- Flynautix (Private) Limited
- Galaxyspace Pakistan (SMC-Private) Limited
- Space P (Private) Limited
- Starlink Internet Services (Private) Limited
- Spacesail Pakistan (Private) Limited

The reported registrations indicate interest in Pakistan's developing satellite communications sector.

## Separate Approvals Required

The Pakistan Space Activities Regulatory Board (PSARB) has established a three-stage process for operators seeking to provide non-geostationary satellite communications services.

Companies must first register with SECP, obtain the relevant satellite-system and associated earth-station authorisations from PSARB, and secure a service licence from the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA).

PSARB's assessment process includes technical evaluations, compatibility with existing satellite networks and security clearance.

The regulator also oversees the registration of satellite systems, gateway stations and user terminals used to deliver connectivity.

## Starlink's Earlier Provisional Registration

PSARB granted provisional registration to Starlink Internet Services Pakistan (Private) Limited on March 21, 2025.

According to the regulator, the temporary approval allowed the company to plan ground infrastructure and complete formalities required by other Pakistani authorities.

It did not constitute final authorisation to begin commercial internet services.

Starlink is among the companies seeking access to Pakistan's satellite broadband market, where low-Earth-orbit systems could provide connectivity in locations that are difficult to serve through conventional telecommunications infrastructure.

## Regulatory Framework Under Development

At its October 28 meeting, the Senate Standing Committee on Cabinet Secretariat was informed that PTA had finalised its satellite services framework, while PSARB was completing its registration and no-objection certificate procedures.

The committee also reviewed the registration status of companies interested in satellite internet services.

PSARB's published guidance states that applicants must undergo regulatory and security assessments before receiving the relevant authorisations.

No confirmed commercial launch timetable for the companies identified in the recent reports was established in the official material reviewed for this article.
