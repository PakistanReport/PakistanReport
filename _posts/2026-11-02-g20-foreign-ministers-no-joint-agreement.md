**ATLANTA:** Foreign ministers from the Group of Twenty (G20) concluded two days of talks in the United States on October 31 without issuing a joint communiqué, reflecting continuing disagreements over major international conflicts.

The meeting in Atlanta ended with a statement from the US chair rather than a document agreed by all participating countries.

The statement identified energy security, supply chains and safe passage through international waterways as important issues but did not announce a collective agreement on resolving them.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio acknowledged after the talks that widely accepted solutions to the disputes under discussion remained distant.

## Ukraine divides participants

Russia's war in Ukraine was a major source of disagreement during the meeting.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov attended despite objections from European diplomats. The tensions were sufficiently visible that the customary group photograph was cancelled.

Rubio defended the decision to include Russia, arguing that diplomatic engagement with Moscow was necessary if negotiations were to produce an eventual settlement.

Disagreements also surrounded the conflict involving Iran, US trade policies and security concerns affecting international shipping.

The absence of a joint communiqué means the ministers did not produce a shared declaration setting out agreed positions on these issues.

## Energy and trade remain on the agenda

Despite the diplomatic divisions, the chair's statement identified several areas for continued discussion.

These included access to affordable energy, more resilient supply chains and investment in critical minerals used in manufacturing and energy technologies.

The statement also addressed the importance of safe navigation through strategic waterways, including the Strait of Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab.

Disruptions in those routes can affect the movement of oil, other energy supplies and commercial goods.

The G20 brings together major developed and emerging economies, making its discussions relevant to countries well beyond those attending the meeting.

The next major gathering is the G20 leaders' summit scheduled for December in Miami.

The Atlanta talks leave participating governments with unresolved differences ahead of that meeting, particularly over security issues that also affect international trade and energy markets.
