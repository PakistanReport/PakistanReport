The Economic Coordination Committee (ECC) approved an immediate Rs2 billion technical supplementary grant for non-sensitive local-election materials on 8 October 2026 after considering an Election Commission of Pakistan (ECP) proposal worth Rs17.873 billion, according to the government's published account of the meeting.

**Pakistan Report calculates that the approved immediate grant is equivalent to 11.19% of the amount proposed.** The remaining Rs15.873 billion — 88.81% of the proposal — is not covered by the immediate grant described in the published decision. That does not mean the balance was rejected, nor does it establish that the ECP has suffered a cash shortfall.

## What the money is intended to cover

The [Finance Division's account of the 8 October ECC meeting](https://pid.gov.pk/site/press_detail/34215) identifies the Rs17.873 billion proposal as funding for the procurement of **non-sensitive materials** for local government elections in Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Islamabad Capital Territory, and for cantonment boards across Pakistan. It does not itemise the goods or disclose the ECP's proposed procurement timetable.

The committee's wording matters. It says the ECC **approved the immediate release** of Rs2 billion through a technical supplementary grant. This is evidence of an approval for release, not documentary proof that Rs2 billion had already reached the ECP's account or been spent. A subsequent Finance Division release order or expenditure record would be needed to establish payment and utilisation.

The ECC also approved a **separate Rs596.18 million** technical supplementary grant to reallocate and revalidate surrendered ECP funds for local elections in Islamabad, local-government by-elections in Sindh and Balochistan, and delimitation work in Punjab. The published account treats that as a different proposal. It should not be added to the Rs2 billion as though it were part of the same procurement approval.

## The questions the decision leaves open

The publicly available meeting account does not say how much of the Rs17.873 billion proposal was needed immediately, whether further funding is planned, or whether the initial grant is sufficient for procurement milestones. It also does not set out any consequence for election dates. Those gaps are important because a funding request, an approval and an actual cash disbursement are separate stages of public financial administration.

The ECP's complete procurement proposal, the ECC's formal minutes and the Finance Division's later release records would allow a more definitive assessment of whether the approval meets the election body's operational needs. Pakistan Report could verify the official public meeting account but **did not obtain those underlying records**.

## How Pakistan Report calculated the share

Dividing Rs2.000 billion by Rs17.873 billion gives **11.1901%**, rounded to **11.19%**. Subtracting Rs2.000 billion from Rs17.873 billion gives **Rs15.873 billion**, or **88.81%** of the proposed amount. The percentages compare a specific ECC-approved immediate grant with the amount requested in the same procurement proposal; they are not percentages of Pakistan's election budget.

**Source and method:** [Government press account of the ECC meeting, 8 October 2026](https://pid.gov.pk/site/press_detail/34215); [Finance Division press-release archive, 8 October 2026, PR No. 653](https://www.finance.gov.pk/press_releases.html). Arithmetic is Pakistan Report's own. Formal minutes, release orders and the ECP's underlying request were not available for independent examination.
