**ISLAMABAD, November 14, 2026 —** Pakistan's weekly inflation declined by 0.46% during the week ending November 12, largely because of lower petroleum prices, although the annual rate remained elevated at 11.21%, according to the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS).

The Sensitive Price Indicator (SPI), which measures changes in the prices of essential commodities, fell to 372.90 points from 374.61 points the previous week.

Petrol prices declined by 7.4% during the week, while diesel became 6.1% cheaper, helping offset increases in several food items.

However, tomatoes recorded a 12.1% weekly increase, followed by eggs at 6%, potatoes at 2.4% and wheat flour at 0.9%.

Of the 51 essential items monitored by PBS, prices of 19 increased, nine declined and 23 remained unchanged.

Despite the weekly reduction, several commodities continued to show substantial increases compared with the corresponding period last year.

LPG prices were 111.4% higher year-on-year, while tomatoes rose 78.9%, wheat flour 36.1%, diesel 30.8% and petrol 26.2%.

The contrasting figures highlight the difference between short-term price movements and the broader inflation experienced by households over the past year.

The SPI tracks prices across 50 markets in 17 cities and provides a weekly assessment of changes in the cost of essential goods and services.

Although cheaper fuel contributed to the latest decline, increases in vegetables, flour and other necessities continued to place pressure on household budgets.

The latest data indicate that relief in petroleum prices has moderated weekly inflation, but the annual cost of several essential commodities remains considerably higher.
