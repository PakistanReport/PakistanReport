**KARACHI:** Saudi and Pakistani business leaders have signed an agreement to establish a joint private equity fund that would direct three-quarters of its investments towards Pakistan, with agriculture, mining, construction and infrastructure among the main areas under consideration.

The Saudi-Pakistan Joint Business Council signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) on Monday, 9 November, at a ceremony in Karachi.

According to the council, the proposed fund will initially be worth several hundred million, with plans to expand as suitable investment opportunities emerge. The announcement did not specify an exact amount or confirm how much capital has already been committed.

Private investors from Saudi Arabia and Pakistan are expected to contribute equally, although the investment allocation will favour Pakistan.

Under the proposed arrangement, 75% of the fund will be invested in Pakistani projects, while the remaining 25% will be directed towards opportunities in Saudi Arabia.

## Agriculture, Mining and Infrastructure Among Targets

The fund intends to support growing private businesses in agriculture, mining, construction and other industries.

It will also examine opportunities linked to the Pakistani government's plans to make commercial use of underutilised public land and assets.

Potential projects include airports, motorways, ports and maritime infrastructure.

In Saudi Arabia, the fund will consider commercial investments across the kingdom, including opportunities in Makkah and Madina.

The arrangement could allow investors to participate in businesses and infrastructure projects rather than relying solely on traditional lending.

Unlike a bank loan, private equity investment generally involves taking an ownership stake in a business or project in exchange for providing capital.

## Leading Business Figures Attend Signing

Prince Mansour bin Mohammad Al Saud, chairman of the Saudi-Pakistan Joint Business Council, attended the signing alongside representatives of major businesses from both countries.

He described Pakistan as an attractive market for Saudi investors and said the initiative was intended to turn longstanding economic discussions into commercial activity.

Pakistani businessman Arif Habib also welcomed the agreement, describing it as a sign of investor confidence in the country's economic opportunities.

Among the Pakistani business leaders associated with the ceremony were Bank of Punjab President Zafar Masud, HBL President Nassir Saleem, Faysal Bank CEO Yousaf Hussain and Ismail Industries Director Maqsood Ismail.

Fatima Group Chairman Fawad Mukhtar, who serves as co-chairman of the council, participated virtually.

The agreement also allows institutions from both countries to be invited to acquire ownership stakes in the company that will manage the fund.

## Saudi Delegation Also Explores Karachi Port Opportunities

Following the signing ceremony, Prince Mansour and a Saudi business delegation visited Karachi Port Trust and South Asia Pakistan Terminals Limited to examine opportunities in the maritime sector.

During the visit, Karachi Port officials presented potential projects involving port infrastructure, transport connections and a proposed Maritime Business District.

The delegation expressed interest in several of these opportunities, according to reporting on the visit.

The fund and the port discussions reflect continuing efforts by Pakistani and Saudi business groups to expand their relationship beyond conventional trade into direct investment.

For now, the next significant step will be securing regulatory approvals and identifying the first projects eligible for funding.
