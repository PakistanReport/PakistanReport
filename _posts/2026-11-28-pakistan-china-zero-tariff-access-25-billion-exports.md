**BEIJING:** Pakistan has sought preferential zero-tariff access for its goods to the Chinese market, aiming to increase annual exports to China from around $3 billion to between $20 billion and $25 billion.

Federal Minister for Planning and Development Ahsan Iqbal raised the proposal during discussions with Liu Haixing, head of the International Department of the Communist Party of China.

The proposal was previously raised by Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif with Chinese President Xi Jinping.

Iqbal also discussed the proposed arrangement with Zhou Haibing, vice chairman of China's National Development and Reform Commission, seeking duty-free access for Pakistani goods without requiring equivalent tariff concessions from Pakistan.

The proposed arrangement would be separate from the existing China-Pakistan Free Trade Agreement.

Under the second phase of the agreement, signed in April 2019, China agreed to immediately eliminate duties on 313 priority tariff lines covering products of export interest to Pakistan.

Pakistan is seeking an arrangement similar to China's preferential trade policy for African countries.

China extended zero-tariff treatment to all 53 African countries with which it maintains diplomatic relations on May 1, 2026. The expanded concessions for 20 African countries are initially applicable until April 2028.

Pakistan recorded a bilateral trade deficit of $19.6 billion with China in FY26, according to a November report by the Centre of Excellence for CPEC at the Pakistan Institute of Development Economics. China accounted for 32 per cent of Pakistan's total imports.

The proposed upper export target represents more than eight times Pakistan's current annual exports to China, a country that imports nearly $3 trillion worth of goods annually.
