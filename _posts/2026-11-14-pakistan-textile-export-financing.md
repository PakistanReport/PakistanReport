**KARACHI, November 14, 2026 —** The Pakistan Hosiery Manufacturers and Exporters Association (PHMA) has welcomed the government's decision to reserve Rs300 billion for small and medium-sized exporters under the Export Finance Scheme administered by the Export-Import Bank of Pakistan (Pak EXIM).

PHMA Chairman Syed Zia Alamdar Hussain said the allocation could improve access to affordable working capital for smaller businesses seeking to increase production, fulfil export orders and compete in international markets.

He urged participating banks to process financing applications promptly and transparently, clearly explain eligibility requirements and help qualifying exporters access the facility without unnecessary delays.

The dedicated allocation forms part of the government's wider export-financing framework for the 2026–27 financial year.

Earlier, the government increased the overall Export Finance Scheme envelope from Rs1 trillion to Rs1.5 trillion to expand working-capital support for exporters.

The Rs300 billion allocation was initially announced for SME exporters, agricultural SMEs and new borrowers. A subsequent government announcement reserved the entire amount for eligible SME exporters.

A separate Rs350 billion Long-Term Export Growth Financing Facility has also been introduced to support investment in machinery, modernisation and expanded export capacity.

Hussain said smaller enterprises played an important role in employment, industrial diversification and foreign exchange earnings, but often faced difficulties securing financing on affordable terms.

He also called for measures to reduce business costs, improve access to credit and ensure competitive energy tariffs for export-oriented industries.

Pak EXIM has introduced a digital portal to connect participating financial institutions and improve the processing of export-financing arrangements.

Although the allocation provides a dedicated funding pool, the amount actually disbursed to SME exporters has not been established in the available reports.

The effectiveness of the initiative will therefore depend on how efficiently banks make financing available to eligible businesses.
