China's economy is expected to have expanded modestly faster in the third quarter of 2026, but weak household spending and a prolonged property downturn are testing Beijing's ability to sustain growth. Official gross domestic product figures are scheduled for Monday, October 19. The figures have not yet been released.

A Reuters survey of 50 economists forecasts year-on-year growth of 4.4% for July through September, compared with 4.3% in the second quarter. The estimate remains below the lower end of China's official full-year growth target of 4.5% to 5%. The same survey anticipates quarter-on-quarter expansion of about 1.1%.

## Exports and domestic demand pull in different directions

China's exporters and factories have shown resilience, including in technology-related manufacturing, while domestic consumption, private investment and the housing market remain under pressure. This uneven performance has complicated efforts to shift the economy toward more sustainable, consumer-led growth.

Beijing introduced further targeted support in late September, including measures aimed at borrowing costs and the housing market. Authorities have also sought to support infrastructure spending, but economists remain divided over whether incremental steps can overcome persistent caution among households and private businesses.

The currency market offers another signal of the competing pressures. On October 16, the yuan was on course for a third consecutive weekly gain, supported by export performance. A stronger currency can reflect investor confidence but may also complicate exporters' pricing in overseas markets.

## Why Monday's release matters

Investors will examine the headline GDP number alongside indicators such as retail sales, industrial output and investment to judge whether policy measures are gaining traction. A reading near the Reuters consensus would suggest a modest improvement, not a broad-based recovery.

For Pakistan and other economies trading with China, changes in Chinese demand matter for commodity markets, regional supply chains and broader Asian economic sentiment. The official data will provide a clearer basis for assessing the outlook than forecasts alone.
