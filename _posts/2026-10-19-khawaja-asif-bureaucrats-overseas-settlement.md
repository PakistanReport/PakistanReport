Defence Minister Khawaja Asif has raised concerns about senior government officials who have established permanent residences abroad or made arrangements to settle overseas while serving in Pakistan.

In a statement posted on social media on Sunday, Asif questioned whether officials planning to leave the country could remain fully committed to their public responsibilities.

He said Pakistan's civil service included capable and honest officers but alleged that a number of bureaucrats had made arrangements to settle in Canada, the United States, Britain and other European countries.

The minister also identified Portugal as a destination that had attracted some officials in recent years.

His remarks have brought renewed attention to questions about the overseas financial interests and residency arrangements of senior public servants.

## Concerns over public-service commitments

Asif claimed that some officials had begun planning their relocation while still holding government positions.

He questioned whether individuals who had occupied influential posts and received the benefits of public service viewed their careers in Pakistan as a long-term responsibility or as a step towards settling elsewhere.

The minister also alleged that a person involved in arranging permanent residency for several wealthy bureaucrats had disappeared after collecting substantial sums of money.

However, Asif did not identify the officials or the alleged intermediary, and he did not provide documentary evidence to support the claims.

The allegations have not been independently established.

Asif has raised similar concerns about bureaucrats' overseas property and residency arrangements in the past.

## Questions of accountability

The minister's statement touches on a broader issue of accountability in public service, particularly for officials responsible for major government decisions and public resources.

Overseas residency or property ownership does not, by itself, establish wrongdoing. Any questions about financial misconduct, conflicts of interest or breaches of service rules would require examination of the relevant facts and applicable regulations.

Asif did not announce a formal investigation or specify whether the government intended to review the residency status of serving officials.

His comments nevertheless raise questions about the standards expected of senior civil servants and how potential conflicts between private interests and official duties should be addressed.

For now, the claims remain allegations made by the defence minister, with no named officials or documented findings made public.
