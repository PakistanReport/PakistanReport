**ISLAMABAD:** The federal government has released an initial Rs10 billion under Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif's Rs160 billion development package for Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, aimed at strengthening public services, infrastructure, security and economic development across the province.

In a statement issued on Monday, the Finance Division clarified that the package constitutes additional funding beyond the Rs151.1 billion allocated for current and development expenditure in eight newly merged districts during the 2026–27 financial year.

The ministry said funds were being disbursed through existing fiscal transfer arrangements.

As many as 430 development projects have been proposed for the 12 designated districts administered by the newly established Area Development Secretariat (ADS), according to official documents reported by The News International.

The proposed projects include:

- Rs39.19 billion for 62 law and order projects.
- Rs12.9 billion for 72 education projects.
- Rs12.7 billion for 32 road improvement projects.
- Rs3.2 billion for 41 healthcare projects.

Education initiatives include upgrading 70 schools, installing solar power systems at 409 schools and establishing three Danish schools.

Other proposals include improvements to the Thall–Parachinar Road, development projects associated with the Gomal Zam Dam, solar-powered water supply schemes at 88 locations and scholarships for 100 students to pursue higher education in China.

The ADS was established following the imposition of governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on October 11, with administrative responsibility for 12 districts, including the former tribal districts, Bannu, Tank, Lakki Marwat and Dera Ismail Khan.

Governor's adviser Samar Haroon Bilour welcomed the initial release of funds on Sunday.
