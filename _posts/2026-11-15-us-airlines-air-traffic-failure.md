**WASHINGTON, 15 November 2026 —** Airlines across the United States are working to restore normal operations after a nationwide air traffic control communications failure forced authorities to halt most departing flights for nearly five hours on Friday.

Although the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) lifted the ground stop on Friday evening, the disruption continued throughout the weekend as carriers attempted to reposition aircraft, arrange crews and accommodate stranded passengers.

Flight-tracking service FlightAware recorded more than 9,200 cancellations and 7,500 delays on Friday, followed by over 6,400 cancellations and 8,000 delays on Saturday. Hundreds of Sunday flights had also been cancelled in advance.

The emergency began when communications and radar systems used by air traffic controllers became unstable. US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the problem followed the installation of a router by FAA contractor L3Harris, which caused excessive traffic across the network.

Officials said the disruption affected 17 of the country's 22 en-route air traffic control centres. The nationwide suspension of departures was ordered as a precaution, even though no control centre completely lost communications.

Authorities have reported no evidence that the failure resulted from a cyberattack. Investigators are examining whether installation errors or faulty equipment caused the overload.

Major airlines, including American, Delta and United, cancelled substantial portions of their weekend schedules, while passengers faced extended waits, missed connections and uncertainty over replacement flights.

FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said the air traffic system remained stable and expected airline schedules to recover largely by late Sunday.

The incident has renewed scrutiny of ageing US aviation infrastructure. Duffy has called for $50 billion to modernise air traffic control systems, while lawmakers have sought further investigation and oversight.

The disruption also comes shortly before the Thanksgiving travel period, when nearly 29 million passengers are expected to fly on US airlines between 20 and 30 November.

Airlines have offered affected travellers rebooking arrangements and other assistance, although the conditions vary by carrier.
