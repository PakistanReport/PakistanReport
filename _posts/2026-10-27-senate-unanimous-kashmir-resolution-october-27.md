**ISLAMABAD, October 27, 2026:** The Senate unanimously adopted a resolution on Monday reaffirming Pakistan's position on Kashmir ahead of the country's annual October 27 observance.

The resolution was moved by Federal Minister for Kashmir Affairs Amir Muqam during proceedings in the upper house.

It reiterated support for Kashmiris' right to self-determination and called for implementation of relevant United Nations Security Council resolutions.

The measure also urged Pakistan to continue raising the issue through diplomatic channels.

October 27 is observed in Pakistan as Kashmir Black Day, marking the arrival of Indian troops in Jammu and Kashmir in 1947. India and Pakistan maintain sharply different positions on the territory's history and political status.

The Senate's action provides a formal parliamentary expression of Pakistan's position at a time when Kashmir remains a longstanding dispute between the two neighbouring countries.

However, adopting a resolution does not itself change the territory's legal status, establish a new international agreement or require another government to take action.

Its immediate significance is political and diplomatic.

Any practical follow-up would depend on subsequent statements, diplomatic initiatives or decisions by the federal government.

The resolution was among several matters considered during Monday's Senate sitting.
