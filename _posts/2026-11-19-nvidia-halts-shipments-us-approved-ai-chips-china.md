**SHENZHEN, 19 November 2026 —** Nvidia has stopped shipping advanced artificial intelligence chips to China despite receiving US export approvals, raising fresh uncertainty over technology trade between the two countries.

Interim chief executive Colette Kress said the company was no longer shipping licensed Hopper-generation data centre processors to Chinese customers and had excluded further sales from its current financial outlook.

Nvidia reported **$2.4 billion in sales** of licensed Hopper products to China during the quarter ending October 25.

### Export Restrictions and Chinese Approval

The Hopper range includes the H200 processor, used to train and operate advanced AI systems.

Washington eased restrictions on H200 exports in January, allowing sales to approved Chinese companies under specific licensing and security conditions. The US had authorised shipments to 10 companies, including Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance.

However, Beijing has restricted imports as it seeks to reduce dependence on American semiconductor technology.

Reuters previously reported that approximately **600,000 H200 chips** were being held in Hong Kong awaiting Chinese approval for large-scale imports.

Kress did not explain the shipment halt or indicate whether it would be permanent.

The development highlights the continuing uncertainty facing American chipmakers and Chinese technology companies as both governments pursue competing semiconductor policies.
