Pakistan and the United States discussed progress towards a bilateral trade framework, possible increases in US energy imports and investment financing during a meeting in Bangkok on October 14, according to Pakistan's Press Information Department (PID).

Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb met a US State Department representative identified in the government's statement as Ambassador Yon on the sidelines of the World Bank Group–International Monetary Fund annual meetings. The statement said the minister reviewed trade framework discussions and opportunities to diversify Pakistan's energy supplies through imports from the United States.

The two sides also discussed anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing arrangements, follow-up work connected to the Financial Action Task Force, and potential financing through the US International Development Finance Corporation and the Export–Import Bank of the United States.

Other subjects included the Reko Diq mining project, critical minerals, project security, private investment, possible Boeing aircraft acquisition and financing, aviation clearances, and digital asset regulation. The government statement also referred to potential applications involving remittances and tokenised Eurobonds.

These subjects were under discussion; the PID statement did not announce a completed trade agreement, a signed energy purchase contract, approved aircraft financing or committed investment. Any such outcomes would require separate confirmation.

This meeting differs from Pakistan Report's October 13 coverage of Aurangzeb's talks with the International Finance Corporation and its October 14 article on Pakistan's international bond plans. Here the focus is the separate bilateral discussion with the US State Department.
