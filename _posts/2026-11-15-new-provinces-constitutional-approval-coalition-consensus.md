**LAHORE, 15 November 2026 —** Discussions over creating additional provinces in Pakistan have brought constitutional requirements and differences among coalition partners into focus, as senior government figures consider how any proposal could move forward.

President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif discussed the issue during a meeting in Lahore on Saturday, according to reports citing officials familiar with the talks.

The two leaders reportedly agreed that any move to establish new provinces or administrative units must follow constitutional procedures, including the necessary parliamentary approvals.

The discussion followed Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi's renewed suggestion that a referendum could be used to seek public opinion on restructuring Pakistan's provincial system.

However, Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry has stressed that a referendum cannot replace the constitutional role of Parliament and the provincial assemblies.

He said the Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz favoured building national consensus through discussions involving political parties and legislative institutions.

Chaudhry also invited Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf to participate in the debate, urging the opposition to set aside political differences and engage with proposals for administrative reform.

The Pakistan Peoples Party, meanwhile, has maintained its opposition to any division of Sindh. Zardari reportedly reiterated that position during his meeting with the prime minister.

Other coalition figures have argued that smaller provinces could improve administration and the delivery of public services. Federal Health Minister Mustafa Kamal has supported the creation of additional administrative units, citing concerns about the concentration of resources and authority.

These differing positions highlight the distinction between discussing governance reforms and securing agreement on changes to provincial boundaries.

Under Article 239 of the Constitution, an amendment altering a province's limits requires approval by a two-thirds majority of that province's assembly, alongside the constitutional amendment process in Parliament.

Although the debate has intensified, **no agreed constitutional amendment creating new provinces has been announced**.

Any formal proposal would therefore face both legal requirements and the challenge of securing support across political parties and affected provinces.
