**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan's Supreme Court and Türkiye's Court of Cassation have signed a memorandum of understanding to expand judicial cooperation, professional training and the exchange of legal expertise between the two countries.

Chief Justice of Pakistan Yahya Afridi and President of Türkiye's Court of Cassation Ömer Kerkez signed the agreement at the Supreme Court in Islamabad on Monday.

The agreement establishes a framework for collaboration between the judicial institutions of both countries, with particular emphasis on professional development, legal research and improvements in judicial administration.

Pakistan's Federal Judicial Academy and the Justice Academy of Türkiye will pursue closer institutional engagement through joint educational programmes, faculty exchanges and specialised training.

The cooperation framework covers several areas of law, including cybercrime, financial offences, constitutional law, human rights, judicial ethics and climate-related litigation.

It also envisages collaboration in alternative dispute resolution, gender-responsive justice and the adoption of modern technologies in court proceedings.

Speaking at the signing ceremony, Justice Afridi highlighted Pakistan's ongoing judicial reforms and the importance of international experience in improving access to justice, accountability and consistency in judicial processes.

Kerkez identified digitalisation, artificial intelligence and increasingly complex cross-border legal disputes as areas requiring greater cooperation between judicial institutions.

A joint working group will be established to determine priorities, coordinate activities and oversee the implementation of the agreement.

The framework also provides for exchanges of judicial decisions, legal research, conferences and seminars.

The Turkish delegation is visiting Pakistan from November 23 to 27, with further engagements involving judicial institutions and legal professionals scheduled during its stay.
