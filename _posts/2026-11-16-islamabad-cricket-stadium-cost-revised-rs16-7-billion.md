**ISLAMABAD, 16 November 2026 —** The Capital Development Authority (CDA) has revised the estimated cost of Islamabad's proposed cricket stadium to Rs16.7 billion from Rs11.9 billion, with plans to increase its seating capacity from 25,000 to 35,000 spectators.

The revised project documents cover construction, finishing work, electrical installations, lighting, air-conditioning, ground preparation and security systems. The CDA Development Working Party will consider the modified PC-I documents for approval.

The stadium is planned near Sector D-12 at the foothills of the Margalla Hills. Officials say it will be modelled on Dubai Cricket Stadium and is intended to become Pakistan's largest cricket venue by seating capacity.

### Construction and project costs

The CDA plans to complete the stadium in two phases. The first will cover the main structure and associated road infrastructure, while the second will include finishing work and remaining facilities.

A joint venture involving Habib, ZKB and EA submitted a bid of Rs8.9 billion for the initial construction package. Following negotiations, the reported contract amount was reduced to Rs8.5 billion.

The authority expects the first phase to take approximately six months and aims to complete the entire project within a year, subject to approvals and construction progress.

Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi has indicated that groundbreaking is expected shortly.

### International cricket and planning concerns

A Pakistan Cricket Board official has suggested that Islamabad could host matches during the 2028 ICC Women's T20 World Cup if the stadium is completed in time. No matches have yet been confirmed for the venue.

The project's location in Islamabad's Zone III has also raised questions because construction in the area is restricted. The CDA maintains that sports and recreational facilities are permitted under the applicable planning rules.

The revised project estimate remains subject to approval, with the CDA expected to proceed with the remaining procurement and construction arrangements afterwards.
