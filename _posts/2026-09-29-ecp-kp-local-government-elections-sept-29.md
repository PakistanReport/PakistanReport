**ISLAMABAD —** The Election Commission of Pakistan has fixed the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa local government elections case for September 29 and summoned the provincial chief secretary and local government secretary to appear in person as the commission examines delays in moving the election process forward.

The hearing follows an earlier extension in which the ECP gave the provincial government three more weeks to complete legislation required for the local government polls.

The case concerns the legal and administrative steps needed before elections can proceed, including the effect of proposed amendments on delimitation.

### ECP seeks progress from provincial government

At an earlier hearing, provincial officials told the commission that a cabinet committee was considering changes to the relevant local government law.

The ECP was also informed that delimitation work had been affected in seven districts and that the provincial government wanted local government elections to be held across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa at the same time.

Commission members expressed concern about the pace of the process and stressed that local government elections must ultimately be held.

The September 29 proceeding is therefore a hearing on progress and compliance, not an announcement that polling itself will take place on September 29.

That distinction matters because a final election schedule depends on completion of the legal and electoral preparations required by the commission.

### Delimitation could be affected by legal changes

Changes to the local government framework can have direct consequences for constituency boundaries and other election preparations.

During the earlier proceedings, the commission was told that amendments to the law could require delimitation work to be undertaken again. That creates a sequencing issue: the legal framework needs sufficient clarity before some of the technical election work can be finalised.

The summons to senior provincial officials raises the level of accountability in the case by requiring the chief secretary and local government secretary to explain the government's progress directly to the commission.

The next point to watch is what the KP government reports at the hearing and whether the ECP sets further deadlines or directions for legislation, delimitation and the eventual election schedule.

Until the commission issues those directions, September 29 should be treated as the hearing date rather than the date of the local government elections.
