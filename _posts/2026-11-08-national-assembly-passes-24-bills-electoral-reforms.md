**ISLAMABAD, November 8, 2026 —** Pakistan's National Assembly passed 24 bills during its October session and unanimously adopted a motion to establish a Parliamentary Committee on Electoral Reforms, according to figures released by the National Assembly Secretariat.

The 30th session, held from October 13 to 30, saw the passage of 17 government bills and seven private members' bills.

The Secretariat disclosed the legislative figures in a statement issued on November 5, highlighting consultations between parliamentary parties over proposed legislation.

## Government and Private Members' Bills

Government bills are legislative proposals introduced by federal ministers on behalf of the government, while private members' bills are introduced by parliamentarians acting outside a ministerial role.

Of the 17 government bills passed by the National Assembly, seven were forwarded to the Senate and 10 were sent to the president for assent.

Among the seven private members' bills, five were transmitted to the Senate and two were forwarded to the president.

The Secretariat's summary did not identify the 24 bills individually. It therefore remains unclear from that announcement which specific legislative subjects accounted for the government's 17 bills and the seven private members' proposals.

The passage of a bill by the National Assembly does not necessarily mean it has become law. Further parliamentary consideration or presidential assent may be required, depending on the legislation.

Lawmakers also introduced 16 additional bills during the session, comprising nine government bills and seven private members' bills.

## Electoral Reforms Committee

The Assembly unanimously adopted a motion concerning the formation of a Parliamentary Committee on Electoral Reforms.

The proposed committee provides a parliamentary mechanism for examining changes to Pakistan's electoral framework.

The motion's approval is therefore a procedural development rather than the adoption of specific electoral reforms.

## Consultations Between Parliamentary Parties

According to the Secretariat, National Assembly Speaker Sardar Ayaz Sadiq convened meetings of a select committee to examine concerns raised by government coalition parties and the opposition over proposed bills.

Representatives of parliamentary parties, relevant ministries and the Assembly Secretariat participated in the discussions.

The Secretariat said consultations helped resolve disagreements on several legislative proposals before they were brought forward for approval.
