**November 7, 2026 —** Seven OPEC+ oil-producing countries have agreed to maintain their September production targets through December as the group assesses global supply conditions and prepares to discuss its production framework for 2027.

Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman reached the decision during a virtual meeting on November 1, according to the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC).

## Production Policy Remains Unchanged

The seven countries reviewed the oil-market outlook before deciding against further adjustments to their December targets.

They also reaffirmed their commitment to the Declaration of Cooperation, which provides the framework for coordinating production policies among participating OPEC and non-OPEC countries.

The decision concerns required production levels rather than actual output, which can differ because of operational constraints and disruptions.

## November Meeting to Address Future Policy

The seven producers are scheduled to meet again on November 29 to review market conditions.

The wider OPEC+ alliance is also expected to consider its production arrangements for 2027, including the allocation of output targets among participating countries.

The discussions are taking place amid uncertainty over energy supplies, regional conflicts and the recovery of disrupted oil shipments.

## Implications for Pakistan

As a major petroleum importer, Pakistan is exposed to changes in international crude prices and the cost of refined fuel.

OPEC+ production policy is one factor influencing those prices, alongside global demand, shipping conditions and geopolitical developments.

Changes in international oil prices can affect Pakistan's import bill, although domestic petrol and diesel prices also depend on the rupee's exchange rate, taxes and petroleum levies.
