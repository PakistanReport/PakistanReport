Russian President Vladimir Putin has said proposals for a settlement in the war with Ukraine remain on the table, while warning that Moscow will decide its next steps according to what it considers Russia's interests and its response to recent Ukrainian attacks.

Speaking to journalists in St Petersburg, Putin said Moscow had been prepared to consider resuming negotiations with Kyiv after Russia's parliamentary elections. He then accused Ukraine of attacking Moscow and polling stations and said those actions would affect Russia's response.

The remarks combined a stated willingness to keep diplomatic options open with a warning of continued military retaliation. They did not amount to an announcement that negotiations had resumed or that Russia had accepted a particular ceasefire or settlement proposal.

## Diplomatic opening remains conditional

Putin said settlement proposals remained possible but stressed that Moscow would have to consider what course best served its interests. His comments came as diplomatic efforts around the war continued, including attempts by the United States to revive negotiations.

Kyiv has also continued to call for measures aimed at reducing attacks and creating conditions for further talks. The positions of the two sides, however, remain far apart on central questions surrounding territory, security guarantees and the terms under which hostilities could end.

Against that background, Putin's language is best understood as keeping negotiating options formally open rather than signalling that a breakthrough has been reached.

The Russian president simultaneously accused Ukraine of provocative attacks and warned of a sustained response. Ukraine and Russia have continued exchanging large-scale drone and missile attacks while diplomatic contacts have proceeded on separate tracks.

## Fighting continues alongside diplomacy

The contrast between diplomatic messaging and battlefield activity has characterised repeated efforts to move toward negotiations. Statements supporting talks have frequently been accompanied by continued strikes, competing demands and disagreements over the sequence of a ceasefire and a broader settlement.

Russia says its military actions are responses to Ukrainian attacks and maintains that any agreement must address Moscow's stated security and territorial demands. Ukraine says a settlement must protect its sovereignty and security and has sought international support for measures intended to deter further Russian attacks.

Neither side's public position establishes that a negotiated end to the war is close. Putin's latest comments preserve the possibility of talks, but his warning about retaliation underscores that the military confrontation remains active.

The immediate significance of the statement is therefore limited but clear: Moscow has not publicly closed the door on settlement proposals, even as it says recent Ukrainian actions will influence how it approaches both military responses and any future negotiations.
