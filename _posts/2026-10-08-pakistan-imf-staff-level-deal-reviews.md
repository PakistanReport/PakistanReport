Pakistan and the International Monetary Fund have reached a staff-level agreement on two reviews of the country's loan programmes, a step that could make approximately $1.21 billion in financing available once the lender's Executive Board gives its approval.

The agreement covers the fourth review of Pakistan's $7 billion Extended Fund Facility (EFF) and the third review of its Resilience and Sustainability Facility (RSF), the IMF said following discussions in Karachi and Islamabad from September 23 to October 7.

Subject to board approval, Pakistan would gain access to about $1 billion, equivalent to SDR 760 million, under the EFF and approximately $210 million, or SDR 154 million, under the RSF. The agreement does not itself constitute a disbursement, and the funds should not be treated as having been received.

The IMF team, led by Iva Petrova, also completed discussions for the 2026 Article IV consultation, the fund's regular assessment of a member country's economy and policies. The review process examines both economic performance and implementation of commitments attached to the financing arrangements.

The lender said Pakistan had maintained a degree of macroeconomic stability despite the effects of conflict in the Middle East, including higher energy costs and disruption to supplies. It estimated economic growth for the 2025-26 financial year at 3.6%, after growth of 4% in the first three quarters, and reported headline inflation of about 10.3% in September following a peak in May.

According to the IMF's assessment, Pakistan's current account was broadly balanced during the financial year, supported by remittances, while gross foreign exchange reserves had risen to approximately $21.5 billion by the end of September. Those assessments describe the position reported by the fund; they do not remove the risks facing the economy.

The IMF specifically identified geopolitical tensions, volatile energy prices, tighter international financial conditions and interruptions to trade as continuing vulnerabilities. Such pressures are especially relevant to Pakistan because imported fuel and external financing remain important to its balance of payments.

The programme's next phase continues to place emphasis on fiscal discipline and revenue collection. The IMF said implementation of the current budget, including an underlying primary surplus target of 2% of gross domestic product, would be important to reducing public debt pressures. It also pointed to risk-based tax audits, digital invoicing and the use of third-party data as elements of revenue administration reform.

On spending, the lender noted plans to raise combined health and education expenditure from 2.5% of GDP in the previous financial year to 2.8% in the current one. It also called for stronger targeted cash transfers and a more focused approach to fuel assistance, rather than broad subsidies that are costly to sustain.

Energy-sector reforms remain another central condition. The IMF called for measures to contain the accumulation of circular debt, improve distribution efficiency, reduce gas losses and maintain cost recovery while protecting vulnerable consumers. It also highlighted exchange-rate flexibility and a monetary policy stance consistent with bringing inflation back towards the State Bank of Pakistan's target range.

The RSF component addresses longer-term resilience to climate risks, including disaster financing, public investment planning, irrigation pricing and energy efficiency. Unlike the EFF, which is primarily aimed at macroeconomic adjustment, this arrangement supports reforms intended to reduce exposure to climate-related economic shocks.

The staff-level agreement therefore represents progress in Pakistan's ongoing programme rather than its completion. The immediate financing decision rests with the IMF Executive Board, while the policy commitments attached to the arrangements will continue to be assessed in subsequent reviews.
