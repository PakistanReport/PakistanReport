Pakistan is set to receive the maximum country weighting in a new JPMorgan index designed to track local-currency government debt in frontier markets, giving Pakistani bonds a prominent place in a benchmark aimed at an increasingly important segment of emerging-market investing.

JPMorgan plans to launch the GBI-EM Edge index by the end of September, according to information reported by Reuters from a note the bank sent to investors. The benchmark is expected to cover nearly $330 billion of eligible local-currency debt across 26 countries.

Pakistan is among the larger components of the index and is expected to reach the benchmark's 8% country cap. The cap prevents any single market from dominating the index and means Pakistan will carry the maximum weight permitted under the methodology.

The index will include eligible government bonds with at least $250 million equivalent outstanding and a minimum remaining maturity of 2.5 years. Other markets expected to feature include Egypt, Vietnam, Morocco, Kazakhstan, Bangladesh, Nigeria and Sri Lanka.

### Why it matters

Global bond indices can influence how international investors assess and allocate money across markets. Inclusion does not guarantee capital inflows, and the new benchmark is focused on frontier markets rather than JPMorgan's larger mainstream emerging-market bond universe. But a maximum weighting gives Pakistan significant representation among the countries the index is designed to measure.

The new benchmark also reflects growing investor interest in local-currency debt issued by smaller developing economies. Reuters reported that the index's nominal yield is around 10.4%, roughly 440 basis points above JPMorgan's mainstream emerging-market local-currency benchmark.

Local-currency borrowing can reduce some of the foreign-exchange mismatch associated with governments borrowing in dollars while collecting most revenue in domestic currency. For investors, however, local-currency bonds add exchange-rate risk alongside interest-rate and sovereign risk.

JPMorgan's back-testing indicated that the frontier index would have produced annualised returns about 1.2 percentage points higher than the mainstream benchmark over nine years, although historical simulations do not guarantee future performance.

### What happens next

The index is expected to launch by the end of September. Its practical importance for Pakistan will depend on how widely investors use the benchmark, the amount of money managed against it and the continued eligibility and liquidity of Pakistani government securities.

For Pakistan, the immediate development is therefore not a guaranteed inflow of foreign money but greater representation in a formal international benchmark for frontier-market local-currency debt.
