Pakistan is expected to receive the maximum permitted country weighting in JPMorgan's new frontier-market government bond index, giving Pakistani securities a prominent place in the benchmark.

The GBI-EM Edge index is expected to launch by the end of September 2026, according to details reported by Reuters. It will cover nearly $330 billion in eligible local-currency government debt across 26 countries.

Pakistan is expected to reach the index's 8% country cap. This limits the share any single country can occupy; it does not guarantee investors will put 8% of their money into Pakistani bonds.

Other countries expected to feature include Egypt, Vietnam, Morocco, Bangladesh, Nigeria and Sri Lanka.

### Why the index matters

A bond index tracks a selected group of securities. International investment funds can use it to decide which bonds to hold or to compare investment performance.

Inclusion may increase Pakistan's visibility among overseas investors, but it does not guarantee fresh foreign investment.

Eligible bonds must have at least $250 million equivalent outstanding and a remaining maturity of at least two and a half years.

Reuters reported a nominal yield of around 10.4% for the new benchmark, higher than JPMorgan's mainstream emerging-market local-currency index.

Higher returns can attract buyers, but foreign investors must also consider exchange-rate and interest-rate risks.

JPMorgan's historical testing suggested the new index could have performed better than its mainstream benchmark over nine years. Past simulations, however, cannot predict future returns.

For Pakistan, the practical impact will depend on how many investment funds adopt the benchmark and purchase its constituent bonds. The immediate development is greater representation in an international index, not a confirmed inflow of funds.
