**ISLAMABAD, 17 November 2026 —** The federal government has signed an interim fiscal agreement with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) under which the province is expected to contribute Rs157 billion during the current financial year, completing a broader arrangement for provincial grants totalling Rs1.035 trillion.

The agreement, signed earlier this month under Article 164 of the Constitution, was confirmed by Tariq Pasha, finance and revenue adviser to KP's Advisory Council, according to The Express Tribune.

The arrangement follows an understanding reached in June for provinces to support national security expenditure and development projects through grants to the federal government.

### Provincial contributions and funding conditions

Under the annual framework, Punjab is expected to contribute Rs556 billion, Sindh Rs264 billion, KP Rs157 billion and Balochistan Rs58 billion.

Federal fiscal figures cited in the latest reporting show that the four provinces contributed approximately Rs107.3 billion between July and October 2026, representing about 10.3% of the annual commitment.

Punjab accounted for Rs55.7 billion, followed by Sindh with Rs27.3 billion, KP with Rs17.8 billion and Balochistan with Rs6.5 billion.

The grants depend on the Federal Board of Revenue meeting its annual tax collection target of Rs15.264 trillion. A revenue shortfall would reduce the provincial contributions.

Balochistan's participation is subject to an additional condition protecting its federal transfers at the previous year's level.

### NFC shares remain unchanged

The agreement provides for grants to be made from provincial revenue allocations without formally changing the distribution established under the seventh National Finance Commission (NFC) Award.

Provincial shares will continue to be calculated and transferred under the existing constitutional framework, while agreed contributions may be deducted at source and treated as grants to the Centre.

The arrangement also covers Rs17.8 billion already deducted from KP's transfers during July–October.

The deductions had previously been disputed by KP's former finance adviser, Muzzammil Aslam, who maintained that the provincial government had not approved the grants and questioned the federal government's authority to withhold funds.

### Implications for federal finances

The agreement comes as the federal government seeks additional resources for security expenditure and nationally important infrastructure projects.

The Centre has recently approved Rs275 billion for six priority water-sector projects, according to the latest reporting.

The agreement leaves the existing NFC revenue-sharing formula unchanged. However, the federal government's actual receipts will depend on tax collection and implementation of the agreed payment mechanism, meaning it may receive less than the planned Rs1.035 trillion.
