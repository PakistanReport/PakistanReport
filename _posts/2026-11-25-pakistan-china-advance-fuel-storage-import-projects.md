**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan and China have agreed to pursue government-to-government arrangements for the development of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) infrastructure and motor gasoline import facilities, as part of broader efforts to strengthen Pakistan's energy security.

Federal Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik and Pakistan State Oil (PSO) Chief Executive Officer Jawwad Ahmed Cheema met Chinese Ambassador Jiang Zaidong and his commercial delegation to discuss investment opportunities.

The two countries identified two projects for priority implementation:

- Development of an LPG plant with associated storage and terminal infrastructure to expand domestic availability and address seasonal shortages.
- Establishment of petrol sourcing, storage and logistics arrangements to improve supply reliability and facilitate competitively priced imports.

Both sides agreed to establish a joint commercial team to assess potential projects, identify prospective investors and formulate commercially viable partnership arrangements.

Particular attention was given to investment opportunities in Balochistan and the potential establishment of integrated offshore energy hubs.

PSO is expected to serve as a principal commercial partner for Chinese investment in LPG facilities, petroleum imports, storage terminals and fuel logistics, while the wider cooperation involving the Oil and Gas Development Company Limited (OGDCL) and other national energy companies would encompass oil and gas exploration, offshore development and mineral sector investment.

Malik outlined the government's plans for gradual petroleum sector deregulation, simplified trade procedures and improved security arrangements for foreign investors.

Ambassador Jiang expressed China's interest in expanding commercial partnerships and facilitating coordination between Pakistani and Chinese enterprises.

The discussions follow the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation leaders' meeting held in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19.
