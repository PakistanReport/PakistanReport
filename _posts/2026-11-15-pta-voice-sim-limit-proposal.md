**ISLAMABAD, 15 November 2026 —** The Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) is considering reducing the number of mobile voice SIM cards permitted against a single Computerised National Identity Card (CNIC) from five to three, as part of a review aimed at limiting the misuse of mobile connections.

The regulator is also examining whether to increase the allowance for data SIMs to accommodate growing demand for internet-connected devices and future telecommunications services.

Under the existing arrangement, an individual may register up to eight SIM cards against one CNIC, comprising five voice SIMs and three data SIMs.

According to officials cited in reports published on Saturday, the proposed reduction in voice SIMs is intended to address concerns about the use of multiple mobile numbers in cybercrime and other illegal activities.

Officials believe that most customers generally use two or three voice SIMs, although people who maintain several numbers for business or personal purposes could be more directly affected by any change.

At the same time, the PTA is considering a higher limit for data SIMs as Pakistan prepares for wider use of fifth-generation mobile technology and connected equipment.

Data SIMs can provide internet access to devices such as routers, monitoring systems and other equipment that communicates over mobile networks.

The proposed changes have not been finalised. Reports indicate that the authority intends to consult mobile network operators before settling on a revised policy.

The review reflects an effort to balance security concerns with changing connectivity requirements.

For now, the existing SIM registration limits remain the relevant rules, and customers should wait for an official PTA announcement before making changes to their mobile connections.
