ISLAMABAD: The number of income tax returns filed in Pakistan reached 5.77 million by September 30, rising nearly 45 per cent from the same point a year earlier as the tax filing base continued to expand.

Federal Board of Revenue data showed 5,767,384 returns had been filed by the end of September, compared with 3,980,692 on the corresponding date last year.

That represents an increase of about 1.79 million returns in a year.

The latest figures extend a multi-year increase in the number of returns filed by the end of September. Comparable filings stood at 1.86 million in 2022, 1.92 million in 2023, 3.72 million in 2024 and 3.98 million in 2025 before reaching 5.77 million this year.

The number is therefore now roughly three times the level recorded at the same stage in 2022.

Non-salaried individuals accounted for most of the latest increase. According to the FBR data reported with the figures, traders, professionals and other non-salaried taxpayers contributed around four-fifths of the year-on-year rise.

The filing count measures returns submitted to the tax system and should not be confused with the amount of revenue collected. A larger number of returns can broaden the documented taxpayer base, but the tax paid by individual filers varies according to income, assets, deductions and other circumstances.

FBR has separately reported Rs3.083 trillion in overall federal tax collection during the first quarter of the 2026-27 fiscal year. That figure covers multiple federal taxes and is not a measure of revenue generated solely by the 5.77 million income tax returns.

The September 30 filing total was recorded before FBR subsequently extended the Tax Year 2026 return deadline to October 15 for people whose returns were due at the end of September.

That means the final number of returns submitted during the extended filing period will be higher than the 5.77 million recorded by the original deadline.

The increase comes as the government seeks to widen Pakistan’s tax base and improve documentation of economic activity. Expanding the number of filers has been a recurring policy objective, particularly in sectors where income and transactions have historically been harder for tax authorities to document.

A rise in filings, however, does not on its own establish how much of the economy has become fully compliant. Some returns can carry relatively small tax liabilities, while enforcement and accurate declaration remain separate issues.

For readers tracking the filing campaign, the clearest comparison is the year-on-year count: 5,767,384 returns by September 30 this year against 3,980,692 by the same date last year, an increase of nearly 45 per cent.

With the deadline now extended to October 15 for eligible Tax Year 2026 filers, the filing total will continue to change over the next two weeks.
