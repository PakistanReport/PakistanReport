**RIYADH, 19 November 2026 —** A skyscraper housing Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund was damaged in Riyadh on Thursday after a missile attack attributed to Yemen's Houthi movement, as fighting between the kingdom and the Iran-aligned group intensified.

The **Public Investment Fund (PIF) Tower**, located in the King Abdullah Financial District, sustained damage across several floors following an early-morning explosion.

Witnesses reported shattered windows and damage to the building's exterior, while emergency vehicles were seen in the surrounding area.

### Saudi Authorities Report Falling Missile Debris

The Saudi-led military coalition said its forces had responded to a missile launched towards Riyadh by the Houthis.

Coalition spokesman Major General Turki Al-Maliki said an initial assessment indicated that debris had fallen and caused material damage, with authorities examining the extent of the impact.

Saudi Civil Defence issued an emergency warning to residents shortly before the explosion and later announced that the immediate danger had passed.

The Houthis had not immediately claimed responsibility for Thursday's attack, although the group has carried out repeated missile and drone operations against Saudi targets in recent weeks.

### Financial District Targeted Again

The PIF Tower stands approximately **385 metres tall** and has 80 storeys, making it one of Riyadh's most prominent commercial buildings.

It is situated in the King Abdullah Financial District, a major business centre that also accommodates the Saudi stock exchange and international financial institutions.

Thursday's incident was the second reported attack affecting the district this month.

On 8 November, falling debris damaged an office building belonging to Saudi oil company Aramco. Saudi authorities said that incident caused no casualties.

Following the latest attack, entrances to the financial district were closed and people were evacuated from the area, according to witnesses cited by Reuters.

### Fighting Escalates Between Saudi Arabia and Houthis

The attack comes amid renewed fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis following the breakdown of a United Nations-brokered truce that had largely reduced hostilities since 2022.

The Houthis have intensified missile and drone attacks against Saudi cities, airports and energy infrastructure, while Saudi forces have conducted airstrikes against Houthi-controlled areas of Yemen.

The latest escalation has added another dimension to the wider regional conflict involving Iran and heightened concerns about security across the Gulf.

Saudi Arabia is a major global oil exporter, and continued attacks on its infrastructure could increase uncertainty surrounding regional investment, energy supplies and commercial activity.
