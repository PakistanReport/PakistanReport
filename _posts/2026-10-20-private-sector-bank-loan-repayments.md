Pakistani businesses repaid more bank loans than they borrowed during the first 100 days of the financial year 2026–27, raising questions about demand for financing and business investment.

According to figures reported from State Bank of Pakistan data, private-sector loan repayments exceeded new borrowing by Rs91.8 billion during the period.

In the same period last year, the difference was Rs31.7 billion.

The increase suggests that businesses are reducing their outstanding bank debt more quickly than they were a year earlier.

## Why are businesses repaying more?

Businesses borrow money for several reasons, including buying raw materials, managing daily expenses, purchasing machinery and expanding operations.

They also regularly repay loans as part of their normal financial activities.

When repayments exceed new borrowing, the total amount of outstanding bank credit falls.

This does not mean banks have stopped providing loans. It means businesses collectively repaid more than they borrowed during the period.

Several factors could explain the trend, including seasonal repayments, changes in borrowing costs and businesses delaying new investments.

The available figures do not establish which of these factors has had the greatest effect.

## Borrowing patterns differ across banks

Conventional banks recorded net repayments of Rs122.6 billion, compared with Rs57 billion during the same period last year.

Islamic banks also reported higher net repayments, reaching Rs107.8 billion compared with Rs38 billion a year earlier.

However, Islamic banking branches operated by conventional banks provided Rs138.6 billion in net financing, up from Rs63.4 billion.

The differences show that lending activity is not moving in the same direction across all parts of the banking system.

## What does this mean for the economy?

Bank financing plays an important role in helping businesses expand production, purchase equipment and manage operating costs.

If businesses consistently borrow less for expansion, it can be a sign of weaker investment activity.

However, a few months of net repayments are not enough to establish a wider slowdown.

Private-sector borrowing increased by approximately Rs1.46 trillion during the previous financial year, showing that lending conditions can change significantly over time.

The coming months will provide a clearer indication of whether businesses are delaying investment or simply following their usual borrowing and repayment cycles.

For now, the figures show a reduction in outstanding bank credit, rather than a complete withdrawal from borrowing.
