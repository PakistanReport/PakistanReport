Workers’ remittances to Pakistan rose about 14% year on year to approximately $10.87 billion during July–September 2026, the first quarter of fiscal year 2026–27, according to State Bank of Pakistan figures reported on 10 October.

September inflows were approximately $3.6 billion, 12.7% above September 2025 but 1.9% below August 2026. The month-on-month decline means the quarterly gain should not be described as uninterrupted monthly acceleration.

## Why the inflows matter

Remittances bring foreign exchange into Pakistan and support household spending. They also appear in the current account as secondary-income inflows, helping offset deficits in merchandise trade and other external payments.

However, stronger remittances do not automatically produce a current-account surplus or an equivalent rise in central-bank foreign-exchange reserves. The State Bank’s latest balance-of-payments figures show a current-account deficit of about $874 million for July–September, reflecting pressure from imports and the trade gap. Reserve levels are also affected by debt repayments, official financing and other financial flows.

## What to watch

The first-quarter remittance total provides an early indication of progress towards the government’s reported $44 billion annual remittance target, but extrapolating three months into a full-year outcome would be premature. Future monthly data will show whether inflows remain resilient amid economic and geopolitical uncertainty in the Gulf, where many Pakistani workers are employed.

This article covers the completed July–September quarter rather than repeating Pakistan Report’s earlier August-only remittance update.
