Pakistan's government is working on about 174 amendments to various laws sought by the International Monetary Fund under the country's two ongoing programmes, the National Assembly Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue has been told.

Finance Secretary Imdadullah Bosal told the committee that the proposed changes cover areas including financial-sector governance, state-owned enterprises, foreign remittances, climate-related measures and local-currency integration.

The amendments have not been enacted. Bosal said the proposed changes would be placed before Parliament, whose approval remains its prerogative.

That distinction is important because programme commitments agreed with the IMF can require the government to prepare or pursue legislative changes, but they do not by themselves replace Pakistan's parliamentary process.

## Committee reviews IMF commitments

The briefing formed part of the committee's examination of Pakistan's engagement with the IMF under the Extended Fund Facility and Resilience and Sustainability Facility.

Officials told lawmakers that about $4.5 billion had so far been disbursed by the IMF and that three programme reviews had been completed.

The government's programme commitments span a wider set of economic policies, including revenue mobilisation, debt sustainability, fiscal consolidation, taxation, energy-sector reforms, governance of state-owned enterprises and trade liberalisation.

The committee was also briefed on commitments involving parliamentary consideration of supplementary expenditure beyond approved budgets, restrictions on new tax amnesties and preferential tax treatment, and implementation of the National Fiscal Pact with the provinces.

Other areas discussed included the Sovereign Wealth Fund, remittances and policy measures affecting different parts of the economy.

## Parliament retains approval role

Committee members sought more detail on implementation timelines, outstanding commitments and the effects of reforms on citizens, businesses and taxpayers.

The reported figure of 174 should be understood as the number of amendments described by the finance secretary during the parliamentary briefing. It is not a claim that 174 separate laws have already been changed or that Parliament has approved them.

Pakistan's IMF-supported programmes are intended to address balance-of-payments pressures and support macroeconomic stability while requiring a broad programme of fiscal, governance and structural reforms.

The next stage for any amendment requiring legislation will depend on the government's proposals and Parliament's consideration of them.
