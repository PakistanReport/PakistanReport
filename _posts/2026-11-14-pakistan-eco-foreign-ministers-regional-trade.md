**ISLAMABAD, November 14, 2026 —** Pakistan will host the 30th Economic Cooperation Organization (ECO) Council of Ministers meeting on November 15 and 16, bringing together regional delegations to discuss trade, transport connectivity and economic integration.

Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar will chair the two-day gathering in Islamabad, with foreign ministers, senior representatives and officials from the ECO Secretariat expected to participate.

According to the Foreign Office, senior officials from ECO member states will meet ahead of the ministerial session to review the organisation's programmes and prepare the agenda and draft outcome documents for consideration by ministers.

Pakistan, which holds the ECO chairmanship for 2026–27, has adopted the theme “Reviving Regionalism for a Resilient ECO”, placing greater emphasis on regional connectivity, economic partnerships and sustainable development.

The government aims to strengthen Pakistan's position as a trade and transit corridor linking Central Asian economies with international markets through improved transport networks and logistics.

The ECO comprises Pakistan, Iran, Türkiye, Afghanistan, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan.

Preparations have included diplomatic consultations with participating countries. Dar recently discussed the meeting with Uzbekistan's Foreign Minister Bakhtiyor Saidov, with both sides reaffirming their commitment to closer bilateral and multilateral cooperation.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi is also expected to attend, according to Pakistani media reports.

Separately, *The Nation* reported, citing diplomatic sources, that Afghanistan's Taliban administration had not been invited amid strained bilateral relations. The Foreign Office's public announcement did not confirm that report or provide a complete list of participating delegations.

Any concrete agreements or policy commitments will become clearer after ministers review the proposed documents and conclude their discussions on November 16.
