**GWADAR:** A Panama-flagged cargo vessel carrying more than 26,000 tonnes of goods has arrived at Gwadar Port from the United Arab Emirates, marking another shipment handled under Pakistan's expanding transshipment operations.

The vessel, MV Great Wen Tao, sailed from Fujairah and berthed at Gwadar, where unloading operations have begun.

The cargo is to be temporarily stored at the port before being loaded onto another vessel for onward transportation to Fujairah, according to details reported on Tuesday.

Transshipment involves transferring goods between vessels at an intermediate port rather than delivering them directly to their final destination.

Gwadar Port Authority Chairman Noor-ul-Haq Baloch said the continued arrival of international cargo vessels reflected growing interest among shipping companies and commercial operators in using the port.

He said Gwadar's location and cargo-handling facilities could help it attract more regional shipping activity, creating opportunities for logistics companies, port operators and related businesses.

The latest arrival follows several transshipment operations at Gwadar earlier this year, as disruptions to established shipping routes in the Gulf encouraged companies to consider alternative arrangements.

In May, the port received MV Shu Long carrying more than 16,000 tonnes of cargo, including industrial equipment and pipes. Port officials said four transshipment vessels had been handled during April.

Another vessel, MV Jahan Brothers II, arrived in July with more than 53,000 tonnes of steel billets intended for onward shipment to the UAE.

Pakistan has also introduced measures aimed at making its ports more attractive to international shipping companies, including changes to transshipment procedures and reductions in certain cargo-handling charges.

The government has been seeking to expand the use of Gwadar as a regional logistics centre, particularly for trade involving Gulf countries and other markets.

Baloch said further improvements to the port's operational capacity were being pursued to support additional cargo traffic.
