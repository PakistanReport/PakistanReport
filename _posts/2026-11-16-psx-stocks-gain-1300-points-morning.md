**KARACHI, 16 November 2026 —** Pakistan's benchmark stock index climbed more than 1,300 points during Monday's morning session, extending the previous week's gains as buying interest spread across energy, banking and other major sectors.

The KSE-100 Index stood at 179,230.41 points at 11:25am, according to the Pakistan Stock Exchange's official data portal. That represented an increase of 1,356.54 points, or 0.76%, from Friday's close of 177,873.87.

The index had earlier touched 179,578.10 points before retreating from its session high, indicating that the initial buying momentum had moderated.

The advance was visible beyond the benchmark index. The KSE-30 rose 0.81%, while the All-Share Index gained 0.72%. The exchange's Oil and Gas Tradable Index increased 1.78%, compared with a 0.69% rise in its banking index.

An earlier PSX market snapshot, taken at 10:46am, showed 335 listed securities advancing against 142 declining, with 90 unchanged. Trading volume across the exchange had exceeded 515 million shares at that point.

Business Recorder reported buying in oil and gas exploration companies, commercial banks, power producers, automobile assemblers and cement manufacturers during early trading.

Among the prominent companies reported higher were Mari Energies, Oil and Gas Development Company, Pakistan Petroleum, Pakistan Oilfields, Hub Power Company and several major banks, including HBL, Meezan Bank, National Bank and United Bank.

The gains followed a strong week for Pakistani equities. The KSE-100 added 3,987.19 points, or approximately 2.3%, during the week ended November 13, despite periods of sharp volatility.

Market coverage by Business Recorder and The Express Tribune linked the previous week's improved sentiment partly to lower international oil prices and stronger remittances from overseas Pakistanis.

The remittance improvement is supported by official figures. The State Bank of Pakistan reported that workers sent home $3.9 billion in October, an increase of 13.8% from a year earlier. Total inflows during July–October reached $14.8 billion, up 14% year-on-year.

A separate weekly review by Mettis Global identified oil and gas exploration shares as important contributors to the earlier advance. It also highlighted investor interest in Mari Energies following a copper-gold discovery reported by one of its subsidiaries in Balochistan.

Pakistan's positive opening contrasted with weaker trading across several Asian markets on Monday. Reuters reported that regional investors were assessing upcoming US technology earnings and Federal Reserve policy signals.

For the local market, the immediate question is whether the morning's broad-based buying can be sustained through the remainder of the session.

Although the KSE-100 remained comfortably above Friday's closing level at the latest verified reading, its retreat from the intraday high showed that gains were not proceeding in a straight line.

**All figures in this report are intraday observations. Monday's closing index level and final trading volumes had not been established at the time of writing.**
