A joint sitting of Pakistan’s parliament on **15 October 2026** approved two resolutions endorsing the presidential proclamations imposing a state of emergency and governor’s rule in **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa**, amid strong objections from opposition lawmakers.

The decisions mark a new parliamentary stage in the dispute over the federal government's intervention in the province. They follow the president's proclamations issued on **11 October**, rather than constituting a new declaration of emergency.

### Two separate constitutional resolutions

According to the parliamentary account, the first resolution approved the emergency proclamation under **Article 232** of the Constitution. The second endorsed the proclamation of governor’s rule under **Article 234**.

Minister for Parliamentary Affairs **Tariq Fazal Chaudhary** moved the resolutions. State broadcaster Radio Pakistan confirmed that the joint sitting adopted both measures before being prorogued.

### Government and opposition disagree

Federal officials have defended the intervention by pointing to the security situation in the province. PTI and other critics dispute the government's justification and have questioned the constitutional basis for the measures.

Opposition lawmakers also challenged the evidence offered for the intervention. The debate is therefore not simply about parliamentary numbers: it concerns the conditions under which the federal government may assume exceptional powers in a province.

The parliamentary endorsements are a significant development, but they do not by themselves resolve legal challenges or the broader political disagreement. The implementation of the measures, any judicial proceedings and the prospects for political negotiations remain central questions.
