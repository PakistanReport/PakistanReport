**ISLAMABAD:** Internet service provider Transworld Associates expects repairs to an international submarine cable fault affecting Pakistan's connectivity to be completed by December 6, warning that some users may experience slower internet speeds during peak hours.

In a notice to customers, the company said repair operations were underway and described December 6 as a tentative completion date.

The affected SEA-ME-WE 5 (SMW5) cable system developed a fault approximately 11 kilometres off Pakistan's coast, according to the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA).

The regulator previously warned that the disruption could cause intermittent connectivity problems and reduced service quality for some internet users.

Transworld said it had arranged alternative international routes to maintain connectivity while technical teams carry out repairs during a scheduled maintenance window.

The company is coordinating with the international SMW5 cable consortium to address the fault and restore normal service.

The latest incident follows another fault in the SMW5 system in July, which was repaired within days, according to the PTA.

Pakistan currently relies on seven international submarine cable systems operated by PTCL, Transworld Associates and Cyber Internet Services to carry much of its international internet traffic.

While alternative routes are helping maintain services, users may continue to experience slower speeds or intermittent disruptions until the affected cable is restored.
