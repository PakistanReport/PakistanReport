Pakistan's regasified liquefied natural gas prices have fallen sharply for September, with rates for the two Sui gas companies dropping by roughly 20 per cent or more from August as the cost of imported LNG declined.

The Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority's September pricing shows Sui Northern Gas Pipelines Limited's RLNG price at the transmission stage falling 19.99 per cent to $13.99 per million British thermal units, from $17.49 in August.

At the distribution stage, SNGPL's price fell 20.14 per cent to about $15.20 per mmBtu, compared with $19.02 a month earlier.

For Sui Southern Gas Company Limited, the transmission-stage price declined 21.53 per cent to $12.61 per mmBtu from $16.07, while the distribution-stage price fell 21.57 per cent to $14.22 from $18.13.

## Lower LNG import cost drives decline

The reduction follows a sharp fall in the delivered cost of LNG cargoes. The average delivered ex-ship price for two September cargoes was about $10.06 per mmBtu, compared with $12.12 in August and $18.96 in July.

The September supply included cheaper Qatari cargoes, easing the import-cost pressure that had pushed RLNG prices higher during the preceding months.

The final RLNG price paid through the gas-company systems remains above the delivered cargo price because it also incorporates elements such as terminal and importer charges, margins, retainage and system losses.

Those costs differ between SNGPL and SSGCL, which is one reason their notified transmission and distribution prices are not identical.

## What the cut means

The reduction is relevant to sectors that use RLNG, including parts of industry and power generation, because a lower fuel price can reduce the gas component of their energy costs.

It should not, however, be read as a blanket 20 per cent reduction in the natural-gas tariffs paid by all households. OGRA's RLNG pricing framework is separate from the notified consumer-gas tariff structure, and the effect depends on the category and fuel supplied to a particular consumer.

September's decline therefore primarily reflects a reversal in imported LNG costs after the unusually expensive cargo environment seen earlier in the summer.
