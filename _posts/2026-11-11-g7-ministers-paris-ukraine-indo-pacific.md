**PARIS:** Foreign ministers from the Group of Seven (G7) countries concluded two days of talks in Paris on Tuesday, reaffirming support for Ukraine and agreeing to coordinate their response to security and economic challenges in the Indo-Pacific.

The meeting, hosted by France on 9–10 November, brought together ministers from Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the United Kingdom and the United States, alongside European Union representatives and invited partner countries.

According to Japan's Foreign Ministry, the G7 ministers confirmed that they would continue supporting Ukraine, including assistance to strengthen its energy security and prepare for winter.

Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha attended the discussions as Russian attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure continued. Kyiv also sought faster deliveries of air-defence equipment and additional support for its electricity network.

On Indo-Pacific security, the ministers reaffirmed their opposition to attempts to change the regional status quo through force or coercion, including in the East China Sea, South China Sea and Taiwan Strait.

They condemned North Korea's nuclear and ballistic missile programmes and called for cooperation against its cyber activities, including cryptocurrency theft.

The ministers also agreed to work together against economic coercion, non-market trade practices and arbitrary export restrictions.

France's wider agenda included Middle East tensions, energy supply security, humanitarian assistance, maritime security and the risks associated with artificial intelligence.

The meeting coincided with the inaugural gathering of the G7+ Ports Network in Marseille, an initiative intended to strengthen cooperation against drug trafficking through international ports.
