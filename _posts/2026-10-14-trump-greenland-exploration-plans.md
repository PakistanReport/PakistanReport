US President Donald Trump has said large-scale exploration for oil, gas and minerals is being prepared in Greenland, renewing attention on the Arctic island's natural resources weeks after Washington reached a security agreement with Denmark and Greenland.

In a [statement posted on Truth Social on October 14](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/trump-says-oil-gas-mineral-exploration-begin-greenland-2026-10-14/), Trump said major exploration activity was getting ready to begin. He did not identify participating companies, specific exploration sites, a timetable or the approvals that any projects would require.

The announcement is a statement of intent, not confirmation that new drilling or mining operations have started.

Trump also claimed that experts believed Greenland had unusually large quantities of easily accessible oil. He did not identify those experts or provide evidence for the comparison, and the size and commercial accessibility of Greenland's potential petroleum resources remain uncertain.

## Security agreement does not confer resource rights

The comments come after the United States, Denmark and Greenland signed an agreement in September aimed at easing tensions over Trump's previous demands for greater US control of the territory.

The agreement expands the potential US military role in Greenland, including provision for additional military facilities and missile-defence cooperation, while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to self-determination.

Crucially, the agreement does not itself grant the United States special rights to explore or extract Greenland's oil, gas or minerals, according to an [Associated Press account of the deal and Trump's latest remarks](https://apnews.com/article/2974b68aae17d97a69439bd100847d24).

Greenland remains a self-governing territory within the Kingdom of Denmark. Trump's description of it as belonging to the United States does not change its legal or political status.

The distinction matters because expanded military access and commercial resource development are separate questions. Any exploration proposal would still have to navigate the territory's applicable regulatory and political framework.

## Resources attract interest but pose major challenges

Greenland has long attracted international interest for its mineral deposits, including rare earth elements used in electronics, energy technologies and defence equipment.

The Arctic territory also has potential offshore oil and natural gas resources. However, potential deposits are not the same as commercially recoverable reserves, and their development would depend on geological findings, economics, infrastructure and regulatory approval.

Exploration and extraction have faced difficult conditions for decades. Greenland's remote locations, harsh climate, limited transport and energy infrastructure, and challenging geology can raise costs substantially.

The Associated Press reported that earlier efforts involving rare earth minerals and petroleum had struggled to advance beyond exploration. No new project timetable or financing plan accompanied Trump's October 14 announcement.

These obstacles mean the prospect of large-scale resource development cannot be assessed solely from a political announcement. Commercial operators would need to establish whether deposits can be developed safely and profitably under the rules governing particular sites.

## Arctic strategy and unanswered questions

Trump's latest statement also emphasised Greenland's strategic location in the defence of North America. He argued that the island would play an important role in detecting and countering future aerial threats.

The United States already maintains a military presence in Greenland, and the recent trilateral agreement is intended to expand security cooperation. Its defence provisions, however, should not be confused with approval for commercial oil, gas or mining activity.

The immediate unanswered questions concern who would conduct the proposed exploration, where it would take place, which licences would be required and whether Greenland's authorities would support the projects.

There was no immediate detailed announcement from the US administration setting out those matters. The governments of Denmark and Greenland had not immediately commented on Trump's latest post, according to reporting available on October 14.

For now, the verified development is that the US president has publicly announced plans for substantial resource exploration. Whether those plans become licensed, financed and operational projects remains to be established.
