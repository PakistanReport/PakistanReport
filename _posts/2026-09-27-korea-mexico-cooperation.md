**MEXICO CITY —** South Korea and Mexico have adopted a four-year action plan to deepen cooperation across trade, investment, supply chains, energy, artificial intelligence and aerospace, broadening a relationship already anchored by substantial commercial ties.

South Korean President Lee Jae Myung and Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum agreed the 2026–30 Joint Action Plan during their September 24 summit in Mexico City.

The plan calls for regular high-level exchanges, renewed economic and scientific consultations and stronger coordination in multilateral forums, while laying out specific initiatives in finance, technology and space.

### Trade and investment remain at the centre

The two governments agreed to further strengthen bilateral economic relations and investment. They welcomed the conclusion of negotiations to modernise their investment protection agreement and committed to completing the steps required for signature.

The action plan also calls for discussions on an interbank credit facility of up to $100 million between the Export-Import Bank of Korea and Mexico’s Banco Nacional de Comercio Exterior.

Lee said during the visit that Mexico is a strategically important partner for South Korea as global supply chains are reorganised. He also raised the importance of expanding trade arrangements and creating a more stable environment for businesses operating across the two economies.

The two sides agreed to establish channels covering crude oil and critical resources as part of their supply-chain cooperation.

### AI and aerospace enter the bilateral agenda

The plan extends well beyond conventional trade.

South Korea and Mexico agreed to pursue cooperation in artificial intelligence and digital government, including exchanges of policy experience and expertise. Their intellectual-property authorities are also expected to explore cooperation on examination standards for AI-related inventions.

In aerospace, the Korea AeroSpace Administration, Mexico’s digital authorities and the Mexican Space Agency are to pursue peaceful space cooperation, including identifying joint projects. The plan also envisages a Korea–Mexico Space Day and greater contact between the two countries’ space industries.

A separate memorandum involves Korea Aerospace Industries, Mexico’s aerospace industry federation and POSCO International in cooperation aimed at advancing the aerospace sector.

Energy and future industries are also part of the wider agenda, alongside automotive supply chains and advanced manufacturing.

### A broader strategic relationship

The scope of the action plan shows both governments seeking to move the relationship beyond its established base in manufacturing and trade.

It also gives the partnership a multilateral dimension. Seoul and Mexico City agreed to strengthen coordination through MIKTA, APEC and the G20, including ahead of South Korea’s G20 presidency in 2028 and Mexico’s bid to hold the presidency in 2029.

Many of the initiatives still require implementation, follow-up agreements or negotiations. The significance of the summit therefore lies less in immediate commercial outcomes than in the breadth of the framework the two governments have established for the next four years.

How quickly that framework produces projects, investment and expanded market access will determine the practical impact of the new phase in South Korea–Mexico relations.
