**ISLAMABAD:** Sui Northern Gas Pipelines Limited (SNGPL) has warned that outstanding payments of Rs169.27 billion from power-sector companies are creating serious financial difficulties and affecting its ability to pay gas suppliers.

The company has written to the Central Power Purchasing Agency-Guarantee (CPPA-G) and the Petroleum Division, seeking urgent action to recover payments from government-operated power plants, independent power producers and other gas-based electricity generation facilities.

SNGPL said continued delays could also affect Pakistan State Oil (PSO) and Pakistan LNG Limited (PLL), which are responsible for payments associated with imported liquefied natural gas.

## Gas-Based Power Plants Owe the Largest Amount

According to figures provided by SNGPL, gas-based power plants account for Rs67.268 billion of the outstanding amount.

Power plants operated by WAPDA owe another Rs63.548 billion, while independent power producers have unpaid liabilities of Rs38.454 billion.

The outstanding payments include regular gas bills, adjustments to imported gas tariffs, contractual charges, the Gas Infrastructure Development Cess and penalties for delayed payments.

Among individual facilities, Guddu Power has outstanding dues of Rs37.959 billion, while Quaid-e-Azam Thermal Power Limited owes Rs30.062 billion.

Other major amounts include Rs22.701 billion against the Balloki power plant, Rs22.642 billion against Liberty Power and Rs21.395 billion against Nandipur Power.

## Delayed Payments Affect Gas Suppliers

SNGPL has attributed the accumulation of unpaid bills to insufficient funds released by CPPA-G and delays in payments from individual power plants.

The company said the situation has weakened its financial position and made it increasingly difficult to meet obligations to suppliers.

It warned that further delays could create problems for PSO and PLL in settling payments for imported re-gasified liquefied natural gas (RLNG), raising the possibility of defaults on international payment commitments.

RLNG is imported in liquid form and converted back into gas for use by power plants, industries and other consumers.

SNGPL has requested the Petroleum Division to coordinate with the Power Division and arrange the release of outstanding funds.

## Company Seeks Immediate Recovery

In separate correspondence dated 8 October, SNGPL demanded payment of Rs25.006 billion in dues associated with several power-sector facilities, including Nandipur and power plants in Muzaffargarh and Faisalabad.

The company has also identified unpaid amounts against power units that are no longer connected to its gas network.

SNGPL said it may use the recovery provisions available under its contracts if outstanding bills are not settled.

The latest warning highlights continuing financial pressure across Pakistan's gas and electricity sectors, where delayed payments by power producers can affect the companies responsible for purchasing and supplying fuel.
