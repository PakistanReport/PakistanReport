Gilgit-Baltistan has unveiled a Rs218.845 billion budget for 2026-27, setting out spending plans for development, government salaries and social programmes three months after the financial year began.

The budget allocates Rs43.97 billion for development expenditure and Rs149.87 billion for non-development spending. It also carries a deficit of more than Rs52 billion, underlining the gap between planned expenditure and available resources.

The delayed full-year budget follows an interim arrangement of Rs20.478 billion that covered the first quarter of the financial year.

Among the measures announced is an increase in the minimum wage from Rs40,000 to Rs44,000 a month. Government salary expenditure is budgeted at Rs63.57 billion, making it one of the largest components of recurrent spending.

The government has also allocated Rs1 billion for social protection and Rs4 billion for a social-sector endowment fund. The health allocation includes an additional Rs170 million for medicines.

Smaller but politically and socially significant allocations include Rs50 million for youth loans and Rs50 million for local elections. The budget also sets aside Rs5.034 billion for development projects spread across Gilgit-Baltistan's 10 districts.

At the same time, the government has ordered a 30 per cent reduction in non-development budgets for departments, an attempt to contain routine expenditure while preserving money for priority programmes.

Climate policy also appears in the spending plan. One per cent of development funds is to be directed to a climate account, while the government has announced a policy encouraging hybrid vehicles.

For Gilgit-Baltistan, the central budget challenge is familiar: the region has large infrastructure and public-service needs, difficult terrain and a relatively limited local revenue base. Roads, health facilities, schools and basic services can be more expensive to build and maintain across remote mountain districts than in densely populated parts of the country.

That makes execution as important as the headline allocations. The effectiveness of the new budget will depend on how quickly development money is released, whether projects can be completed within the financial year and how the government manages a deficit exceeding Rs52 billion.

With the first quarter already covered by an interim budget, attention now shifts from presentation to implementation — particularly the district development programme, social protection measures and the effort to restrain non-development spending.
