Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye have agreed to move immediately toward the practical implementation of collective-defence commitments under the Makkah Alliance for Defense, including arrangements for agreed military forces and capabilities to be rapidly deployed in Saudi Arabia.

The decision was announced after an emergency meeting of the alliance’s Strategic-Political and Defense Committee in Riyadh on October 5. In a joint statement issued by Pakistan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the three countries said the measures would be carried out under approved arrangements and the national laws of the participating states.

The statement did not disclose troop numbers, military units, equipment allocations or a timetable for deployment. It therefore does not establish that a specific Pakistani or Turkish force has already been deployed to Saudi Arabia.

According to the joint statement, the committee reviewed political and military consultations held before the meeting and approved recommendations intended to strengthen readiness, military integration and the three countries’ collective capacity to respond to threats.

The parties said the alliance represents a commitment to collective defence and that the security of each member is part of the collective security of all three countries.

The emergency meeting was convened as Saudi Arabia faced heightened security threats. Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye condemned attempted attacks targeting Makkah and Madinah as well as attacks on Saudi cities and civilian and economic facilities. Pakistan and Türkiye also reaffirmed support for Saudi Arabia’s security and territorial integrity.

The committee also appointed retired Pakistani Lieutenant General Nauman Mahmood as the alliance’s first secretary-general for a three-year term. The appointment is part of the institutional development of the defence arrangement.

The latest decision is significant because it moves the three-country framework from a political commitment toward operational planning. However, the public statement leaves important implementation details unspecified, including the size and composition of any forces that could be made available.

Pakistan has previously described the defence arrangement as defensive in nature and aimed at regional peace and security. The October 5 statement similarly framed the latest measures around deterrence, readiness and collective defence rather than announcing operations against a particular country.

For Pakistan, the next material development will be whether the alliance publishes further information on command arrangements, force contributions or actual deployments. Until then, the verified development is that the three governments have approved measures to provide agreed forces and capabilities and ensure their rapid deployment in Saudi Arabia when required under the alliance’s arrangements.
