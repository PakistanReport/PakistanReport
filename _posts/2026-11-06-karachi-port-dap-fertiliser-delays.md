**KARACHI, November 6, 2026** — Imported Di-Ammonium Phosphate (DAP) fertiliser shipments are facing delays at Karachi Port, raising concerns about supplies for Pakistan's 2026–27 wheat crop.

According to industry sources, vessels carrying DAP remain in berthing queues and at outer anchorage because of congestion and cargo-handling delays.

### Farmers Face Supply Difficulties

Agricultural areas in Punjab and Sindh are already experiencing fertiliser shortages during the October–November sowing period.

DAP provides phosphorus needed for early root development in wheat plants. Delayed application can affect crop growth and eventual yields.

Industry representatives say customs clearance delays and congestion at bulk cargo terminals are contributing to the disruption.

### Calls for Faster Port Clearance

Some fertiliser dealers have reportedly begun limiting sales because of reduced stocks, while concerns about price increases are growing.

Agriculture-sector representatives have urged the government and port authorities to prioritise fertiliser vessels, accelerate customs processing and arrange faster transportation to farming areas.

They warn that prolonged shortages could increase production costs and affect the coming wheat harvest.
