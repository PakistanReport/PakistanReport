**RIYADH, October 26, 2026:** Saudi Arabia has partially reopened King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh after a suspension of operations caused by a deadly attack earlier this month, even as missile and drone threats from Yemen's Houthi movement continue.

Airport authorities announced that Terminal 1 would resume handling domestic flights, while Terminal 2 would serve international flights operated by national carriers from Sunday, October 25.

The reopening follows an October 10 attack claimed by the Houthis that killed 13 people and injured more than 300, according to reports.

Despite the resumption of operations at two terminals, flight activity remained limited on Sunday, indicating that services had not returned to normal.

Saudi authorities also reported intercepting further missiles and drones targeting different parts of the kingdom over the weekend.

The Houthis renewed warnings against the use of Riyadh's airport, describing it as a potential target. Saudi Arabia has continued defensive operations in response to the attacks.

The security situation has raised concerns for airlines and passengers travelling through the Gulf, including Pakistani travellers who use Saudi airports for work, religious visits and connecting flights.

Passengers with bookings through Riyadh are advised to check directly with their airlines for the latest flight arrangements and any schedule changes.

The partial reopening marks a step towards restoring air connectivity, but continuing security threats mean further disruptions remain possible.
