The International Monetary Fund expects Pakistan's economy to grow by 3.5% in fiscal year 2026–27, while inflation is projected at 9.9%, according to its October 2026 economic outlook.

The figures are forecasts rather than final results. They reflect the IMF's assessment of economic conditions and assumptions at the time of publication.

### Growth outlook

A 3.5% expansion would indicate continued economic growth, although the pace would depend on developments in agriculture, manufacturing, services and investment.

Pakistan's performance is also sensitive to the cost of imported fuel, external financing conditions and demand for its exports.

Higher international energy prices can increase costs for businesses and households, potentially weakening activity in sectors that depend heavily on transport or electricity.

The government's reform programme and access to financing may also influence confidence and investment decisions.

### Inflation remains a concern

The IMF's 9.9% inflation projection suggests that price pressures could remain significant during the fiscal year.

Inflation measures the rate at which prices change. A lower inflation rate would not necessarily mean that the overall cost of living has returned to an earlier level.

Food, fuel and utility costs can also move differently from the national average, affecting households in different ways.

### Forecasts can change

The IMF may revise its estimates as new information becomes available. Changes in commodity prices, weather conditions, government policy and international markets could alter both growth and inflation outcomes.

The forecast should therefore be read as a guide to the Fund's current expectations, not a guarantee of what will happen.

For policymakers, the challenge is to support economic activity while limiting inflation and managing public finances.

Future official data on production, consumer prices and external payments will provide a clearer picture of whether the economy is following the projected path.
