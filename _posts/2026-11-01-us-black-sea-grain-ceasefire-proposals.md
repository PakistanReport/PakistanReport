**ATLANTA:** The United States is examining proposals for a limited ceasefire in the Black Sea as governments seek ways to protect grain shipments from disruption caused by the Russia-Ukraine war.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on October 31 that Türkiye and India had presented ideas for restoring the movement of agricultural cargo through the waterway.

Speaking after a G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta, Rubio said international interest was growing in arrangements that could protect commercial shipping without waiting for a settlement of the wider war.

The discussions are focused on a narrower objective: allowing vessels carrying grain and potentially energy supplies to move more safely.

## Türkiye and Ukraine discuss grain corridor

On the same day, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan met Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Istanbul.

Following their talks, Zelenskyy said the two countries had agreed to continue work on a Black Sea grain corridor.

Erdogan urged Russia and Ukraine to stop attacks on civilian vessels, warning that further threats to commercial shipping would worsen tensions.

Türkiye has maintained diplomatic contact with both countries throughout the war and has previously played a role in negotiations over agricultural exports.

The original Black Sea Grain Initiative, agreed in 2022, allowed Ukrainian grain to leave Black Sea ports under an internationally supported arrangement. Russia withdrew from that agreement in 2023.

## Food supplies and winter energy concerns

The latest discussions extend beyond shipping alone.

Canadian Foreign Minister Anita Anand said she had spoken with Ukrainian and Indian counterparts about a limited Black Sea ceasefire and a possible halt to attacks on Ukraine's electricity infrastructure.

Those discussions reflect concerns over food supply chains and energy security as winter approaches.

For countries dependent on imported grain, interruptions to Black Sea trade can affect access to supplies and contribute to price uncertainty.

However, the proposals remain under discussion. Neither Russia nor Ukraine has announced a new, mutually agreed Black Sea ceasefire.

The immediate diplomatic effort is to find a limited arrangement that protects commercial vessels while broader peace negotiations remain unresolved.
