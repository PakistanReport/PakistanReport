Ten local and foreign prospective investors have submitted expressions of interest for the privatisation of Islamabad Electric Supply Company, moving the sale of one of Pakistan's major electricity distribution companies into its next stage.

The Privatisation Commission said it received 10 expressions of interest for the acquisition of between 51% and 100% of IESCO's shareholding together with management control. Three of the interested parties are from Türkiye, while seven are Pakistani companies or consortiums.

The Turkish companies are Aktor Elektrik Enerji Yatirimlari, Genvera Enerji and Cengiz Enerji. Pakistani participants include Engro Energy, Sapphire Fibres, Novatex and Bestway Cement, as well as consortiums led by Artistic Milliners, Hub Power Holding and Hasnaat Brothers Construction Company.

The submissions are expressions of interest rather than binding bids. The commission will now evaluate the applicants' statements of qualification against its approved prequalification criteria.

Applicants that meet the requirements will be invited to the next stage and given access to a virtual data room to conduct detailed buy-side due diligence before the transaction proceeds further.

## First batch of DISCO privatisations

IESCO is one of three electricity distribution companies included in the first batch of the government's DISCO privatisation programme, alongside Faisalabad Electric Supply Company and Gujranwala Electric Power Company.

The Privatisation Commission says 10 interested parties have already been prequalified for FESCO. It received 11 expressions of interest for GEPCO, which are being evaluated for prequalification.

The government is seeking private-sector participation in electricity distribution as part of a wider effort to reform a power sector burdened by distribution losses, weak recoveries and persistent financial pressures.

Distribution companies sit at the consumer-facing end of the electricity system, delivering power and collecting bills. Their operational and financial performance therefore has direct implications for cash flows across the power sector.

The commission has said the IESCO transaction is intended to improve operational efficiency, modernise distribution infrastructure, strengthen customer service and reduce losses. Those are the government's objectives for the sale; the eventual outcome will depend on the transaction terms, regulatory framework and performance of any successful buyer.

Adviser to the Prime Minister on Privatisation Muhammad Ali, who also chairs the Privatisation Commission, described the response as an important milestone in the DISCO privatisation programme. The commission said it would engage with prequalified investors during due diligence and seek a transparent and predictable post-privatisation framework.

## What happens next

The immediate step is prequalification, not bidding. The number of expressions of interest does not indicate the eventual sale price, and interested parties are not committed to submit final offers simply by entering the qualification process.

The proposed structure is nevertheless significant. By offering 51% to 100% of the company together with management control, the government is seeking to transfer effective operational control rather than sell a passive minority holding.

IESCO serves Islamabad and surrounding areas, making the transaction an important test of the government's ability to attract private capital and management into electricity distribution.

The response follows domestic and international roadshows conducted by the Privatisation Commission and comes as the government attempts to move the first group of DISCO transactions from preparation towards competitive sale processes.
