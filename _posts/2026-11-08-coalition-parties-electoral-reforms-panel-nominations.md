# Coalition Parties Submit Electoral Reforms Panel Nominations as Opposition Decision Remains Pending

**ISLAMABAD, November 8, 2026 —** Pakistan's ruling coalition parties have begun submitting nominations for a proposed parliamentary committee on electoral reforms, while opposition parties, including Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI), have yet to confirm their participation.

Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry said the proposed committee would comprise 30 members drawn from the National Assembly and Senate, with representation based on parties' parliamentary strength.

## Proposed Committee Membership

Under the proposed allocation, Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz (PML-N) would receive 10 seats and Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) seven, according to the minister.

Chaudhry said PML-N might need to adjust its allocation to accommodate smaller parties.

The minister had earlier announced that the Senate component of the committee would include two members each from PML-N, PPP and PTI.

Awami National Party, Balochistan Awami Party, National Party and Jamiat Ulema-i-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) would each receive one Senate seat.

## Opposition Yet to Confirm Participation

Chaudhry said he had requested nominations from PTI's Aamir Dogar, but the party had sought additional time to consider the proposal.

PTI Information Secretary Sheikh Waqas Akram said the party supported electoral reforms but questioned whether the proposed representation would allow the opposition to influence the committee's recommendations.

He said PTI wanted reforms addressing election transparency, including the use of electronic voting machines.

The party had not announced a final decision on participation.

JUI-F spokesperson Aslam Ghouri also said his party had not yet decided whether to join the proposed committee.

## Coalition Nominations Move Forward

PPP and other coalition partners have submitted proposed names, although the full membership list has not been formally announced.

Chaudhry said PML-N was awaiting final approval from its leadership before completing its own nominations.

The proposed committee is intended to provide a parliamentary forum for discussing changes to Pakistan's electoral framework.

Its final composition and the extent of opposition participation remained unresolved as of November 8.
