**ISLAMABAD:** A World Bank energy mission is undertaking a five-day visit to Pakistan from November 9 to 13 to review ongoing projects, assess progress across its energy-sector portfolio and hold discussions with senior government officials and development partners.

The delegation is led by Moez Cherif, the World Bank's Practice Manager for Energy in the Middle East, North Africa, Afghanistan and Pakistan (MENAAP) region. It is his first visit to Pakistan since assuming the position on July 1, 2026.

According to details of a World Bank letter addressed to Economic Affairs Secretary Muhammad Humair Karim, reported by Business Recorder and Profit by Pakistan Today, the mission will engage with federal ministries, energy institutions and international development organisations during meetings in Islamabad and Lahore.

The discussions are intended to review existing cooperation, examine progress on ongoing initiatives and address matters relating to the World Bank's energy-sector portfolio in Pakistan.

## Meetings With Power and Petroleum Authorities

The programme begins with internal World Bank meetings in Islamabad on November 9, followed by a visit to Lahore on November 10.

In Lahore, the delegation is scheduled to meet the managing director of the National Grid Company (NGC) and the chairman of the Water and Power Development Authority (WAPDA).

The meetings will bring the World Bank team into discussions with two institutions involved in Pakistan's electricity infrastructure and power development.

On November 11, the mission is scheduled to meet the federal secretaries of the Power Division and Petroleum Division in Islamabad.

## Energy Efficiency, Provincial Projects and Privatisation

The mission's agenda also includes meetings with the National Energy Efficiency and Conservation Authority (NEECA) and the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Energy Development Organisation (PEDO).

Discussions with NEECA will focus on energy efficiency, while the engagement with PEDO will provide an opportunity to review matters involving provincial energy development.

The delegation is also scheduled to meet officials from the Privatisation Commission and Economic Affairs Division.

The inclusion of these institutions places energy conservation, provincial development and the government's wider institutional engagement with development partners on the visit's agenda.

## Consultations With International Development Partners

Alongside meetings with Pakistani authorities, the World Bank delegation will hold discussions with representatives of several international financial institutions.

These include the Asian Development Bank (ADB), International Finance Corporation (IFC), Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) and Islamic Development Bank (IsDB).

The reported schedule provides for meetings with ADB and IFC on November 11, followed by virtual consultations with AIIB and IsDB on November 12.

The delegation is also expected to attend the Pakistan Minerals Investment Forum on November 12, with a separate virtual discussion relating to mining included in its programme.

The mission is scheduled to depart Islamabad on November 13.

## Review of Existing Energy Engagement

The visit comes as Pakistan continues to work with international development institutions on electricity infrastructure, energy-sector performance and related reforms.

The World Bank has an existing financing relationship with Pakistan's power sector. In July 2026, it approved $375.9 million for the Grid Stability Enhancement Project, the first phase of a longer-term programme intended to modernise the country's electricity transmission network.

The latest mission forms part of the continuing engagement between Pakistan and the World Bank on energy-sector development and reform.
