**ISLAMABAD, November 8, 2026 —** Four of Pakistan's five major oil refineries have signed agreements to modernise their facilities under the amended refinery policy, while Pak-Arab Refinery Limited (PARCO) has requested an additional three months to complete its approval process.

Attock Refinery Limited, National Refinery Limited, Pakistan Refinery Limited and Cnergyico Petroleum signed their upgrade agreements with Inter State Gas Systems (ISGS) on September 24.

PARCO, the country's largest refinery, has yet to sign. The Petroleum Division has sought approval from the Cabinet Committee on Energy (CCoE) to extend its deadline from October 24, 2026, to January 24, 2027.

## Four Refineries Move Ahead With Upgrades

The agreements form part of the government's programme to modernise existing refineries, improve fuel quality and reduce the production of furnace oil.

The four participating companies are expected to invest approximately $5 billion over five years in upgrading their facilities.

The projects are intended to support the production of Euro-V standard petroleum products, increase refining efficiency and reduce dependence on imported fuels.

ISGS has been designated to implement the policy and oversee the agreements on behalf of the Petroleum Division.

## PARCO Awaits Board Approval

PARCO has informed the Petroleum Division that its upgrade agreement remains under consideration by the company's board.

The requested extension would provide time to complete internal approvals and execute the agreement.

PARCO accounts for approximately 38% of Pakistan's domestic petroleum-product production, making its participation particularly important to the country's refining sector.

The Petroleum Division has cautioned that restricting PARCO from selling fuel that does not yet meet Euro-V standards could disrupt domestic supplies and force Pakistan to import additional petroleum products.

## Temporary Regulatory Relief Proposed

Under the amended policy, refineries that sign upgrade agreements can receive temporary waivers from Euro-V fuel specifications while their modernisation projects are implemented.

The four companies that signed in September received these waivers on October 7.

The Petroleum Division has proposed similar temporary relief for PARCO during the requested extension period, subject to approval by the CCoE.

The proposal also addresses the treatment of deemed duty on high-speed diesel, including a recovery schedule linked to the committee's decision.

## Refinery Modernisation Programme

The amended refining policy was approved in July 2026, ratified by the federal cabinet in August and notified in September.

It provides incentives for existing refineries to invest in cleaner fuel production and modern processing technology.

With four agreements already signed, PARCO's pending participation remains a significant outstanding step in implementing the programme.
