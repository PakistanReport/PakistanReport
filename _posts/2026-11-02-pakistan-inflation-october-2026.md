**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan’s annual consumer price inflation eased to 9.6% in October 2026, compared with 10.3% in September, according to figures attributed to the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS) and reported on Monday, 2 November.

The latest reading represents a decline of 0.7 percentage points in the annual inflation rate.

Despite the slowdown, inflation remained above the 6.2% recorded in October 2025, indicating that price pressures were stronger than during the same month last year.

The October figure was broadly consistent with the Finance Ministry’s recent forecast of inflation between 9.5% and 10.5%.

## What the lower rate means

A reduction in inflation does not mean that consumer prices have fallen overall. It means the general price level increased at a slower annual pace than in the preceding month.

The consumer price index measures changes in the cost of a basket of goods and services purchased by households, including food, housing, transport and energy.

Changes in the index are closely watched because they influence household purchasing power, business costs and expectations about monetary policy.

Although October’s annual rate was lower than September’s, the reported figure indicates that households continued to face higher average prices than a year earlier.

## Energy prices remain an important risk

In its October economic update, the Finance Ministry warned that the future inflation outlook would remain sensitive to international energy prices, food supplies and the statistical effects of comparisons with previous years.

Higher global oil prices can affect domestic transport and production costs, while changes in agricultural supply can influence food prices.

The latest inflation reading will be one of several indicators considered by businesses and policymakers when assessing economic conditions.

More detailed analysis of price changes across individual categories will require the complete October CPI release from PBS.

The reported decline provides some relief in the pace of inflation, but it does not establish that the cost-of-living pressures faced by Pakistani households have ended.
