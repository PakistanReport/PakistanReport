PIA Holding Company's accumulated losses have reached roughly Rs741 billion, showing the scale of the financial burden that remains with the state even after the restructuring and privatisation of Pakistan International Airlines.

The holding company recorded an additional loss of about Rs21.6 billion during the July-December 2025 period, taking accumulated losses to approximately Rs740.6 billion.

PIA Holding Company was created as part of the restructuring of the national airline. Large amounts of old debt and other liabilities were transferred away from the operating airline and placed in the holding company, helping create a cleaner balance sheet for the carrier being offered to private investors.

That separation was important for the privatisation process, but it did not make the liabilities disappear. They remain a public-sector obligation and continue to carry financing costs.

At least Rs30 billion has been provided in the current federal budget to service old PIA debt, illustrating the continuing cost to taxpayers of liabilities accumulated over many years.

The distinction is particularly important when assessing the financial outcome of PIA's privatisation. The winning bid for a 75 per cent stake was valued at Rs135 billion, but only about Rs10 billion represented direct proceeds to the government. Most of the broader amount was structured as investment into the airline itself.

That investment can strengthen the operating company, but it is separate from the legacy debt sitting in the holding structure.

PIA's case is also part of a wider problem across Pakistan's state-owned enterprise sector. Loss-making federal SOEs recorded losses of roughly Rs343 billion in the first half of the relevant reporting period, placing continued pressure on public finances.

The holding-company model can make a business more attractive to investors by separating historic liabilities from its future operations. For the government, however, the key question becomes how those liabilities will eventually be financed, reduced or retired.

The airline's future performance and the state's legacy obligations should therefore be viewed separately. A commercially healthier PIA would be an important change, but it would not by itself erase the hundreds of billions of rupees in losses and debt that were moved out of the airline during restructuring.
