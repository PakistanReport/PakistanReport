**ISLAMABAD, October 27, 2026:** The Senate approved an amendment to Islamabad's consumer protection legislation on Monday despite opposition from the federal government, as a disagreement between lawmakers from the ruling coalition disrupted proceedings.

The Islamabad Consumers Protection (Amendment) Bill, 2026, introduced by Senator Samina Mumtaz Zehri, passed by 10 votes to four.

The vote followed an exchange involving Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz Senator Nasir Butt and Pakistan Peoples Party Senator Sherry Rehman.

PPP lawmakers subsequently walked out of the chamber.

Minister of State for Interior Tallal Chaudhry opposed the legislation on behalf of the government, but the bill secured sufficient support to pass the upper house.

The proposal seeks amendments to the Islamabad Consumers Protection Act, 1995, including changes to Section 2 and the addition of a new Section 7A.

It had previously been examined by the Senate Standing Committee on Interior and Narcotics Control, which recommended its passage.

The disagreement drew attention because the PPP remains an important parliamentary partner of the PML-N-led federal government.

However, differences over an individual bill do not necessarily indicate a change in the parties' broader political relationship.

The outcome also illustrates the role of private members' legislation, which can progress through Parliament without government sponsorship.

Senate approval does not, by itself, mean that the proposed amendments have become law. Further legislative requirements must be completed before they can take effect.

The next development will be the bill's progress through the remaining parliamentary process and any response from the coalition parties to Monday's disagreement.
