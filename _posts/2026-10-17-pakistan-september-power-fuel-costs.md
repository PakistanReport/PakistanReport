Pakistan’s average electricity-generation fuel cost rose to Rs10.022 per unit in September 2026 from Rs7.29 a year earlier, a 37.48% increase, according to Central Power Purchasing Agency (CPPA) figures reported by Dawn on 17 October. Electricity consumption rose about 4% from September 2025, showing that the cost jump was substantially larger than the change in demand.

## Why costs rose

The fuel mix shifted towards more expensive generation as liquefied natural gas supply constraints and nuclear maintenance affected output. Imported coal generation rose to 2,155 gigawatt-hours (GWh), from 1,019 GWh a year earlier, increasing its share to 16.3% from about 8%. Imported-coal generation cost Rs17.24 per unit, compared with Rs13.74 a year earlier.

High-speed diesel generation reached about 213 GWh at a reported fuel cost of Rs62.14 per unit. Regasified LNG generation fell to about 804 GWh from 1,815 GWh, while nuclear output declined to 1,893 GWh from 2,227 GWh.

## What it means for consumers

The CPPA sought an additional fuel-cost adjustment of approximately Rs2.5624 per unit. That is a request, not evidence that the regulator has approved a corresponding charge. Any impact on consumer bills depends on the regulator’s decision and applicable tariff rules.

The September fuel-cost figure is not the same as the full retail electricity tariff, which can include capacity payments, distribution charges, taxes and other adjustments. Hydropower supplied roughly 37% of generation at no fuel cost, but its contribution did not prevent the average fuel cost from rising.
