**ISLAMABAD —** Pakistan and the International Monetary Fund have moved into the formal Islamabad phase of programme negotiations, with about $1.2 billion in combined financing potentially available if the fourth review of the Extended Fund Facility and third review of the Resilience and Sustainability Facility are completed successfully and later approved by the IMF Executive Board.

The visiting mission, led by Iva Petrova, began discussions with Pakistani authorities last week in Karachi. The review is now expected to involve the Ministry of Finance, Federal Board of Revenue and other federal institutions as the two sides assess programme performance through the end of June and the policy framework for the months ahead.

The current exercise also includes Pakistan’s Article IV consultation, the IMF’s broader periodic assessment of a member country’s economy.

### About $1.2 billion linked to the reviews

Successful completion of the two reviews would make Pakistan eligible for about $1 billion under the EFF and a further $200 million under the RSF. The financing is not automatic: an agreement at staff level would still require approval by the IMF Executive Board before funds could be released.

The EFF is a 37-month arrangement approved in September 2024. The IMF completed the third EFF review and second RSF review in May 2026, allowing Pakistan to draw about $1.1 billion under the EFF and about $220 million under the climate-focused facility at that stage.

The fourth EFF review is tied to end-June 2026 programme targets. Discussions are expected to examine fiscal performance, revenue mobilisation, energy-sector reforms and other structural commitments, alongside the effect of regional instability and higher energy costs on Pakistan’s economic outlook.

### Review comes amid external pressures

The talks are taking place against a more difficult international backdrop than at the time of the previous review. The Iran conflict and disruption around the Strait of Hormuz have increased uncertainty over fuel and freight costs, creating additional risks for an economy that remains dependent on imported energy.

That makes the assumptions underpinning growth, inflation, the current account and government finances particularly important during the review. Any revisions to the macroeconomic framework would have implications for the targets Pakistan is expected to meet during the remainder of the programme.

Tax collection will also remain closely watched. Revenue performance is central to the government’s fiscal targets, while power and gas sector reforms continue to form part of the structural agenda agreed with the Fund.

### Staff agreement would be the next milestone

The immediate milestone is not disbursement but completion of the mission and, if the two sides resolve outstanding issues, a staff-level agreement. Only after that stage would the reviews move to the IMF Executive Board.

The distinction is important because the roughly $1.2 billion associated with the reviews remains prospective until the required approvals are completed.

For Pakistan, the review also has significance beyond the direct IMF financing. Continued performance under the programme is closely watched by other official and commercial creditors and forms part of the wider assessment of the country’s external financing position.

The mission is expected to continue discussions into early October, leaving the final outcome dependent on whether Pakistan and IMF staff can reach agreement on programme implementation and the policy path ahead.
