**LAHORE:** Lahore topped the global air pollution ranking on Saturday morning with an air quality index (AQI) of 400, according to Swiss air quality monitoring platform IQAir.

Kabul ranked second with an AQI of 304, followed by Delhi at 238. Dubai and Karachi recorded readings of 168 and 160, respectively.

Pollution levels were considerably higher in some parts of Lahore, with a monitoring station in Allama Iqbal Town recording an AQI of 680.

IQAir's separate ranking of Pakistani cities showed eight urban centres recording hazardous air quality between 9am and 10am on November 28.

| City | AQI |
| --- | ---: |
| Peshawar | 414 |
| Sargodha | 404 |
| Gujranwala | 388 |
| Faisalabad | 384 |
| Lahore | 363 |
| Multan | 347 |
| Rahim Yar Khan | 328 |
| Sialkot | 301 |

An AQI above 300 is classified as hazardous, posing health risks to the general population. Children, older people and individuals with respiratory or cardiovascular conditions are particularly vulnerable.

Lahore previously topped the global major-city pollution ranking on November 22, when its AQI reached 538.

The Punjab government is implementing a $300 million World Bank-supported Clean Air Programme covering emissions from transport, agriculture and industrial activities.

The programme, which runs from April 2025 to June 2030, forms part of a broader effort to reduce concentrations of fine particulate matter (PM2.5) in Lahore Division by 35 per cent over a decade.

Vehicle emissions, industrial pollution and crop-residue burning contribute to deteriorating air quality during winter, when atmospheric conditions restrict the dispersal of pollutants.
