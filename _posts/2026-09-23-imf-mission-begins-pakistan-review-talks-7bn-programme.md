**KARACHI:** An International Monetary Fund mission has begun discussions with Pakistani authorities for the next review of the country's $7 billion Extended Fund Facility, opening a new round of talks that will examine fiscal, monetary and structural reforms.

The mission, led by Iva Petrova, is conducting the fourth review under the Extended Fund Facility and the third review under the Resilience and Sustainability Facility alongside the IMF's Article IV consultation, according to the Fund's resident representative in Pakistan.

The discussions began in Karachi, where the mission is engaging with the State Bank of Pakistan. Monetary policy, inflation and the exchange rate are among the areas expected to be reviewed before talks move to Islamabad for meetings with the Ministry of Finance, Federal Board of Revenue and other government departments.

The EFF, approved in September 2024, is a 37-month programme worth about $7bn. Pakistan also has a separate Resilience and Sustainability Facility arrangement focused on reforms intended to strengthen the country's ability to manage climate-related risks.

The review will assess Pakistan's performance against programme commitments through the end of June 2026. Tax reforms, energy-sector measures, fiscal performance and structural benchmarks are among the issues expected to figure in the negotiations.

A successful conclusion at the staff level would not itself release fresh financing. Any agreement reached between IMF staff and Pakistani authorities would subsequently require approval by the Fund's Executive Board. Current programme schedules indicate that approval could make roughly $1bn available under the EFF and about $200 million under the RSF.

The negotiations are taking place as Pakistan faces renewed pressure from higher international energy costs, complicating efforts to contain inflation and maintain external stability.
