**ISLAMABAD, 18 November 2026 —** Pakistan aims to increase annual gemstone exports to $1 billion within five years through modern mining practices, international certification and greater local processing.

Special Assistant to the Prime Minister on Energy Muhammad Zafar Iqbal announced the target at an international gemstone workshop in Islamabad.

He said Pakistan's gemstone resources were estimated at $450 billion, while recorded annual exports stood at approximately $5.8 million.

### Reforms to boost gemstone exports

The government is pursuing reforms under the *National Policy to Realise Pakistan's Gemstone Potential 2026–2030*.

Key measures include internationally recognised certification, geological mapping, modern mining equipment, specialised training and improved access to overseas markets.

The government also plans to establish a dedicated sector authority and a National Warranty Office.

Officials aim to expand domestic cutting, polishing and processing so Pakistani businesses can capture more value from gemstone exports.

### Significant resources, limited exports

Pakistan has gemstone deposits across Gilgit-Baltistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Azad Jammu and Kashmir, and Balochistan.

More than 5,000 businesses process over 30 gemstone varieties, including emerald, ruby, topaz, aquamarine and peridot, according to Iqbal.

However, outdated extraction methods, limited processing facilities and inadequate certification have restricted export growth.

### International cooperation

The November 17–20 workshop, *Unlocking the Value of Indigenous Gemstones*, is organised by the Asian Productivity Organization, Pakistan's National Productivity Organization and the Pakistan Gems and Jewellery Development Company.

Participants are discussing gemstone standards, modern processing techniques, trade compliance and international partnerships.

The government hopes improved certification, investment and branding will strengthen Pakistan's position in the global gemstone market.
