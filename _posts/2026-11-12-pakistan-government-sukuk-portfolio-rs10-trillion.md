**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan's government Sukuk portfolio has crossed Rs10 trillion in both outstanding value and cumulative issuance, marking a significant expansion of the country's Islamic sovereign debt market.

The milestone was highlighted at a gathering attended by Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb, State Bank of Pakistan Governor Jameel Ahmad and representatives of leading Islamic financial institutions.

According to figures presented at the event, the government issued Rs4.179 trillion in Sukuk between January and October 2026, more than double the Rs2.052 trillion issued throughout 2025.

The expansion has been supported by new financing structures and wider participation from banks and other financial institutions.

Pakistan has traditionally issued sovereign Sukuk through Ijarah arrangements backed by government assets. More recently, a hybrid structure combining Ijarah and Commodity Murabaha has increased the government's capacity to issue Islamic securities.

The introduction of tradable three-month and six-month Sukuk has also created additional short-term investment options for Islamic banks.

Officials and industry representatives said these developments could strengthen Pakistan's Islamic capital market and provide alternatives to conventional government borrowing instruments.

The milestone reflects the growing role of Shariah-compliant securities in financing the government's domestic borrowing requirements.
