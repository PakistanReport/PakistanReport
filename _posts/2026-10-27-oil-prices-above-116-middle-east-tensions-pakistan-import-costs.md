**ISLAMABAD, October 27, 2026:** International oil prices remained elevated on Tuesday as diplomatic efforts to ease Middle East tensions competed with renewed threats to energy infrastructure and shipping.

Brent crude futures rose to approximately $116.26 a barrel by 0918 GMT, while US West Texas Intermediate crude traded near $102.16, according to Reuters market data.

The price movements followed attacks affecting Saudi Arabia and continued security concerns around the Strait of Hormuz, a major route for global energy shipments.

Diplomatic discussions involving Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Türkiye and Iran offered some prospect of reducing regional tensions, but traders continued to account for possible supply disruptions.

For Pakistan, sustained high crude prices increase the cost of imported petroleum products and can place additional pressure on foreign exchange requirements.

Higher import costs may also influence domestic petrol and diesel prices, depending on international product prices, exchange rates, taxes and the government's pricing decisions.

Transport, agriculture and industrial production are particularly exposed to changes in diesel and other fuel costs.

Pakistan's vulnerability reflects its reliance on imported energy and the importance of Gulf shipping routes to international oil markets.

The direction of prices will depend partly on whether regional diplomacy restores confidence in shipping security and reduces the risk of further supply interruptions.
