**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan and Malaysia have reaffirmed their commitment to expanding defence and security cooperation, including potential trilateral collaboration with Türkiye and closer engagement in maritime security and counterterrorism.

The understanding was reached during a meeting between Pakistan's Chief of Defence Forces and Chief of Army Staff Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir and Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim in Putrajaya on Monday.

According to Inter-Services Public Relations (ISPR), the discussions covered bilateral military relations, regional security developments and opportunities to strengthen institutional cooperation between the armed forces of both countries.

In a separate statement, Anwar identified maritime security and counterterrorism as potential areas for further collaboration.

The Malaysian prime minister said the discussions also addressed efforts to reduce tensions in West Asia and the importance of protecting international maritime security.

Both sides emphasised diplomatic engagement, national sovereignty and the protection of civilians in addressing regional disputes.

During his official visit, Field Marshal Munir also met Malaysian Defence Minister Mohamed Khaled bin Nordin to discuss expanding military cooperation and identifying additional areas of bilateral engagement.

The visit follows the 16th meeting of the Malaysia–Pakistan Joint Committee on Defence Cooperation, held in Rawalpindi on October 14.

During that meeting, officials discussed cooperation in defence science, technology and industrial development, including opportunities for joint ventures and technology transfer.

Anwar also congratulated Field Marshal Munir on his induction into the Hall of Fame of the Malaysian Armed Forces Staff College.

Pakistan and Malaysia agreed to maintain regular senior-level military exchanges and continued consultations on international and regional security matters.
