United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres held separate meetings with Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir in Islamabad on Wednesday, discussing regional diplomacy, international peace and the threat of terrorism during his visit to Pakistan.

According to the UN Secretary-General's spokesperson, Guterres expressed appreciation for Pakistan's role as an elected member of the UN Security Council, including its efforts to encourage the peaceful settlement of disputes. He also thanked Pakistan for its support for the United Nations and its contributions to peacekeeping operations.

The meeting with Sharif took place as Pakistan sought to highlight dialogue and diplomatic engagement in addressing regional tensions. In the Pakistani government's account, the prime minister described diplomacy as the preferred route to preventing escalation and resolving international disputes.

Sharif also drew attention to Pakistan's diplomatic efforts, including its stated mediation role between the United States and Iran. That characterisation reflects the Pakistani government's account of its activities; the meeting itself should not be taken as evidence of a new agreement between those countries.

The prime minister reaffirmed Pakistan's support for multilateral institutions and an effective United Nations. The government said he welcomed Guterres's commitment to the UN Charter, international law and peaceful dispute resolution.

Earlier in the day, Guterres met Field Marshal Munir, who serves as Chief of Army Staff and Chief of Defence Forces. The UN spokesperson said the Secretary-General highlighted Pakistan's role in international affairs and discussed the threat posed by terrorism.

The spokesperson also said Guterres expressed the United Nations' solidarity with Pakistan in confronting that threat. The statement described the subject of the discussion but did not announce a new UN military operation or security assistance package.

The separate meetings reflect two dimensions of Pakistan's engagement with the United Nations: diplomatic participation through international institutions and the country's concerns about regional security and militant violence.

Pakistan's elected membership of the Security Council gives it a role in deliberations over international peace and security. The UN's reference to Pakistan's efforts towards peaceful dispute settlement was part of its official summary of the Secretary-General's visit, rather than an announcement of a new Security Council resolution.

Pakistan has also been a longstanding contributor to UN peacekeeping. The Secretary-General's expression of gratitude acknowledged that contribution, although the briefing did not provide a new troop commitment or a revised deployment figure.

The meetings came against a backdrop of heightened regional tensions and continued concern over terrorism. Both subjects featured in the accounts issued by the UN and Pakistani authorities, but the available statements do not establish that the visit produced a formal joint declaration or binding new initiative.

The distinction between discussion and decision is important in reporting high-level diplomatic visits. Official summaries can indicate the priorities raised by participants without necessarily recording an agreed policy change.

Guterres's visit therefore underscored Pakistan's continuing engagement with the UN on diplomacy and security. Any subsequent commitments or initiatives would require separate confirmation from the relevant institutions.
