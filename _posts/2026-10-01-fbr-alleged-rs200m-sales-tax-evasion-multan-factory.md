MULTAN: The Federal Board of Revenue has identified roughly Rs200 million in alleged sales tax liability during scrutiny of an industrial unit in Multan, according to officials, with the final amount still under examination.

The case is being handled by the Large Taxpayer Office in Multan and concerns sales tax compliance at the factory.

Officials said more than Rs70 million has been deposited in connection with the case, while security cheques worth more than Rs130 million have also been submitted.

Those amounts should not be treated as a final tax assessment or a judicial finding of evasion. FBR’s scrutiny is continuing, and officials have said the final liability has not yet been determined.

The distinction matters because tax investigations can involve preliminary calculations that change after records are examined, explanations are considered and statutory proceedings are completed.

According to information released about the case, the alleged discrepancy was identified through scrutiny of the taxpayer’s sales tax affairs.

Sales tax is generally collected and deposited through Pakistan’s federal tax system, with registered businesses required to maintain records and file returns according to applicable law and regulations.

Large Taxpayer Offices handle tax administration for significant taxpayers and can conduct audits, scrutiny and enforcement proceedings where discrepancies are identified.

The Multan case comes as FBR expands the use of data analysis and electronic systems to identify inconsistencies in returns and business records. The authority has increasingly emphasised technology-based compliance and enforcement as part of efforts to improve federal revenue collection.

For businesses, an initial tax discrepancy can lead to requests for records, explanations, assessments and, depending on the findings, recovery or other proceedings. Taxpayers retain the legal rights available to them under the tax framework.

The reported deposit of more than Rs70 million indicates that a substantial amount has already been paid during the process, while the security cheques cover more than Rs130 million. Neither figure by itself establishes the final liability.

The approximately Rs200 million headline figure therefore represents the amount currently alleged or identified during scrutiny, not a final adjudicated tax demand.

Until FBR completes its examination, the most accurate description is that the authority is investigating an alleged sales tax shortfall at the Multan industrial unit.
