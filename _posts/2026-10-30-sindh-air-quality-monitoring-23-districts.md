**KARACHI, October 29, 2026** — The Sindh government has proposed a $7 million project to set up air-quality monitoring stations in 23 districts, with the aim of improving pollution measurement and supporting environmental planning.

According to project documents reported by Wealth Pakistan, the plan calls for 23 continuous monitoring stations connected through a real-time data network.

The proposed programme forms part of wider development cooperation associated with the World Bank's Country Partnership Framework for Pakistan.

Sindh's Environment, Climate Change and Coastal Development Department would oversee implementation in coordination with district administrations.

The stations are intended to measure pollutants such as fine particulate matter, carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, sulphur dioxide and ground-level ozone.

They would also record weather-related information, helping authorities examine how environmental conditions influence pollution levels.

The project provides for equipment calibration, data management, quality assurance and long-term maintenance.

An earlier air-quality monitoring initiative has already been initiated in Karachi, Hyderabad and Sukkur.

The new network would extend monitoring to other districts where consistent pollution data is currently limited.

More reliable measurements could help environmental authorities locate pollution hotspots, assess public-health risks and design more targeted pollution-control measures.

The data could also support Pakistan's efforts to track progress towards its national climate and emissions-reduction commitments.

If the network is completed and properly maintained, it could give Sindh a stronger basis for issuing air-quality warnings, enforcing environmental regulations and managing pollution over the longer term.
