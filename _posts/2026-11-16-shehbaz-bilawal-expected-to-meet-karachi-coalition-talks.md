**ISLAMABAD, 16 November 2026 —** Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif is expected to meet Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari in Karachi on Friday, November 20, as the country's two main governing allies seek closer political coordination amid disagreements over provincial affairs and relations with opposition parties.

The meeting is planned at Bilawal's invitation, according to The Express Tribune, which reported that the leaders would discuss relations between the Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz (PML-N) and the PPP.

The proposed visit follows several high-level contacts between the parties. However, an official announcement confirming the November 20 appointment has not yet been identified.

The latest consultations come against a backdrop of political differences that have periodically strained relations between the coalition partners, despite their continued cooperation at the federal level.

### Efforts to improve coalition coordination

Shehbaz hosted PPP leaders for dinner in Islamabad on November 3, during which the two sides discussed improving their working relationship.

According to subsequent reporting, the prime minister proposed establishing a joint coordination committee under his and Bilawal's leadership. The proposed body would meet monthly to address matters of mutual concern.

There has been no confirmation that the committee has formally been established.

Speaking in Larkana on November 6, Bilawal described his recent discussions with the prime minister positively and said another meeting was expected in Karachi following Shehbaz's participation in the COP31 climate conference.

He also suggested holding federal cabinet meetings in different provinces to improve cooperation between the Centre and provincial governments.

Bilawal has repeatedly argued that political disagreements should not prevent the government from addressing economic pressures facing the public.

At a Karachi event on November 10, he urged the prime minister to rebuild confidence among coalition partners and avoid further confrontation with opposition parties. He also reaffirmed the PPP's willingness to support government efforts to address economic difficulties.

### Zardari–Shehbaz meeting

The renewed engagement follows President Asif Ali Zardari's meeting with Shehbaz at the prime minister's Lahore residence on November 14.

According to the Prime Minister's Office, the two leaders discussed the country's political, economic and security situation, cooperation among coalition parties and progress on public welfare initiatives.

They reaffirmed their commitment to continued consultation and economic stability.

National Assembly Speaker Ayaz Sadiq, Law Minister Azam Nazeer Tarar, former prime minister Raja Pervez Ashraf, Senator Saleem Mandviwalla and other senior figures attended.

The meeting also took place amid renewed debate over creating additional provinces.

Dawn reported, citing official sources, that the leaders discussed constitutional requirements for any proposed referendum and the need for parliamentary approval.

The Express Tribune, however, quoted two participants who said the meeting did not address the provinces question or other specific political issues.

The government's public account did not mention any discussion of new provinces, leaving the competing accounts unconfirmed.

The PPP has maintained its opposition to dividing Sindh, while some PML-N figures have supported examining proposals for additional administrative units.

### Opposition relations remain sensitive

Differences over the federal government's approach to opposition parties have also become part of the political discussion.

On November 15, Bilawal spoke by telephone with Jamiat Ulema-e-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman and criticised what the PPP described as government actions against the party.

According to a PPP statement, Bilawal emphasised the importance of allowing opposition parties to criticise the government and participate in parliamentary politics.

He has also advocated dialogue with Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI), particularly over the security situation in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

Earlier this month, Bilawal said he would support ending governor's rule in KP if the federal government and PTI reached an understanding on counterterrorism.

These statements indicate that the PPP's approach to political engagement is not always identical to that of its coalition partner.

### What the Karachi meeting could address

The reported Friday meeting would provide another opportunity for the PML-N and PPP to discuss federal-provincial cooperation, coalition decision-making and their differing approaches to opposition politics.

The proposed coordination committee could offer a more regular mechanism for resolving disagreements, although its creation and responsibilities remain unconfirmed.

For now, the two parties have publicly reaffirmed their commitment to working together. Whether the anticipated Karachi talks produce specific agreements will depend on the discussions and any subsequent official announcements.
