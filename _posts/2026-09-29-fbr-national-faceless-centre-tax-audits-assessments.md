**ISLAMABAD —** The Federal Board of Revenue has established a National Faceless Centre in Islamabad, introducing a new structure for tax audits and assessments that is designed to remove direct contact between taxpayers and the officers handling their cases.

Under the system described by FBR, cases will be selected through a computerised, risk-based process rather than by an individual officer. A selected case will then be assigned automatically to an officer who may be based anywhere in the country.

The taxpayer will not know the identity of the officer handling the case, while the officer will not choose which taxpayer's case is assigned.

### Audit, assessment and review separated

A central feature of the model is the separation of key stages of a case.

FBR says one officer will conduct the audit, a second will make the assessment and a third will review the work for quality before an order is issued. That means a single officer will no longer control the same case from beginning to end under the faceless process.

Notices, replies and hearings are to take place electronically through FBR's IRIS system. Where the law requires physical verification or recovery action, that work will be carried out by a separate field team.

The structure is intended to reduce face-to-face interaction and individual discretion while creating a clearer division of responsibility inside the tax administration.

### Legal basis comes from Finance Act 2026

FBR says the National Faceless Centre draws its authority from the Finance Act 2026.

The legislative framework provides for a national centre handling proceedings in a faceless manner, algorithmic assignment of functions and separate officers for audit, assessment and quality control. It also provides for electronic communication in covered proceedings.

The centre will be headed by a Chief Commissioner Inland Revenue and will include dedicated functions for faceless audit, faceless assessment, quality control and field operations. FBR has also established a Programme Management Unit to oversee implementation.

The reform does not mean that every tax matter immediately becomes faceless. Its practical scope will depend on the cases and functions brought under the new system as the centre is rolled out.

For taxpayers whose cases are covered, however, the operating model represents a material change: selection and assignment become technology-led, routine interaction shifts to IRIS and the main stages of a case are divided among different officers.

The test for the new system will be whether those safeguards translate into more consistent decisions, faster proceedings and less unnecessary taxpayer-officer contact in practice.
