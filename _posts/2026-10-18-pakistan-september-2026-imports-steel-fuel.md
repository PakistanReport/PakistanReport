Pakistan's merchandise imports increased by **$668 million in September 2026**, according to provisional figures from the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS), while the composition of the import bill showed sharply different movements across fuel and industrial products.

Imports reached **$6.492 billion**, compared with **$5.824 billion in August** and **$5.848 billion in September 2025**. Pakistan Report's calculation from those published dollar totals puts the month-on-month rise at approximately **11.47%** and the year-on-year increase at **11.01%**, consistent with PBS's published percentages. The absolute year-on-year increase was **$644 million**.

## A split within energy imports

PBS recorded **Rs163.173 billion** in petroleum-product imports during September, an increase of **22.74%** from August in rupee terms. In contrast, **petroleum crude** imports were **Rs143.902 billion**, down **28.47%** from the previous month. The combined value of those two categories was **Rs307.075 billion**, or approximately **17.03%** of the month's **Rs1.803 trillion** total merchandise imports by value in rupees.

That contrast matters: a rise in refined petroleum-product imports does not mean crude imports moved in the same direction. The values alone do not establish whether prices, quantities, shipment timing or product mix caused either change.

## Steel and vehicle-related imports accelerated

**Iron and steel** imports reached **Rs80.850 billion**, up **60.50%** month on month. Imports of **iron and steel scrap** totalled **Rs69.062 billion**, up **23.75%**. Taken together, those two separately reported categories amounted to **Rs149.912 billion** in September, approximately **8.31%** of the total rupee import bill.

Imports of motor cars in completely knocked-down or semi-knocked-down form reached **Rs68.149 billion**, rising **46.45%** from August. Palm oil imports stood at **Rs96.310 billion**, up **27.28%**; plastic materials were **Rs87.308 billion**, up **28.03%**. Electrical machinery and apparatus, however, declined **9.11%** month on month to **Rs77.492 billion**.

These changes are reported as **import values**, not physical volumes. They should not be interpreted by themselves as proof of increased domestic consumption, factory output or industrial recovery.

## Three-month picture and trade balance

Across July–September 2026, imports totalled **$19.213 billion**, against **$16.973 billion** in the comparable period a year earlier. That is an increase of **$2.240 billion**, approximately **13.20%**. The September merchandise trade deficit was **$3.531 billion**, according to PBS, while the cumulative July–September deficit was **$10.768 billion**.

The September increase was therefore not an isolated comparison with an unusually low August: the first three months of the fiscal year also showed a larger import bill than the same period a year earlier. Whether this reflects stronger demand, international prices or shipment timing requires quantity-level and price-level analysis beyond the release's headline values.

## What these figures do—and do not—measure

**These are PBS customs-based merchandise-import statistics, not the State Bank of Pakistan's banking-channel import-payment series.** The two sources use different recording concepts and timing; their totals and country rankings must not be mixed. This analysis does not claim to identify September's largest SBP payment recipients or rank countries by bank-settled imports.

**Methodology:** Pakistan Report independently subtracted the monthly and year-earlier US-dollar import totals and calculated the combined share of the two petroleum categories and the two steel categories using the rupee figures in the original PBS release. Dollar figures are rounded by PBS to the nearest million, so independently calculated percentages are approximate. The PBS release labels September data provisional. The article does not infer changes in volumes from changes in values.

**Primary source:** Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, *Advance Release on External Trade Statistics for September 2026*, pp. 1–2: https://www.pbs.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Release_Statement_September-2026.pdf
