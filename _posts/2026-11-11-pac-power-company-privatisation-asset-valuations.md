**ISLAMABAD:** The National Assembly's Public Accounts Committee (PAC) has recommended delaying the privatisation of state-owned electricity distribution companies until their assets are properly assessed and revalued.

The recommendation followed an audit finding that power-sector entities had failed to revalue fixed assets worth Rs624.47 billion, raising concerns about the accuracy of valuations ahead of the planned sales.

The government's first phase of distribution company privatisation covers Islamabad Electric Supply Company (IESCO), Faisalabad Electric Supply Company (FESCO) and Gujranwala Electric Power Company (GEPCO).

During Tuesday's committee meeting, Auditor General of Pakistan Maqbool Ahmad Gondal raised concerns about outdated asset valuations and weaknesses in record-keeping.

Audit officials reported that several distribution companies had failed to properly tag and document their assets, making it difficult to establish reliable valuations of land, machinery and other property.

Committee members warned that proceeding without updated assessments could result in public assets being undervalued during privatisation.

Acting PAC Chairperson Shahida Akhtar Ali called for the process to be reviewed before the companies were offered for sale.

The committee directed the Power Division and Privatisation Commission to submit detailed reports on the outstanding valuation issues within three weeks.

The government is pursuing the sale of distribution companies as part of wider power-sector reforms aimed at reducing financial losses and improving operational efficiency.

The first-phase transactions are targeted for the first quarter of 2027, with the committee seeking resolution of the valuation concerns before the sales proceed.
