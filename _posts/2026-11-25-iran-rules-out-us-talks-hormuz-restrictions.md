**TEHRAN:** Iran has ruled out negotiations with the United States until Washington fulfils its seven conditions, while its military leadership has warned that restrictions on maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz will be progressively tightened.

Iranian Parliament Speaker and chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf reiterated Tehran's position during a parliamentary session on Tuesday, warning that renewed military action against Iran could lead to a geographical expansion of the conflict.

Iranian Army Commander Major General Amir Hatami said Tehran would strengthen its control over the strategic waterway until its demands were fulfilled.

Although Iran has not officially published the complete list of seven conditions, its stated demands include:

- Lifting US economic sanctions.
- Ending the American naval blockade of Iranian ports.
- Releasing frozen Iranian assets.
- Ending hostilities across all fronts.
- Providing guarantees against renewed military action.
- Paying compensation for war-related damage.

Meanwhile, Iran's navy claimed on Tuesday that two oil tankers had caught fire after striking naval mines near the Strait of Hormuz.

The announcement followed a separate claim by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps on Monday that three tankers had struck mines despite receiving American military protection. The reported incidents have not been independently verified.

Diplomatic efforts continue, with Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani holding discussions in Washington with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Vice President JD Vance to revive negotiations.

US President Donald Trump has previously described Iran's proposed terms as unacceptable, while Washington maintains that Tehran's nuclear programme must form part of initial negotiations.

The current restrictions follow the collapse of the Pakistan-brokered Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, signed by Washington and Tehran on June 17. The 14-point agreement sought to end hostilities, reopen the Strait of Hormuz and establish a framework for permanent peace negotiations. It expired on August 17 without a comprehensive settlement.
