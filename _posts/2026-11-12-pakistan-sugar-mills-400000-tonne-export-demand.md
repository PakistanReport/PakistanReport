**LAHORE:** Pakistan's sugar industry has urged the federal government to allow exports of an additional 400,000 metric tonnes, warning that large inventories and financial pressures could delay the start of the new sugarcane crushing season.

The demand was made by the Pakistan Sugar Mills Association (PSMA) following a general body meeting on Wednesday involving mill owners from Punjab, Sindh and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

According to the association, approximately 1.1 million tonnes of surplus sugar remain in storage, creating difficulties for mills preparing to begin the 2026–27 crushing season.

PSMA said the government had previously approved an export quota of 200,000 tonnes, but the industry continued to face delays in securing permission to proceed under the agreed arrangements.

Mill owners maintained that the accumulation of unsold sugar had tied up financial resources needed to purchase sugarcane and operate factories during the upcoming season.

The association's members agreed that crushing operations could be delayed until December unless the situation improves.

PSMA has authorised its executive committee to approach the federal government and seek permission for a further 400,000 tonnes of exports.

The industry body is also pressing for a comprehensive deregulation policy, arguing that changes to the existing framework are needed to address recurring problems involving sugar stocks and market access.

According to the association, another substantial surplus could emerge by the end of the 2026–27 season, adding to pressure on mills if existing inventories remain unsold.

The timing of crushing operations is important for sugarcane growers, who depend on mills to purchase and process their harvests.

A delay in the season could also affect the movement of sugarcane from farms to factories and the availability of payments to growers.

PSMA said it would continue discussions with government officials over export permissions and other outstanding industry concerns.

The association has proposed seeking a meeting with the deputy prime minister to present its position and discuss possible measures.

Sugar mill owners are also expected to hold a press conference in Lahore on 16 November to outline their next course of action.
