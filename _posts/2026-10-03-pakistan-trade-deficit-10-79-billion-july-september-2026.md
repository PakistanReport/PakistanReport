The figures show that Pakistani businesses sold more goods overseas, an encouraging development for exporters. However, spending on imported products also increased, widening the difference between the two totals.

In September alone, exports reached around $2.94 billion, up 17.6% from a year earlier. Imports stood at approximately $6.49 billion, leaving a monthly goods trade deficit of about $3.56 billion.

Export earnings help Pakistan obtain foreign currency and support industries that sell to international markets. Imports include products and materials needed by consumers and businesses.

The goods trade deficit is one part of Pakistan's wider international financial position. Money sent home by overseas Pakistanis and earnings from services also contribute to the country's foreign currency inflows.

The latest figures highlight the importance of continued export growth as Pakistan works to strengthen its external trade position.
