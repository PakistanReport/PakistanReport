Pakistan’s merchandise trade deficit widened to $10.79 billion in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026-27 as imports grew faster than exports, according to Pakistan Bureau of Statistics data.

The July–September deficit increased by more than 15% from $9.37 billion in the same three months of the previous fiscal year.

Exports rose nearly 11% year-on-year to about $8.42 billion during the quarter, up from $7.59 billion.

Imports increased by more than 13% to about $19.22 billion, compared with $16.97 billion in the same period last year.

Both sides of Pakistan’s goods trade therefore expanded, but the larger import bill increased by more in absolute terms, widening the overall merchandise trade gap.

September alone recorded a trade deficit of about $3.56 billion, up more than 6% from roughly $3.35 billion in September 2025.

Exports in September were about $2.94 billion, an increase of 17.6% from $2.5 billion a year earlier. Imports reached about $6.49 billion, up more than 11% from $5.85 billion.

Compared with August, the September trade deficit also increased by nearly 8% from $3.29 billion.

The first-quarter figure provides a fuller picture than the earlier July–August data because it covers the complete opening quarter of FY27. The latest numbers show export growth continuing, but not by enough to offset the larger value of imports.

A merchandise trade deficit means the value of goods imported exceeds the value of goods exported. It is not the same as Pakistan’s current-account balance, which also includes services, income flows and transfers such as remittances.

The widening goods deficit can increase the amount of foreign currency required to finance trade, although its eventual effect on the broader external account depends on other flows, including services trade, remittances and financing.

For now, the PBS data shows that Pakistan entered FY27 with stronger exports than a year earlier but an even larger increase in imports, pushing the three-month merchandise trade gap to $10.79 billion.
