**ISLAMABAD:** Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar has said respect for the Indus Waters Treaty is essential to regional stability, reaffirming Pakistan's determination to protect its rights under the agreement.

Speaking at the Margalla Dialogue 2026 in Islamabad on Tuesday, Dar opposed the use of shared water resources as a means of political pressure and called for countries to honour their treaty commitments.

His remarks placed water security within a broader discussion of regional tensions, international law and the need for diplomatic engagement.

### Water Security and Regional Relations

The Indus Waters Treaty, signed by Pakistan and India in 1960 with the World Bank's involvement, establishes arrangements for the use of the Indus river system and provides procedures for addressing disagreements.

The agreement has become a major point of contention between the neighbouring countries. Pakistan maintains that the treaty remains binding and that disputes must be handled through its established mechanisms.

### Wider Call for Diplomacy

During his address, Dar also spoke about growing uncertainty in international relations and the pressure facing multilateral institutions.

He called for dialogue and cooperation to manage regional conflicts, while reiterating Pakistan's position on Kashmir and its concerns about security along the Afghan border.

The Margalla Dialogue brings together policymakers, diplomats and researchers to discuss foreign policy and security issues. This year's programme includes sessions on geopolitical competition, economic challenges and international law.

Dar's comments underline the government's view that water-sharing obligations should remain part of a rules-based framework even when relations between neighbouring states are strained.
