**ISLAMABAD:** Pakistan is expected to receive approximately $1.21 billion from the International Monetary Fund (IMF) in December, subject to Executive Board approval of the latest reviews of its two ongoing financing programmes.

The IMF Executive Board is expected to consider Pakistan's programme reviews on December 11, following a staff-level agreement reached in early October.

The anticipated disbursement comprises:

- Approximately $1 billion under the $7 billion Extended Fund Facility (EFF).
- Approximately $210 million under the $1.4 billion Resilience and Sustainability Facility (RSF).

Approval would conclude the fourth review of the EFF and the third review of the RSF. According to the IMF, the latest tranche would bring cumulative disbursements under both programmes to approximately $5.7 billion. Both programmes are scheduled to conclude in September 2027.

An IMF mission led by Iva Petrova conducted discussions with Pakistani authorities between September 23 and October 7, also completing the country's 2026 Article IV consultation.

The Fund assessed Pakistan's economic stabilisation programme as broadly on track despite external pressures associated with the Middle East conflict.

Economic growth reached 4 per cent during the first three quarters of FY26 before higher energy costs and supply disruptions weakened momentum. The IMF estimated full-year growth at 3.6 per cent.

Gross foreign exchange reserves reached approximately $21.5 billion by the end of September, while remittance inflows helped maintain a broadly balanced current account during FY26.

Pakistan's commitments under the programme include achieving an underlying primary fiscal surplus equivalent to 2 per cent of GDP in FY27, improving tax administration, strengthening energy sector finances and expanding social protection.

The IMF has identified geopolitical tensions, energy price volatility, disruptions to international trade and tighter global financing conditions as continuing risks to Pakistan's economic recovery.
