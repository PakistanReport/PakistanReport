**KARACHI:** Standard Chartered expects the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) to raise its benchmark interest rate to 13 per cent by June 2027, citing persistent inflationary pressure from elevated international oil prices.

Speaking at a media roundtable in Karachi, the bank's economist Talha Nadeem forecast average inflation of around 10 per cent during fiscal year 2026–27, above the central bank's medium-term target of 5–7 per cent.

The bank expects Brent crude oil to average approximately $101 per barrel during the final quarter of 2026 and around $90 in 2027, as conflict-related disruptions in the Middle East continue to affect energy markets.

Standard Chartered also projects Pakistan's economic growth will slow to 3 per cent in FY27, compared with 3.7 per cent in the previous fiscal year.

The forecast is below the government's 4 per cent growth target and the SBP's projected range of 3.5–4.5 per cent. The policy rate currently stands at 12 per cent.

The bank said remittances and improved foreign exchange reserves have helped Pakistan withstand external pressures, although higher energy costs remain a significant economic risk.

Standard Chartered's global geopolitical strategist Philippe Dauba-Pantanacce warned that damage to energy infrastructure and depleted reserves could keep oil prices elevated even if regional tensions ease.

The bank's Pakistan chief executive, Adil Salahuddin, called for stronger exports and a broader tax base, while stressing the importance of maintaining fiscal discipline after the current IMF programme ends in 2027.

Nadeem said foreign investors favour continued IMF engagement because they regard it as supporting policy stability and fiscal discipline.
