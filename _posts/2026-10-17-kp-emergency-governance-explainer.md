Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is operating under emergency and governor's rule following presidential proclamations issued on 11 October 2026 and subsequently endorsed by Parliament. The measures have raised questions about the roles of the governor, the elected provincial government and the courts.

### What has changed?

The federal government invoked Article 232, concerning a proclamation of emergency, and Article 234, which addresses the failure of constitutional machinery in a province. Parliament approved resolutions endorsing the proclamations during a joint sitting on 15 October.

Reporting on the measures describes the elected KP administration as non-functional under governor's rule. The change is therefore more substantial than a routine disagreement between the federal and provincial governments.

### Does the governor replace the entire provincial government?

Article 234 permits the president, through a proclamation, to assume specified functions of the provincial government or direct the governor to assume them on the president’s behalf. It also permits the proclamation to place provincial legislative powers under Parliament’s authority. Article 234 expressly protects the powers of the High Court from being assumed under that provision. The exact allocation of executive and legislative powers depends on the wording of the proclamation and any subsequent legal instruments.

That distinction is important. Governor's rule should not be described as giving the governor unlimited personal authority, nor should the continued existence or powers of provincial institutions be assumed without consulting the operative orders.

### What happens to the chief minister?

The Federal Constitutional Court disposed of a challenge concerning the appointment of Chief Minister Sohail Afridi after the emergency and governor's rule rendered the dispute academic in the court's assessment. Contemporary reporting describes Afridi as non-functional and in preventive custody. This is a description of the reported situation, not a final ruling on the constitutionality of the emergency itself.

### Can the measures be challenged?

Yes. PTI Chairman Barrister Gohar Ali Khan and another petitioner have challenged the emergency and governor's rule before the Peshawar High Court. As of 17 October, the court had fixed 20 October for hearings. The filing of a petition does not mean the court has decided the case.

### What should readers watch?

The next material developments are the Peshawar High Court proceedings, any published orders defining administrative powers, and any changes to the duration or scope of the emergency measures. Reports describe an advisory council, but the Constitution does not by itself specify that council’s membership or confer unlimited powers on it. Until the operative proclamations and subsequent orders are examined in full, detailed claims about the council’s powers should be treated cautiously.
