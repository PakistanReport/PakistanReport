**CAIRO, October 27, 2026:** Egypt has cleared the way for possible military assistance to Saudi Arabia after parliament authorised President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi to take defensive measures in coordination with Riyadh.

The decision, approved on Monday by a two-thirds parliamentary majority, follows escalating attacks on Saudi territory and growing concerns about the security of critical infrastructure.

Egyptian lawmakers considered the proposal during closed-door sessions held on October 18, 19 and 26.

The authorisation allows the government to support the defence of Saudi territory, sovereignty and religious sites against external attacks.

However, Egyptian authorities have not publicly confirmed the deployment of troops or specified the military equipment that could be provided.

El-Sisi has previously stated that Egypt's involvement would be defensive and would not include attacks on Yemen.

The proposal prompted concerns among some opposition lawmakers, who questioned the potential mission's duration, financial costs and operational limits.

Egypt's decision comes as Yemen's Houthi movement continues military operations affecting Saudi Arabia, increasing pressure on regional governments to strengthen air defence and infrastructure security.

The situation also carries economic implications for Cairo because instability around the Red Sea threatens shipping routes connected to the Suez Canal.

Saudi Arabia remains an important economic partner for Egypt through investment, financial assistance and employment opportunities for Egyptian workers.

The parliamentary approval provides a legal basis for possible military cooperation but does not establish when assistance will begin or what form it will take.

Further announcements from Cairo and Riyadh will be needed to determine whether the authorisation leads to an operational deployment.
