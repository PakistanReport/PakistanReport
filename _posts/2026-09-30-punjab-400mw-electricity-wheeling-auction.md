The Punjab government is considering participation in Pakistan's first 400-megawatt electricity wheeling auction, as officials assess the commercial and financial case for buying power through the country's emerging competitive electricity market.

Punjab officials have reviewed expected electricity prices, grid charges, commercial viability, financial implications, risks and a possible bidding strategy, according to reporting on the provincial government's deliberations.

Punjab Financial Advisory Services has also been engaged to assess the financial, commercial and economic aspects of potential participation.

The province has not yet been reported as having won capacity or completed a purchase. The current development is an assessment of whether and how Punjab should participate in the auction.

## What is the 400MW auction?

The federal Power Division has described the auction as the first phase of Pakistan's Competitive Trading Bilateral Contract Market, or CTBCM.

The initial auction offers 400MW of electricity, while the broader programme envisages 800MW over five years.

Under electricity wheeling arrangements, eligible buyers and sellers can enter bilateral electricity transactions while using the existing transmission and distribution network to move power between them.

That differs from the traditional model in which consumers generally purchase electricity through their local distribution company under regulated arrangements.

## Punjab studies costs and risks

For Punjab, participation requires more than comparing the headline price of electricity.

Grid and wheeling charges, contractual terms, expected demand, financial exposure and other market costs can affect whether a bilateral electricity purchase is commercially attractive.

That is why the province's assessment includes expected electricity prices as well as network charges and bidding risks.

The Independent System and Market Operator has listed proposal submissions for November 20, 2026, with the auction scheduled for January 15, 2027. Its published timetable lists February 28 for regulatory approval of the auction results.

## A test for Pakistan's competitive power market

The auction is an early practical test of reforms intended to introduce more competition into electricity trading.

Pakistan has spent years developing the regulatory and market framework for CTBCM. Moving from rules and institutional preparation to an actual capacity auction is therefore an important implementation stage.

Punjab's consideration of participation adds a provincial-government dimension to that process. A decision to bid would make the province a potential participant in the new market rather than simply an observer of the federal reform.

For now, however, Punjab remains at the evaluation stage. The eventual significance will depend on whether it submits a bid, the terms offered in the auction and whether those terms prove competitive against the province's other electricity-supply options.
