**ISLAMABAD:** The Water and Power Development Authority (WAPDA) has ordered a departmental inquiry into an incident at the Tarbela Fifth Extension Hydropower Project after a rock collapse allowed water to enter a connecting tunnel and the under-construction powerhouse.

The incident occurred on Monday during excavation of the second bench connecting tunnel at the 1,530-megawatt project.

According to WAPDA, a weak section of rock collapsed, allowing water from the intake pit to flow into nearby construction areas.

Workers were evacuated following the incident, and the authority reported no injuries or loss of life.

Construction activities were disrupted as project teams began draining the affected areas and assessing the situation.

WAPDA said water had been completely removed from the intake pit, while dewatering operations had begun at the powerhouse.

The authority rejected reports that the project's main tunnel had collapsed, saying there was no damage to the main tunnel, powerhouse structure, tailrace culvert or switchyard.

WAPDA Chairman retired Lieutenant General Muhammad Saeed visited the project on Monday and Tuesday to inspect the affected areas and review the response.

During a meeting with consultants, contractors and project officials, he directed the consultants to prepare a detailed report examining the incident and its possible causes.

He also ordered a departmental inquiry and instructed the project team to implement remedial measures without delay.

The authority said the consultants, contractor and its own engineers had concluded that the incident posed no immediate threat to the integrity of the main Tarbela Dam or its critical structures.

The Ministry of Water Resources separately requested an initial assessment, which WAPDA submitted on Tuesday.

Officials were also directed to prepare a contingency plan aimed at limiting any further delay to the project's completion.

The incident has brought renewed attention to the project's construction history.

In July, reporting on an earlier government inquiry found that WAPDA, the contractor and the consultant had all been held responsible for a previous cofferdam collapse.

The earlier inquiry found that unauthorised changes to the project's design, made after the contract was awarded, had contributed to the cofferdam collapse.

The Tarbela extension has also faced substantial cost increases and delays.

Its revised project cost was approved at approximately Rs316 billion, compared with an earlier estimate of Rs82 billion.

The project's completion timetable had already been extended to June 2028 before Monday's incident.

Separate reporting by The Express Tribune on Wednesday said records from federal project approval bodies had raised concerns about consultant selection, design changes and delays.

According to that report, the Central Development Working Party and the Executive Committee of the National Economic Council had called for a high-level review of procedural lapses during earlier project assessments.

Those concerns relate to the project's wider management and should not be treated as proof of what caused the latest rock collapse.

The Tarbela Fifth Extension is intended to increase electricity generation from the existing dam, with financial support from the World Bank and the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank.

WAPDA has not announced a revised completion date following the incident.

The findings of the newly ordered inquiry will be important in establishing the cause of the water inflow, the extent of any necessary repairs and whether the project faces additional delays.
