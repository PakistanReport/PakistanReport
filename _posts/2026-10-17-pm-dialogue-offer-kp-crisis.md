Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif renewed an invitation to the opposition to resume political dialogue during Parliament’s joint sitting on 15 October, according to The News. PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari separately urged renewed engagement as the confrontation over Khyber Pakhtunkhwa deepened.

The exchange came as lawmakers endorsed the federal government’s emergency and governor’s rule measures in KP, while opposition parties rejected the intervention. No agreement to restart formal negotiations was announced.

### Dialogue amid a parliamentary confrontation

The News reported that the prime minister reiterated his willingness to engage during an encounter with National Assembly Opposition Leader Mahmood Khan Achakzai at the joint sitting. Separately, PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari urged engagement with the opposition in parliamentary remarks.

Those statements should not be confused with a negotiated breakthrough. The opposition alliance Tehreek Tahaffuz Aiyeen-e-Pakistan (TTAP) said it would not continue talks without access to former prime minister Imran Khan and information about his condition. The alliance also raised demands concerning detained PTI politicians.

### What triggered the dispute

The federal government imposed emergency and governor's rule in KP on 11 October, citing security concerns and a breakdown in cooperation over counterterrorism. Parliament subsequently considered resolutions endorsing the proclamations, with the joint sitting approving them on 15 October.

PTI and other opposition figures dispute the measures and their justification. Government representatives argue that the steps were taken under constitutional provisions designed for exceptional circumstances. These competing positions remain contested and are now also subject to litigation.

### The political question ahead

A dialogue offer does not itself establish that formal talks have restarted. The principal unresolved issue is whether the government and opposition can agree on conditions for engagement while the KP emergency remains in force.

This story concerns the political negotiations and parliamentary relationships surrounding the crisis. The separate accompanying explainer addresses the legal structure of emergency and governor's rule, rather than repeating the political exchange.
