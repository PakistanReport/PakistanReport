**ISLAMABAD —** Huawei’s Global Service Center in Islamabad is using Pakistani engineers and technology professionals to support international ICT operations, providing services that include remote technical support, network operations and service assurance for overseas customers.

The centre’s role was highlighted during a September 28 visit by Federal Minister for Information Technology and Telecommunication Shaza Fatima Khawaja, who toured the facility and received a briefing on its operations.

According to the Press Information Department, the Islamabad centre functions as a hub for global technology operations and allows Pakistani technical staff to contribute to international customer services while working from Pakistan.

### Remote services delivered from Islamabad

The Global Service Center provides remote technical support, network operations, service assurance and other advanced ICT services. These are functions that can be delivered across borders digitally, meaning the underlying work can be performed in Pakistan even when the customer or network being supported is elsewhere.

That makes the centre relevant to Pakistan’s wider technology-services economy. Unlike exports of physical goods, IT and ICT-enabled services can generate export earnings through work carried out remotely by engineers, developers, support teams and other specialists.

The government release did not disclose the centre’s revenue, number of international customers or total export earnings. Those figures therefore should not be inferred from the minister’s visit or from broader statements about Pakistan’s technology-export potential.

### Focus on Pakistani technical staff

During the visit, Khawaja interacted with Pakistani engineers and technology professionals working at the centre. Huawei Pakistan CEO Ethan Sun and other company executives were also present.

The minister described the model as an example of high-value digital services being delivered to international markets from within Pakistan. That is the government’s assessment of the centre’s significance; the concrete operational fact is that the Islamabad facility supports global ICT work using locally based professionals.

For Pakistan’s technology sector, centres of this kind can provide experience in large-scale network operations and service management. The value of that experience depends on the complexity of the work performed locally, the development of technical skills and the extent to which staff can progress into higher-value engineering and management functions.

### Existing operations, not a new launch

The September 28 development was a ministerial visit and public briefing on an operating Global Service Center, not the inauguration of a newly opened facility. That distinction separates the story from Huawei’s separate Spare Parts Repair Center in Islamabad, which was inaugurated on September 24.

The GSC is focused on remote and international technology operations, while the repair centre provides local physical testing, troubleshooting and maintenance of Huawei telecom equipment deployed in Pakistan.

For the Global Service Center, the key takeaway is that Pakistani engineers are already participating in international ICT support operations from Islamabad, with remote technical support, network operations and service assurance among the services identified by the government.
