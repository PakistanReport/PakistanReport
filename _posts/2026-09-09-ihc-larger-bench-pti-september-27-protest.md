The Islamabad High Court has constituted a three-member larger bench to hear a petition seeking court intervention over Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf's planned September 27 protest.

The case puts the opposition party's planned mobilisation before the court weeks ahead of the protest date and raises questions about political protest, public order and the possible use of provincial government resources.

The bench comprises Islamabad High Court Chief Justice Sardar Muhammad Sarfraz Dogar, Justice Muhammad Azam Khan and Justice Muhammad Asif.

The court is expected to take up the matter again on September 10.

## What the petition is asking for

The petition was filed ahead of PTI's planned mobilisation in Islamabad.

Among the concerns raised by the petitioner are possible disruption to routine life, traffic and business activity in the federal capital.

The petition has also raised concerns over the potential involvement of resources belonging to the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa government.

Those are claims raised before the court and have not been treated by Pakistan Report as established findings.

## Court seeks responses from officials

The Islamabad High Court issued notices to a number of federal and provincial officials as the case proceeds.

Those include the attorney general for Pakistan as well as senior administrative and police officials from Islamabad and several provinces.

The involvement of officials from multiple jurisdictions reflects the wider administrative questions surrounding a large political mobilisation directed towards the federal capital.

## What PTI says

PTI has maintained that it intends to proceed with the September 27 protest.

The party says the mobilisation is intended to press for the release of its founder, former prime minister Imran Khan, as well as what it describes as the supremacy of the Constitution and rule of law.

PTI representatives have also argued that peaceful protest is a constitutional right.

Whether and how the planned mobilisation proceeds could now be influenced by the continuing court proceedings and any directions issued by the Islamabad High Court.

## Why the case matters

Political demonstrations in Islamabad have repeatedly created disputes over the balance between the right to protest and the government's responsibility to maintain security, traffic movement and access to public and commercial areas.

The present case could therefore have implications beyond PTI's September 27 plans if the court provides broader guidance on how authorities should handle large political protests.

At this stage, however, the court has not issued a final ruling on the planned protest.

## What happens next

The larger bench is due to take up the matter on September 10.

Responses from government and law-enforcement officials, along with the court's observations at the next hearing, will provide a clearer indication of how the case may affect PTI's planned mobilisation.
