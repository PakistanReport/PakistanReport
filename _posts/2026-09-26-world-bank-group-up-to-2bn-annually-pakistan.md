The World Bank Group plans to mobilise up to $2 billion annually for Pakistan under its 2026-35 Country Partnership Framework, according to the government's Economic Affairs Division, as Islamabad seeks greater private-sector investment and long-term development financing.

The figure was highlighted during a meeting between Federal Minister for Economic Affairs Ahad Cheema, International Finance Corporation Division Director Simon Andrews and World Bank Country Director for Pakistan Bolormaa Amgaabazar.

The Economic Affairs Division said Cheema welcomed the commitment to mobilise up to $2 billion a year under the framework over the coming decade. Pakistan's priorities discussed under the cooperation include small and medium enterprises, public-private partnership projects and housing finance, alongside other sectors.

## Ten-year development framework

The World Bank Group's Country Partnership Framework runs from fiscal year 2026 through fiscal year 2035 and is designed around six long-term development outcomes.

According to the World Bank, these include reducing child stunting and learning poverty, strengthening resilience to climate-related disasters, supporting cleaner energy and better air quality, increasing fiscal space for development and expanding productive and inclusive private investment.

The framework represents a longer-term approach than short adjustment programmes, with the World Bank saying it intends to focus on more selective and sustained investments in areas where results require time and continuity.

Private investment is a central component. The September meeting focused in part on the role of the IFC, the World Bank Group's private-sector arm, and on opportunities to support SMEs and projects that can attract private capital.

## 'Up to' $2bn is not a guaranteed annual payment

The $2 billion figure should not be read as a guaranteed cash transfer to Pakistan every year or as an automatic $20 billion financing package.

The government's statement describes the commitment as mobilising **up to** $2 billion annually. The broader World Bank framework also makes implementation dependent on available resources and the development of projects and programmes over the partnership period.

That means actual annual financing and mobilisation can vary according to project pipelines, approvals, investment conditions and the resources available across the World Bank Group.

The framework nevertheless gives Pakistan and the World Bank Group a long planning horizon at a time when the country is seeking higher investment, stronger private-sector participation and more durable development outcomes.
