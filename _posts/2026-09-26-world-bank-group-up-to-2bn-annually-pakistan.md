Pakistan could benefit from up to $2 billion a year in financing and investment support under the World Bank Group's long-term partnership framework, according to the government's Economic Affairs Division.

The plan covers 2026 to 2035 and aims to support development projects, private businesses and investment opportunities across the country.

The funding target was discussed during a meeting involving Federal Minister for Economic Affairs Ahad Cheema, International Finance Corporation official Simon Andrews and World Bank Country Director Bolormaa Amgaabazar.

Officials discussed support for small and medium-sized businesses, housing finance and projects developed jointly by the government and private investors.

The International Finance Corporation is part of the World Bank Group and works with private businesses to encourage investment and economic activity.

The wider ten-year framework also focuses on improving education and child nutrition, strengthening protection against floods and other climate-related disasters, and supporting cleaner energy.

These priorities address important needs for Pakistani families, businesses and communities.

The proposed figure is **up to** $2 billion annually. The amount mobilised in any particular year will depend on available funding, approved projects and investment opportunities.

A longer-term partnership can help Pakistan plan major improvements that take several years to deliver, including infrastructure, education and climate resilience.

The government is seeking to attract more private investment alongside international development financing.

The framework provides a basis for continued cooperation between Pakistan and the World Bank Group, with individual projects and financing arrangements to be developed over the coming years.
